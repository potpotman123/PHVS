PHVS — AlphaSense commercial-expert search, 8 October 2026

Result: four full interviews add opposing commercial mechanisms, but no defensible
numerical PHVS switching or paid-conversion estimate. Existing physician evidence
is complemented by pricing/contracting and field-sales perspectives.

LONG: The BioCryst VP (January2024) anticipated switching to a better oral if
covered. The regulatory SVP (May2022) described researching convenience appeal even
among satisfied injectable users. This challenges treating stable patients as
permanently inaccessible. Neither supplies measured conversion rates; the former
relied partly on a quick PHVS press-release review.

SHORT: The Takeda pricing/contracting director (April2025) described incumbent
contracting strength and payer reluctance to disrupt stable patients. This supports
access friction as a mechanism, not an automatic denial rate or permanent TAM cut.
The expert explicitly could not quantify switching back.

EXECUTION: The CSL field manager (December2024) described hub complexity, access
frustration, and a launch waitlist; preferred streamlining access over more marketing.
Haegarda's efficacy/safety positioning did not remove convenience competition.
These are recollections, not causal estimates of lost sales. Some remarks concerned
Kcentra and cannot be imported into outpatient HAE economics.

MODEL IMPLICATIONS (inferences, not fitted coefficients)
1. Separate stable/satisfied, dissatisfied and untreated/newly diagnosed patients.
   Keep nonzero potential switching among stable patients; estimate its size later.
2. Separate physician reach, willingness to try, prescription submission, successful
   access/first fill, time to paid therapy and continued use. Clinical attractiveness
   cannot substitute for evidence on each transition.
3. Link payer access to negotiated net price and support-service execution; do not
   independently impose overlapping access/abandonment discounts on the same loss.
4. Historical launches need supply, pandemic and access context before their revenue
   slopes can calibrate PHVS. A launch waitlist can confound inference about demand.

WHAT THESE CALLS DO NOT ANSWER
Exact PHVS or comparator sales headcount/territories, prescriber concentration,
account ownership, hub service levels, paid conversion by segment, and comparative
unit economics remain open. No direct Ekterly operations interview was recovered
in this bounded pass. No additional uploads needed to use the four calls found.
Next useful work: issuer launch disclosures/earnings calls and PHVS hiring evidence
for concrete organization plans; then targeted Ekterly and hub-operator evidence
only where the public record leaves a material gap.

METHOD / BIAS CONTROL
One focused AlphaSense Expert Insights query returned15 documents; read four full
transcripts and biographies. All Dates. Result scope is not a library census.
Read transcripts rather than relying on generated summaries. Generic VP titles
were corrected to actual functions; regulatory experience is not sales ownership.
Former-employee incentives, selection bias, mixed geographies/products and stale
expectations limit transfer. Cross-trial superiority statements remain opinions.
No paid commission, bulk scrape, new agent, model update or numerical calibration.
Source dates, URLs, timecodes and claim limitations are in the evidence JSON.
