PHVS commercial preparation and adoption calibration
8 October 2026 | US first | bounded evidence pass

DECISION
PHVS has a coherent launch design with direct HAE precedents. Public evidence does
not support calling the planned field organization obviously inadequate. It also
does not establish that PHVS can deliver the patient conversion and durable paid
use required by valuation. Commercial plans are better evidenced than adoption rates.

The sharper investment test is whether differentiated treatment can recruit and
retain enough patients at an acceptable net price. A generic execution haircut or
an assumption that satisfied incumbents never switch would discard relevant evidence.
No new national peak-sales estimate or probability distribution is justified yet.

READINESS SCORECARD
1. Prescriber targeting — PARTLY SPECIFIED
   The call universe is disclosed; exact specialty/volume tiers and regional account
   lists are not. Historical BioCryst execution reached beyond major centers.
   Evidence: P2; B2021-05-06; B2022-08-04. Model: reach/activation, not patient TAM.

2. Concentration — HISTORICAL BENCHMARK, CURRENT MAP MISSING
   BioCryst reported top500 physicians treating50% of HAE patients. In Q2 2022,
   top120 represented30% of its opportunity and32% of new prescriptions; the next
   approximately1400 represented40% of opportunity and48% of prescriptions. This
   supports a core plus broader-practice strategy; it is not a current national
   census. These groups overlap the top500 segmentation and cannot be summed.
   Evidence: B2022-08-04 p5. Model: consider two reach strata, avoid tiny fitted cells.

3. Field operation — PLAUSIBLE PLAN, DEPLOYMENT UNVERIFIED
   Compare P01 with BioCryst's planned30–50 US reps. Similar scale is a feasibility
   check, not productivity proof. P2 describes avoiding territory disruption between
   launches. Exact account ownership, vacancies and operating territory maps remain
   unknown. Medical liaison postings do not count as sales hires.

4. Proposition — IDENTIFIABLE, CONVERSION UNMEASURED
   P1/P2 distinguish control/tolerability aspirations for XR and reliable rapid oral
   rescue for IR. Management targets former oral users and injectable users as well
   as entrants; commercial positioning does not establish comparative superiority.
   E0/E1 support both treatment interest and stable-patient reluctance. Long-interval
   injection preferences can compete with daily pills. No preference-to-share fit.

5. Prescription-to-paid treatment — FUNCTIONS BEING BUILT, PERFORMANCE UNKNOWN
   P3/P6 specify account, distribution and patient-service responsibilities. The
   national account role seeks payer/PBM relationships and authorization knowledge.
   A removed posting cannot tell us whether the role was filled. No named PHVS
   dispensing/hub partner, service-level contract, conversion cohort, bridge terms
   or negotiated price was verified. Comparator K3 offers coordinator and access
   services; program existence is not measured success. Model: delay, abandonment
   and price jointly, never a second generic access haircut.

6. Persistence — IMPORTANT UPSIDE CLAIM, NO COMMERCIAL VALIDATION
   Historical Orladeyo benchmarks differ:61% at12months in a45-day-gap claims cohort;
   70% among selected paid patients in the2022 company disclosure; selected switchers
   had78%/73% at6months after authorization. These cannot be pooled as a statistical
   interval or applied repeatedly each year. Half of disclosed discontinuations
   occurred within two shipments, supporting distinct initiation/mature hazards.
   Evidence: A04; B2022-05-05 pp5–6. PHVS's much stronger retention illustration is
   a testable hypothesis, not a calibrated base case. No observed PHVS persistence.

7. New patients — CHANNEL INTENT, INCREMENTAL DIAGNOSIS NOT MEASURED
   Disease education and consented outreach may generate treatment discussions with
   existing patients. They do not prove additional diagnoses. P03's150–250 annual
   entrant estimate has no independently verified ascertainment method. Do not
   import Japan's historical underdiagnosis opportunity into the US, or count AAE
   expansion inside the existing HAE population. Model: diagnosis flow, eligibility
   and capture are separate. Family-screening/referral yields remain unknown.

CALIBRATION DECISIONS
- Use a composite prescription-attempt rate per coarse source cohort for now.
  Keep physician reach as an execution diagnostic until independent component rates
  can be estimated. Multiplying multiple unsupported rates adds false precision.
- Add prior oral-treatment experience to every current regimen. A former Orladeyo
  user now taking an injectable belongs in that injectable stock, not an extra pool.
- Keep opt-in status as a marketing flag, never an additional disease population.
- Add explicit pending, bridge/free, paid and discontinued states. Reauthorization
  can temporarily move paid patients to free supply without ending treatment.
- Separate initial rescue stock from refills; choose inventory accounting OR an
  empirical composite dispensing rate. Claims doses averaged across all LTP users
  already incorporate people with zero claims, so do not multiply by rescue-claim
  prevalence again. Successful prevention lowers demand; oral rescue can increase
  the fraction treated or reduce redosing. Model those mechanisms together.
- Keep subtype/age/label eligibility explicit. Neither a corporate population
  estimate nor a small subgroup efficacy signal establishes the reimbursable pool.
- Shared sales infrastructure can reduce incremental costs; it does not prove
  same-patient dual-product use. IR adoption can affect subsequent XR consideration.
  Paid IR volume can then decline as prevention improves. Couple these transitions.

Neither the management retention illustration nor its diagnosis estimate is observed

Arithmetic diagnostics: in identical hypothetical100-start cohorts, applying95%
instead of61% retention leaves34 more patients at12months, a56% stock difference.
Versus70%, the difference is25 patients or36%. These are assumption sensitivities,
not head-to-head outcomes or annual revenue uplifts. They explain why retention
deserves as much scrutiny as initial switching. The check file also shows a gross
five-year ceiling of750–1250 entrants under management's flow estimate, assuming
100% eligibility/capture and zero exits. That hypothetical flow cannot alone be
treated as the whole launch opportunity; existing-patient recruitment still matters.

PRIORITY UNKNOWNS / CONCRETE RESOLUTION
1. Current eligible treatment stocks and history: need deduplicated US age/subtype,
   current regimen, prior oral exposure and confirmed diagnosis date. Manufacturer
   stocks plus a consistent claims panel can help; current public sources do not
   reconcile a national partition. Do not silently use9000 as a validated denominator.
2. Attempted and successful switches: need considered/submitted/denied/approved,
   first-supply dates and free/paid status by prior regimen. Hub plus pharmacy records
   can answer; claims alone miss free supply and unsubmitted prescriptions.
3. Persistence: need inception cohorts, gaps, restarts, competing destinations and
   reasons, plus at-risk counts. Longer follow-up must identify mature survival.
4. IR economics: need pack size, price concessions, first and repeat shipments,
   prophylaxis background and observation time. Diaries can distinguish inventory
   from consumption; dispensing alone cannot establish attacks treated.
5. Execution milestone verification: target/territory deployment, filled field and
   access roles, partner/service agreements, payer terms and onboarding readiness.
   Company primary disclosures can advance this without proprietary patient records.

These missing inputs have higher information value than more unweighted doctor
opinions. Linked commercial records would require a separately scoped access/paid
data decision. No such collection was initiated. The next quantitative use is a
conditional threshold/sensitivity build with explicit user-selected assumptions,
not an allegedly data-calibrated national forecast.

VALUATION CONNECTION
Retain the dated reverse-DCF hurdle as conditional worldwide success-case revenue.
Before converting it into US share, specify ex-US economics and realized net prices.
XR required paid patient-years = US XR revenue / annual net price per paid patient.
IR required paid packs = US IR revenue / net pack price; required users additionally
depend on refill intensity. No current price refresh or reverse-DCF change was made.
An end-year patient count is not patient-years, and a single broker ramp is not Street.
