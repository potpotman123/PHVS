PHVS launch analogues — bounded primary-source audit, 7 October 2026

Question: What can observed oral rare-disease launches tell us about switching, persistence, paid access and deucrictibant prophylaxis revenue? Deucrictibant, not deucravacitinib, is the PHVS candidate.

PATIENT DISCLOSURES / LIMITATIONS
January 2022: >half of starts since launch had previous prophylaxis; most remaining patients previously acute-only. Does not identify all prior prophylaxis as injectables. Approximately 70% of starters remained during first year; disclosure lacks standardized cohort follow-up definition, so not an exact 12-month survival curve.
December 2023: 1,104 US patients on paid or long-term free product, 71.5% paid. Derived approximately 789 paid and 315 long-term free, rounding applies. Net additions 321 include short-term quick-start patients, a different denominator; do not subtract321 from1104 to infer prior stock.
June 2024: paid rate74.4%, up2.9 percentage points from year-end2023. More than60% of patients on paid therapy stay at least one year. Cohort differs from2021; cannot infer retention deterioration. Paid share is not discount from list price and does not equal probability of conversion for a new start.
No complete public monthly prescription/new-to-brand/unique-paid-patient series recovered in bounded pass. Revenue cannot isolate switching inertia: price, geography, persistence, paid share and new starts all contribute. Global revenue divided by US year-end patients is not annual net revenue per patient.

ANALOGUE SCREEN
Cerdelga/eliglustat after Zavesca/miglustat in Gaucher type1: genuine prior oral and established effective infused therapy. FDA approved2014; global reported sales2015 EUR66m,2016 EUR106m,2017 EUR126m. FY2015 original report says66, not an inferred67. Different currencies/market sizes prevent direct absolute comparison with Orladeyo. Cerdelga's first-line positioning and CYP2D6 eligibility, prior oral restrictions, and Sanofi's incumbent franchise prevent treating this as a matched efficacy-delta experiment. Useful second-oral existence proof, not PHVS share estimate.
Livmarli after Bylvay in PFIC screened but not selected: aggregated Livmarli sales include earlier ALGS indication, so brand ramp does not isolate PFIC launch.
Danazol is an older oral androgen; Orladeyo was not literally the first oral HAE prophylaxis. Historical launch economics and product profile are insufficiently comparable for direct calibration.

MODEL IMPLICATIONS
Use same-disease Orladeyo as principal commercial calibration. Segment new starts by dissatisfied oral users, stable oral users, injectable users and acute-only/new-to-prophylaxis users. For each monthly cohort, calculate starts * probability still on therapy at month t * probability paid conditional on remaining at month t * monthly net revenue. Sum across cohorts. Start timing and therapy/free-paid transitions matter; avoid multiplying endpoint retention and endpoint paid share into annual revenue as though constant all year.
No evidence establishes a universal switching-cost haircut. Similar efficacy deltas across diseases cannot be asserted; no PHVS-versus-Orladeyo randomized head-to-head is supplied. Strong observed launch is disconfirming evidence against the broad claim that HAE stickiness blocks oral uptake. Free treatment and discontinuation support testing monetization and persistence assumptions.
Next high-information evidence: deidentified specialty-pharmacy/hub or longitudinal claims cohorts with prior therapy, actual first dispense, free/paid status, 90/180/365-day persistence, switching destination and reason. Specialty-channel completeness and missing free-drug claims must be audited. No paid dataset purchased or bulk collection performed.
