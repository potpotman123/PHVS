PHVS US PATIENT-ADOPTION EVIDENCE — 7 OCTOBER 2026

The bounded search recovered actual switching, product-specific persistence,
rescue dispensing and newer paid-share evidence. These support a model with
observable historical benchmarks, but do not identify Pharvaris's capture rate
or the current national treatment mix. Clinical superiority remains unresolved.

WHAT CHANGED

The strongest change favors neither a simple long nor a simple short. HAE
prevention is demonstrably contestable: people change treatment. However,
winning an initial prescription does not guarantee continuing paid demand.
Prevention and rescue also overlap economically; they are not mutually
exclusive patient markets. All quantitative references below map to source
IDs and fully defined observations in the accompanying evidence ledger.

1. PATIENT POOLS: A USEFUL ANCHOR, NOT A COMPLETE CURRENT TAM

BioCryst reports >1,600 US Orladeyo patients on therapy at year-end 2025 and
>3,500 who had tried it since launch [S1]. Active patients include QuickStart,
paid and patient-assistance supply. Neither subtracting nor dividing these
lower-bound stock/cumulative figures establishes discontinuation or retention.

The existing claims study projects 6,595 expert-adjudicated versus 8,694
unadjusted treated US patients in 2020 [S8]. KalVista's approximately 9,000
diagnosed-patient estimate is a later company estimate [S9]. These provide
planning anchors, not a measured 2026 confidence interval. Definitions, ages,
subtypes, insurance capture and observation dates differ.

Current injectable-prevention and acute-only stocks remain unmeasured. Do not
subtract the Orladeyo figure from an old total and label the remainder
injectable users. Maintain an other-prevention/unknown category alongside the
requested oral, injectable and acute-only groups. New diagnoses and genuinely
new prevention starts also remain unknown. Initiation-cohort drug shares are
not national market shares.

2. SWITCHING AND PERSISTENCE: DIRECT HAE BENCHMARKS

In a commercial-claims cohort, 50/328 patients (15.2%, derived) received a
different preventive drug within a year of initiation [S3]. This is a useful
historical transition benchmark. It is not a 15.2% annual opportunity among
stable incumbents or the probability of switching to PHVS.

A linked EHR/claims study reports 12-month persistence of 61% for berotralstat
(n=90), 58% for lanadelumab (n=189), and 53% for subcutaneous plasma-derived
C1 inhibitor (n=78), using no gap of at least 45 days [S2]. Use these as
new-start cohort benchmarks, not evidence that every incumbent loses roughly
40% annually. Stable survivors may behave differently. The study is not a
randomized comparison. BioCryst repeats its results; that is not independent
validation.

A separate Komodo study finds 58.7% pooled 12-month persistence (95% CI
53.8–63.4), using a 90-day gap or switching [S4]. Its 56/499 switches occurred
over variable follow-up, not one year. Do not pool these studies or average
their percentages. Different rules can produce different commercial meanings.

The clinic registry adds destination evidence: among 83 discontinuers, 41
changed LTP, seven restarted the same LTP and 35 had no subsequent LTP
recorded [S5]. These are variable-duration histories; no subsequent record
does not establish permanent acute-only treatment.

3. REASONS TO SWITCH: MECHANISMS, NOT NATIONAL CAPTURE RATES

Among 76 clinic discontinuers with known reasons, 29% cited inadequate efficacy,
22% administration/dosing, 14% adverse events and 11% payer/payment issues
[S5]. Reasons are nonexclusive and conditional on stopping. They do not mean
14% of all treated patients stop because of adverse effects. The paper's
4.8-month berotralstat median describes 14 discontinuers only, not median
persistence among all users.

A US preference study found 97/201 current LTP users extremely satisfied,
while 197/250 respondents expressed openness to a preferred-route medication
[S6]. Both can be true. Its fitted preference classes distinguish efficacy
from treatment-burden priorities, supporting heterogeneous adoption. Neither
the class weights nor stated openness should become national switching rates.
The advocacy-group sample is selected; S4–S6 were Ionis funded. Sponsor
involvement warrants scrutiny, not automatic rejection of the observations.

4. ACCESS: UPDATE THE BENCHMARK, PRESERVE THE DENOMINATOR

Orladeyo's paid share was 84% at Q1-end 2025 and 81% at year-end 2025,
excluding QuickStart [S1]. This updates the historical 71.5% December 2023
benchmark. It measures the composition of the active population, not the
eventual conversion probability of a new prescription. Multiplying 81% by
the >1,600 active count is invalid because their denominators differ.

Public first-paid-fill delays, bridge conversion and abandonment by prior
regimen were not recovered. More unweighted payer policies will not repair
this gap. The earlier UHC benefit-specific correction remains in force.

Ekterly's 1,702 US start forms through February 2026 and $49.1m global revenue
through December 2025 remain useful launch signals [S10], but cannot identify
paid patient conversion: the units, dates and geography differ.

5. RESCUE DEMAND: OWNERSHIP, DISPENSING AND CONSUMPTION DIFFER

In the same 328-person claims cohort, 220 (67.1%) had a rescue claim in the
year after starting prevention. Mean claims-derived rescue doses were 11.8
per patient-year overall and 8.0 among 147 patients with minimal refill gaps
[S3]. These are historical dispensing benchmarks, not attacks consumed or a
deucrictibant forecast. Baseline burden, treatment gaps, stocking, free supply
and product dosing confound interpretation.

The fraction of attacks treated, doses per attack, initial stocking and expiry
replacement remain unidentified. Do not set rescue consumption to zero when
someone starts XR, or count every rescue owner as a recurring heavy user.

WHAT CAN ENTER A MODEL NOW

Historical Orladeyo stock/paid-share anchors; separately defined initiation
persistence and switching benchmarks; historical rescue dispensing intensity;
and evidence supporting multiple switch mechanisms. These calibrate plausible
scenarios and consistency checks. None directly supplies PHVS adoption.

Explicit scenarios are still required for current segment sizes, PHVS capture
by prior regimen/control, paid conversion timing, comparative persistence,
net price and rescue intensity. Keep efficacy/control linked to switching and
rescue demand, and access linked to paid conversion and net price. Avoid
independently maximizing XR share and IR use. Combined IR/XR use also depends
on clinical evidence and eventual labeling. No valuation or Monte Carlo was
performed.

HIGHEST-VALUE MISSING INPUT AND WHETHER COLLECTION IS WORTH IT

The key gap is the probability of a paid, persistent transition by previous
regimen and disease control, including the denominator who could switch but
do not. This would distinguish contestable demand from a stock of satisfied
incumbents better than another general preference survey.

A minimum useful specification combines patient identifiers, prior products,
treatment tenure, refill dates/quantities, enrollment, and competing destination
products, with 6–12 months of lookback and at least 12 months of follow-up.
Hub/pharmacy records must add prescription receipt, approval/rejection,
reversals, bridge/PAP supply and first paid fill. Clinical records or diaries
must add control and actual attacks; claims alone cannot distinguish voluntary
switches, treatment failure and access barriers reliably.

Existing studies demonstrate feasibility in selected samples, not complete
national coverage. BioCryst announced CareMed would become the sole-source
Orladeyo pharmacy in Q3 2026 [S7]; historical Optime-only extraction could
misclassify a channel transfer as discontinuation. Exact availability, linkage,
sample size and cost remain unknown. No vendor or clinician was contacted.

Recommendation: use these benchmarks to define transparent adoption scenarios
first. Additional collection is worthwhile if the long/short decision changes
across plausible transition assumptions and a provider can demonstrate aligned
denominators, channel continuity and paid status. A targeted physician audit
of recent actual decisions may clarify reasons more cheaply, but cannot
replace dispensing records or establish national incidence. Stop if only
stated intentions, unlinked starts or broad revenue proxies are available.
