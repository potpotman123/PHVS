DECISION
Use pooled small-molecule evidence as the broad benchmark, with matched rare-disease
cases as modifiers. The existing Kuvan-only curve is a provisional scenario, not a
validated PHVS base case. No workbook inputs changed in this audit: the recovered
pooled evidence does not identify a matched annual manufacturer-net-revenue curve.

AUDIT OF THE THREE CASES
Kuvan is useful as a chronic rare-disease oral analogue, more relevant to XR than
attack-driven IR. Its worldwide 2020–24 revenue series mixes territories and generic
entry timing; 2020 already contains US generic exposure. Competing therapies and
changes in treatment mix can also affect sales. It cannot isolate generic causality.
Keep it as an observed branded-revenue scenario, with limited transferability.
Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1048477/000104847724000016/bmrn-20231231.htm

Xenazine offers a specialty oral comparison but its 2015 anchor is partly exposed.
Austedo's April 2017 approval introduces branded competition into later declines.
Its commercial setting therefore does not establish a pure generic erosion curve.
Keep as corroboration, not an independent clean estimate of the same parameter.
Sources: https://www.lundbeck.com/content/dam/lundbeck-com/masters/global-site/global-site/investors/reports-and-presentations-/2016/Q4-2016-release.pdf
https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/opdlisting/oopd/detailedIndex.cfm?cfgridkey=383212

Orfadin is the weakest clean calibration. Sobi reported 2017 sales SEK862m versus
770m while generic competition increased; North American growth was mainly driven
by 20mg and oral-suspension launches. Its later 2020–21 decline is not an entry-year
experiment. Use it to examine formulation protection, not to set generic attrition.
An XR label alone does not establish similar protection for deucrictibant.
Source: https://www.sobi.com/sites/default/files/pr/2017_Q4_sobi_presentation.pdf (slide17)

POOLED BENCHMARKS — DIFFERENT ENDPOINTS, NOT THREE INDEPENDENT REVENUE CURVES
Grabowski et al. (2021) examined 356 NMEs with initial generic entry during 1995–2019.
For the 2017–19 entry cohort, average branded unit share after one year was 23%
overall and 18% for products with pre-entry sales above $250m in 2008 dollars.
These are market shares, not retention of starting brand volume or revenue. The
total market can change; 356 is the full study sample, not the recent cohort size.
Source: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.1080/13696998.2021.1952795

A 2025 observational study covers 530 US active substances. Generic volume share
averaged 90% after five generic competitors entered. US originator prices did not
show the same downward association with competitor counts as generics. Its price
data omit confidential rebates; competitor count is not elapsed time.
Source: https://bmjpublichealth.bmj.com/content/3/2/e002535

Health Affairs (5 October 2026) studies 271 small-molecule compounds with first
generic entry in 2011–23 using Part D claims and confidential rebate information.
It reports >90% average loss of branded prescriptions after entry and a doubling
of brand net prices between post-entry years1 and4. This supports separate P/Q
assumptions, not a universal brand-price cut. Its 57% decline in total net spending
includes generics and authorized generics: it is not originator revenue erosion.
Source: https://www.healthaffairs.org/doi/10.1377/hlthaff.2026.00257
Applicability limits: Medicare population, pooled formulations, changing follow-up
sample and weighted normalized averages. Never multiply separately pooled average
price and volume paths as though that equals average molecule revenue. Proprietary
rebate inputs cannot be reproduced from public CMS spending files.

HOW TO BUILD THE EVENT-ALIGNED PANEL
Define the target as originator net-revenue retention after actual substitutable
generic launch, by geography and formulation. Patent expiry and FDA approval are
not equivalent to commercial generic entry. Normalize each product to its last
unexposed four quarters; retain the partial-entry year separately.

Use a prespecified broad oral small-molecule cohort, then compare subgroups by
pre-entry dollar sales, entrant count, chronic versus episodic dosing, formulation
barriers and distribution. Rare disease alone is insufficient matching. Exclude
or flag divestitures, changing territorial rights, new indications, replacement
formulations and major new branded competition. Maintain an inclusion/exclusion
ledger before examining outcomes to limit selection by attractive-looking curves.

For each molecule compute annual net-revenue retention first; summarize medians,
quartiles and equal-weight means with sample counts. Report sales-weighted means
separately. Use balanced-follow-up sensitivity and leave-one-out checks. Do not
splice countries, infer unavailable prices, or treat a missing report as zero.

Model brand revenue as branded standardized volume × realized net price. Model
branded volume as total molecule demand × retained brand share. Add authorized-
generic royalties/profits separately only if PHVS has such economics. Use product-
specific evidence for any price/volume correlation; absent that, scenarios remain
assumptions. IR and XR require separate entry dates and formulation assessments.

STOPPING POINT / MODEL READINESS
This bounded pass recovered aggregate research and a public P×Q check without paid
data or bulk company scraping. It did not construct a new many-product manufacturer
revenue panel. Published pooled evidence is sufficient to reject the presumption
that rare-disease brand revenue must decline mainly through price cuts; it cannot
yet supply an exact PHVS annual erosion curve. The existing Kuvan case can remain
a sensitivity, but should not be promoted to a calibrated central estimate.
Further collection is justified if erosion sensitivity changes the investment
conclusion; then a matched product-level panel is preferable to a larger unfiltered
average. No new model valuation, price target or perpetual decline is asserted.
