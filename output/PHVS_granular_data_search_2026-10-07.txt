Bounded search for individual and subgroup clinical data
7 October 2026

RESULT
Recovered useful additional aggregate evidence, particularly a RAPIDe-3 analysis including participants with unpaired attacks. No downloadable participant/attack-level dataset, new CHAPTER-3 switch counts, final PHVS SAP, or numerical amendment sensitivity results were recovered. The new evidence supports robustness checks; it does not enable a defensible patient-adjusted comparative superiority estimate.

METHOD AND STOPPING RULE
Checked existing repository and four current ClinicalTrials.gov API records; searched publication/supplement, sponsor conference, regulatory, and data-sharing sources. Followed concrete leads to the September 2026 RAPIDe-3 regional presentation, October German poster, June 2026 pooled sebetralstat paper, and CHAPTER-1 publication sharing policy. Reused prior FDA reviews and CTIS retrieval findings. NEJM appendix/data-sharing PDF retrieval failed; EMA assessment-report fetch also failed. An indexed Vivli search produced no trial-specific access listing; this is not proof that no listing exists. No account-based request, bulk collection, outreach, or patient-data acquisition was performed. Stop here because the missing linked records require sponsor/regulatory disclosure, and repeated aggregate searches offer diminishing returns.

NEW RAPIDe-3 REGIONAL ANALYSIS
September3-4,2026 Bradykinin Symposium deck, slides7,9-14; sponsor source. Slide7 explicitly includes all participants with treated attacks in subgroup analyses, whereas its overall reference result uses the paired set. This partly fills the earlier gap concerning outcomes outside the 88 pairs; it is not the full prespecified SEAS model output.
Slide9 randomized regional cohorts: North America38, Europe/rest of world96. Current LTP13/38 (34.2%) versus18/96 (18.8%). Mean attacks in the three months before screening3.4(SD2.5) versus4.8(SD3.5; n94). These are randomized baseline cohorts, not necessarily the analyzed-attack distribution. Regions differ on more than prophylaxis, so regional contrasts cannot identify an LTP effect.
Slide14 onset-of-relief median: active1.20h/placebo6.55h in North America; active1.28h/placebo>12h in Europe/rest of world. Observed four-hour response proportions:14/18 (77.8%) versus12/26 (46.2%) North America;66/78 (84.6%) versus19/72 (26.4%) elsewhere. Thus apparent regional placebo heterogeneity coexists with similar active medians, but small samples and different populations prevent causal attribution or formal heterogeneity claims.

WHAT CAN NOW BE DONE
Regional and severity subgroup evidence can enter a qualitative robustness discussion. Do not enter a numerical efficacy/switching multiplier into the commercial model from these sources. A plot-digitization reconstruction could approximate marginal event-time curves but cannot restore attack severity, treatment delay, crossover pairing, rescue events, or missing diaries; not worth presenting as true individual data. A matching-adjusted comparison needs IPD for at least one trial plus suitable comparator summaries and assumptions about all important effect modifiers; that threshold remains unmet. Prevention still needs CHAPTER-3 switch counts/timing, exact assigned attack rates, and alternative analysis results.
Smallest useful next disclosure package: anonymized linked attack records (sequence, onset/dose/assessment times, PGI-S/PGI-C, missingness, rescue, LTP, genotype) for acute trials; baseline/follow-up exposure, attacks, treatment discontinuation/switch dates, assigned values, and final sensitivity tables for prevention. A sponsor-produced harmonized subgroup table would be a lower-cost partial alternative. No requests sent.
