PHVS revenue-comparability audit | 8 October 2026
Question: Can Orladeyo and Ekterly revenue histories calibrate deucrictibant revenue potential?
Conclusion: Yes, paired by setting (XR/Orladeyo; IR/Ekterly), with material adjustments. No evidence that equal efficacy or equal patient counts imply equal sales. No model coefficients changed.

1. Daily versus episodic economics. XR and Orladeyo monetize paid time on prevention. IR and Ekterly monetize paid dispensing, which depends on attacks, fraction treated, repeat doses, inventory and stocking/replacement. Prevention success need not reduce preventive dosing; rescue success can reduce redosing. Same setting comparators remain appropriate.
2. Dose and pack mismatch. Ekterly standard starting dose is two 300mg tablets, with a second 600mg dose permitted >=3h later (max1200mg/24h); interaction-adjusted dosing differs. PHVS trials use20mg IR single capsule. Milligrams/tablets/packs cannot be compared directly as economic units. PHVS commercial price, package, realized discounts and refill policies remain unverified. No proven comparative repeat-dose advantage; PHVS placebo-controlled data do not establish superiority over Ekterly.
3. Market position. First oral rescue and first modern targeted oral prophylaxis can recruit injection-averse patients; PHVS can recruit dissatisfied oral users and remaining injectable/untreated patients. Second entrant loses novelty but inherits awareness and prescribing infrastructure. First-mover and later-entrant curves are not mechanically transferable.
4. Eligible population. Current Orladeyo includes age2+, unlike its initial12+ launch population. Ekterly current reviewed label12+; PHVS pivotal IR12+. Match age and launch-era labels; do not credit hypothetical PHVS pediatric expansion or acquired-angioedema expansion. Normal-C1-INH inclusion in PHVS research does not automatically establish incremental reimbursable population relative to broad HAE incumbent labels.
5. Both settings and MOA. XR lowers breakthrough attack volume but not necessarily rescue ownership. This affects all rescue agents, not uniquely same-MOA rescue. PHVS shared call points may lower commercial costs; that is a margin benefit, not automatically more revenue. Same-molecule rescue after XR breakthrough remains a prescribing/label evidence question, not an established contraindication. Do not impose same-MOA exclusion or automatic two-product capture.
6. Positioning restrictions. Orladeyo food/GI and drug-interaction considerations; Ekterly CYP3A4 restrictions. PHVS proposed tolerability/convenience advantages could alter acceptance/retention, but final label and comparative commercial effects are not established. Molecular target alone establishes neither payer tier nor addressable patient count.
7. Access and net price. Compare matched setting and payer. Orladeyo preventive step rules do not automatically apply to acute IR. Rebate-driven price/access tradeoffs, free bridge, abandoned prescriptions and persistent paid supply differ by company. PHVS terms unknown; avoid equal net price assumption by default.
8. Accounting and geography. Ekterly global revenue includes Germany while patient-start disclosures are US; start forms are not paid users. Q42025 refills >50% revenue, with potential holiday pull-forward: evidence of recurring demand, not annualized refill intensity. BioCryst2025 European transaction generated licensing revenue; isolate product net sales and match territory. Channel inventory, launch partial years, historic FX and later price increases also confound raw curves. Royalties and shared selling costs belong in cash-flow conversion, distinct from end-market sales.

Priority observed inputs: annual paid packs per IR user by prophylaxis status; doses per treated attack; net realized price and pack definition; XR paid months and source-treatment mix; matched age/geography/launch-age sales. Unknowns remain unknown rather than becoming illustrative fitted adoption coefficients.
