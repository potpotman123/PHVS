CENTRAL FINDING
The interviews support real clinical enthusiasm, but show that enthusiasm, prescriptions, paid starts and durable revenue are different events. The best short case is constrained switching, access friction, low rescue consumption and competing convenience. The best long case is a better-tolerated, credible oral preventive plus an oral B2 rescue option that can complement existing injected prophylaxis. Same-mechanism rescue is a disputed commercial preference, not an established pharmacological failure.

SAME-MECHANISM DISPUTE: ADJUDICATION
E4 at 13:39 assumes a breakthrough during prophylaxis occurs while all relevant receptors are already blocked. That is not demonstrated: a competitive antagonist may leave inadequate exposure at the time of breakthrough. Additional acute exposure could therefore help. Conversely, a different mechanism may be a reasonable physician preference and could limit dual-product prescribing even without proof of biological superiority.
Pharvaris 20 Apr 2026 describes exposure-based combined IR/XR safety margins and a post-hoc CHAPTER-1 analysis of icatibant-treated breakthrough attacks during deucrictibant prevention. This is counterevidence to categorical same-target futility, not a randomized trial establishing IR-on-XR superiority or universal coverage. Treat prescriber acceptance, clinical efficacy, safety and joint reimbursement as four separate questions.
https://ir.pharvaris.com/news-releases/news-release-details/evidence-supporting-combined-use-deucrictibant-ir-deucrictibant

QUALITY CONTROL: CLAIMS NOT TO IMPORT INTO THE PITCH
1. E3/E5 repeatedly conflate Takhzyro with B2 receptor antagonism. Takhzyro targets plasma kallikrein; deucrictibant and icatibant target B2. Therefore the same- versus different-mechanism commercial argument must use the corrected map.
2. E3/E5 imply antibody-driven waning makes small molecules future-proof. FDA Takhzyro labeling states observed anti-drug antibodies, including neutralizing antibodies, did not appear to impair PK, PD, safety or clinical response. That does not rule out individual loss of response, but refutes treating antibody failure as established general attrition.
https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/761090s020lbl.pdf
3. E4 says pemivibart for three-/six-month HAE prevention. The relevant HAE development candidate is navenibart; this may be a verbal or transcription error. Do not reproduce the wrong name.
https://ir.biocryst.com/node/27821/html
4. E2 and E1 wording sometimes mixes attack-rate reduction, control and proportion attack-free. 83%/87% reduction does not mean 83%/87% of patients have no attacks. Existing CHAPTER-3 source reports 25/55 (45.5%) attack-free over 24 weeks; this is not a head-to-head comparison with another trial.
5. E3 implies no severe or serious events. CHAPTER-3 sponsor table shows three active patients maximum grade 3, one grade 4 and one serious event; none of the serious events judged treatment-related. Keep severity, seriousness and relatedness distinct.
https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1830487/000119312526384472/phvs-ex99_1.htm
6. E3/E5 speculate broad efficacy across angioedema and diagnosis clinched by response to a sample. A response can support diagnosis but is not standalone confirmation; all angioedema is not bradykinin-mediated. Sparse normal-C1 trial enrollment is not proof of all-subtype efficacy. Claims that confirmed HAE responds to antihistamines/steroids require clarification of diagnosis/coexisting conditions.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41189715/
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40053270/
7. E2/E3/E5 assume same manufacturer means one authorization, one medicine or a package deal. No demonstrated PHVS contracting or automatic cross-coverage. Separate IR/XR dosing and benefits must be assessed. Do not assume the same tablet serves both uses.
8. E5 characterization of European recommendations as favoring C1 replacement over other mechanisms should not be treated as the current international guideline: WAO/EAACI recognizes multiple first-line options. Historical recollections, dates and absolute cross-trial efficacy rankings are not independently verified by these calls.
9. E3/E5 ZENITH history, cause of discontinued acute development, and commercial population counts remain unverified recollections; do not use as evidence that a pandemic caused discontinuation or as current market sizing.

MODEL TRANSLATION
For prevention: segment otherwise-eligible patients into inadequate-control/intolerance switches, stable convenience switches and new preventive starts. Model consideration at a treatment-review visit, patient choice, office willingness to submit, approval, paid conversion, months retained and net price. Office burden occurs before submission, so denial rates alone miss suppressed demand.
For acute: separate competitor-prevention users, PHVS-XR users and acute-only patients. For each estimate residual attacks, fraction treated, PHVS share and doses per treated attack. Then reconcile consumption to paid dispensing, patient inventory, expiry and free supply. Better prevention reduces events; oral convenience may increase treatment propensity; earlier relief may reduce repeat doses. Do not set all three assumptions independently to favorable extremes.
For dual franchise: do not assume automatic IR attachment to XR. Mechanism-diversifying physicians can favor PHVS IR on competitor prevention and competitor rescue on PHVS XR. Better rescue can also move some patients away from prevention. Shared patients cannot be counted twice as unique TAM, although two separately paid products can generate revenue.
For diagnosis expansion: separate increased awareness and family screening from new disease incidence. Confirmed subtype, symptoms, diagnostic workups and reimbursed patients are different denominators. Acquired disease is a separate optional expansion.
None of the quoted switching percentages is ready for base-case share. Suitable use: define segments, plausible directions and sensitivity cases. Not suitable: average five opinions, add patient rosters, multiply TAM by excitement scores, or treat local subtype shares as population prevalence.

STRONGEST COMPETING PITCHES
Short steelman: Good efficacy is insufficient to displace well-controlled patients. Small practices face unpaid switching work; oral preference does not ensure approval or paid conversion; infrequent injections can be more convenient; low breakthrough frequency limits acute replenishment; second-to-market IR must overcome existing oral access and habit. Dual-franchise forecasts may overstate attachment because some clinicians diversify mechanisms and easier acute treatment can substitute for prevention.
Long steelman: A credible oral preventive can address patients frustrated by Orladeyo efficacy/GI effects and those who refuse injections. Oral B2 rescue fits existing kallikrein-based prevention, so PHVS need not win XR to build IR demand. Easier acute treatment can increase treatment of previously untreated attacks. Shared familiarity and a strong support program may lower practical friction. Awareness and adjacent indications provide optionality, although their size is unproven.
What would disconfirm short: rapid paid conversion; many controlled injectable users switch and remain; sustained low abandonment; robust IR adoption on competitor prevention; low office workload; realized net pricing supports attractive economics.
What would disconfirm long: starts largely free; low paid conversion; switches mainly small existing oral pool; stable patients decline; daily adherence or chronic adverse events reduce retention; dual attachment weak; infrequent injections win convenience preference; normal-C1 expansion denied or unsupported.
