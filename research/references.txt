1. PHAT: what the example actually establishes
The deck has a useful variant-perception structure: narrow an overstated population, accept much of the bear's TAM objection, then identify a different measurable earnings driver. Slide 9 argues that symptomatic PPI failure overstates acid-mediated disease; slide 10 substitutes better capture/adherence under GI prescribing as the main upside driver; slide 11 specifies the market-implied assumption and forecast variance. Slides 12–13 address execution costs and timing, slide 15 lists an event path, and slides 18–21 connect to valuation. This structure transfers to PHVS: separate clinical utility, eligible patients, adoption, treatment intensity, net revenue, costs, and valuation rather than treating trial success as a share-price thesis.

However, slide 10 blends four quantities that are not interchangeable: correct administration timing (71% GI vs 47% PCP in a cited survey), one-year PPI persistence by severity (27%/54%/73%), aggregate refill share, and anecdotal 80–90% refill experience. A higher proportion of repeat prescriptions can arise because new starts slow, even when patient retention does not improve. Prescriber case mix and selection also confound a causal GI effect. The workbook calls its scalar “% capture / adherence,” while the slide and sensitivity table call it a refill rate. The user should copy the causal question, not those definitions.

2. M Loses Capital: retain the tests, reject unwarranted certainty
Part 2: Retain incentive analysis, scrutiny of selected endpoints and populations, distinction between biomarkers and outcomes, source independence, and prospective calibration of forecasts. Credentials and financing are insufficient proof of efficacy. But an unconditional roughly 90% drug-development failure statistic is not the appropriate prior for every late-stage, target-validated asset. Management communication can contain verifiable facts; incentive conflict warrants corroboration, not automatically zero weight. No unrelated factual claims in the article were checked here.

Part 3: Transfer its questions about target biology, selectivity, exposure at the relevant tissue, duration, metabolites, safety margins, and meaningful benefit versus incumbents. Molecular resemblance or “stickiness” alone cannot establish clinical equivalence or delivery. For PHVS, examine formulation-specific exposure and trough coverage separately from acute rescue, and require direct clinical evidence for both uses.

TTRX: Strong methods include comparing preclinical disease construction with human pathophysiology, testing an active vehicle, recovering integer denominators, assessing baseline imbalance, and reconstructing registry/endpoint/subgroup chronology. Independently recomputing two-sided Fisher tests from supplied 27-versus-23 counts reproduces approximately .030 (25/27 vs 15/23), .387 (12/27 vs 7/23), and .264 (14/27 vs 8/23). Those checks validate arithmetic only. Nonsignificance does not prove no effect, and a tiny 11-patient study of another intervention cannot establish a hard efficacy ceiling for a whole mechanism. Post-hoc enrichment can generate a prospectively testable hypothesis; it is not confirmation. For PHVS, prioritize ITT, prespecification, multiplicity, attack selection, missingness and replicate evidence.

ZURA: The useful question is whether biological heterogeneity explains aggregate efficacy and whether dual targeting reaches additional patients. The article goes too far: an assumed 33% endotype prevalence and assumed 38.3% response in everyone else imply approximately 78.6% subgroup response, but do not observe it. Many latent mixtures yield the same aggregate. Supplied 16/31 versus 23/60 gives a 13.28-point difference and an approximate unadjusted Wald 95% interval of −8.19 to +34.75 points. This is not the registry's Bayesian analysis and does not confirm a biological subgroup signal. Reasons for withdrawal must be observed, not inferred as nonresponse. The text also shifts between graded, overlapping T-cell response and mutually exclusive nonresponding endotypes. Transfer the heterogeneity hypothesis to PHVS only if patient-level or prespecified subgroup evidence distinguishes it from alternatives.

SLGL: Retain the requirement for adequate drug exposure in the compartment where disease arises and the obligation to explain what changed after a failed study. Similar physicochemical properties across molecules are a clue, not a validated PK bridge; formulation, tissue state and potency matter. A smaller sample lowers power only holding effect size, variability, allocation and design constant. Values below assay detection are not proven biological zero, so division by “zero” does not establish infinite delivery enhancement. For PHVS, the relevant test is actual formulation/dose exposure and randomized clinical outcomes, not analogical certainty.
