DECISION FINDINGS
1. Both pivotal efficacy readouts already exist (RAPIDe-3 3 Dec 2025; CHAPTER-3 8 Sep 2026). A prediction of basic Phase 3 efficacy is stale. Remaining investable uncertainties are breadth/robustness of the label, chronic safety/exposure, comparative value and commercial adoption.
2. RAPIDe-3's headline enrollment is 134, but primary efficacy is 88 patients completing BOTH crossover attacks (176 attacks), 65.7% of randomized. 113 treated at least one attack, so 21 treated none and 25 treated only one (derived). This is a paired attack analysis, not conventional all-randomized ITT. No evidence supports calling the 46 absent from the primary set toxicity/nonresponse dropouts. First-period/all-treated sensitivity results and reason-by-sequence disposition were not located.
3. Rescue rules materially define efficacy: RAPIDe-3 posters say attacks with rescue use are right censored at the UPPER END of the data-entry window; other unachieved events are censored at last observation before that upper end. This is not ordinary censor-at-rescue. Interpretation is relief under the trial's rescue-accounting rule, not actual elapsed time to relief including effective rescue. Need full SAP to resolve events preceding later rescue and precise windows. Placebo median not reached within12h/48h cannot be interpreted as placebo patients literally remaining untreated that long.
4. CHAPTER-3: adjusted rate0.35 versus2.06 attacks per FOUR WEEKS,83% reduction(95%CI72–90),p<0.0001. Absolute difference1.71/4wk; over24wk,10.26 fewer estimated attacks (simple scaling, not new model estimate). 25/55(45.5%) versus1/30(3.3%) attack free; more than half of treated patients still had an attack. Attack-rate reduction is not proportion cured, nor a direct commercial switching probability.
5. Normal-C1INH breadth is sparsely demonstrated: RAPIDe3 only4/134 randomized (genetically documented cases); CHAPTER3 only5/85 (3 active,2 placebo). Type1/2 CHAPTER3 reduction87% is an additional subgroup analysis without multiplicity adjustment. Overall83% versus subgroup87% cannot identify the five patients' true response by a prevalence-weighted mixture of adjusted rate ratios.
6. IR/XR same molecule, different dosing/exposure. CHAPTER1 was IR twice daily, not intended commercial once-daily XR. CHAPTER3 directly tested commercial XR and therefore substantially resolves the simple formulation efficacy bridge. Joint-use IR+XR safety evidence retrieved is PK SIMULATION and exposure-margin comparisons, not observed breakthrough-attack outcomes under combined dosing.

BIOLOGY / EXPOSURE CHAIN
HAE type1/2: deficient or dysfunctional C1 inhibitor permits excessive contact-system/kallikrein activity and bradykinin generation. Bradykinin activates B2 receptors on vascular endothelium, increasing permeability and producing swelling. Deucrictibant selectively blocks that downstream receptor. Icatibant validates B2 blockade for acute HAE; it does not prove daily chronic blockade, oral absorption during attacks, competitive superiority, or activity across every genetically/clinically heterogeneous normal-C1 disorder. CHAPTER1/3 and RAPIDe1/3 supply direct spontaneous-attack clinical evidence beyond a healthy-volunteer challenge model.
Immediate release (old PHVS416; active molecule PHA121/PHA-022121) targets fast absorption for attacks. XR (old PHVS719) aims to maintain daily protective exposure. Continuous prophylaxis requires adequate trough exposure and years of tolerability; acute therapy prioritizes speed and sufficient duration without frequent redosing. Mean healthy-volunteer PK cannot prove reliable trough in every patient, adherence forgiveness, or all acute severe gastrointestinal/airway situations.
C020/C021/C017 poster (27Feb2026): C02015 enrolled/14 evaluable crossover healthy volunteers XR40mg vs IR40mg; one stopped because blood sampling failed. Mean XR24h concentration exceeded proposed EC85=13.8ng/mL ~fourfold;12/14 aboveEC85,all aboveEC50. IR crossedEC85 in15–30min. XR fed/fasted mean24h29.3±19.6 vs26.8±17.4ng/mL;tmax5 vs7h;t1/2~6h. Repeated XR40mg(N14) steady state2–3days;trough29–41ng/mL. Threshold derives from pharmacological modeling/icatibant read-through; it is not a guaranteed clinical success threshold. New XR findings are preferable to extrapolating food effects of older oral solution.
CYP3A4 involvement is documented in historical filing and current trials exclude interacting drugs: RAPIDe3 strong inhibitors/inducers; CHAPTER3 moderate/strong inhibitors and strong inducers. Magnitude of final-label interactions, renal/hepatic adjustments, metabolite contribution and real-world missed-dose effects remain unverified.
Safety history: FDA US holds Aug2022 followed repeat-dose rat findings described by company as unrelated to B2 antagonism. On-demand hold lifted Jun2023 after planned interim26-week rodent analysis; prophylaxis hold lifted Jan2024 after full dataset. Lift permits development, not blanket proof of chronic safety. No basis to invent organ pathology absent regulator reports. Sept4 2026 CV poster pooled Phase1/2/3 data cutoff10Jun2025(~570 exposed) and concentration-QTc analysis; no identified CV signal, but predates CHAPTER3 outcome and cannot rule out rare chronic risk.
IR+XR poster Sept4: population-PK simulates XR40 plus IR20 or twoIR20 four hours apart, peaks deliberately aligned. Minimum animal NOAEL margins: Cmax8.8x/7.4x; AUC2.8x/2.0x for1/2IR. Estimated exposure below previous oral-solution50mgBID10-day human exposure. Supports feasibility, not clinical combined-use effectiveness or formal approval. Do not give this independent revenue synergy without label/payer/clinical evidence.
