PHVS commercial research | as of 6 October 2026
Prepared as a bounded primary-source research workstream. No private claims access or interviews. Fact, derived calculation, inference, and unknown are separated below. Source summaries intentionally compact; most web sources have 200-word attribution ceilings.

DECISION-RELEVANT CONCLUSIONS

1. Oral convenience is commercially validated but PHVS is not entering an oral-white-space market. Ekterly is already approved for acute HAE; Orladeyo is already approved for prevention. By PHVS launch, fewer injections may also mean monthly/every-eight-week prophylaxis or possibly one-time gene editing. The actual debate is incremental control/reliability, tolerability, paid access and persistence conditional on approval.

2. There is direct evidence that patient demand and revenue decouple. Orladeyo Q4 2022 had the expected number of patients on therapy but revenue lagged company expectations because paid shipments and free-to-paid conversion were weaker than expected [C6]. This is a particularly transferable disappointing-launch-period analogue: same disease, oral chronic treatment, specialist and specialty pharmacy channel. It is not evidence the whole launch failed. Later improvement supports commercial execution upside, not just downside.

3. Do not mistake patient start forms, prescriptions, paid starts, attack consumption, and refill sales. KalVista's 1,702 US start forms/724 prescribers through February 2026 and majority Q4 2025 revenue from refills validate interest and recurring shipments [C4]. Neither is a disclosed paid conversion rate, retention curve, nor number of attacks treated. The January preliminary update specifically cautioned some refills could reflect holiday pull-forward [C5]. Chiesi acquired KalVista June 11, 2026 [C3], potentially strengthening competition and weakening future public launch-data granularity.

4. Diagnosis growth is plausible, not measured well enough for a high base-case rate. A 2025 claims study requires an HAE-drug claim in its ascertainment algorithm: it estimates treated/captured diagnosis, cannot find untreated undiagnosed patients, and cannot establish that new drugs CAUSED new biological cases or diagnoses [C9]. Its point estimates actually declined over 2018–20. There is no basis to equate a larger claimed TAM with net additions to reimbursed demand.

5. Strong prophylaxis mechanically reduces acute consumption, even though patients still need rescue inventory. The biologic/behavioral tradeoff is real: convenient acute therapy can increase fraction of attacks treated; better prophylaxis lowers attack count; earlier effective rescue could lower repeat-dose demand. These parameters must move together, not all in PHVS's favor independently.

COMPETITIVE MAP (US; label facts unless noted)

On demand
- Ekterly/sebetralstat: oral plasma-kallikrein inhibitor; approved July 3, 2025 for age >=12. At earliest recognition: 600 mg (two tablets), possible repeat >=3h, maximum 1,200 mg/24h. CYP3A4 restrictions/dose adjustments. KONFIDENT crossover FDA efficacy population 110; background prevention in 24/110; severe throat attacks excluded. FDA symptom-relief endpoint was at least a little better at two consecutive timepoints within 12h. This is an established oral competitor, with a different target from PHVS. Headache most common adverse reaction [C1]. Chiesi-owned from June 2026 [C3].
- Icatibant/Firazyr/generics: injected bradykinin B2 antagonist, self-administered; adult US label. Strongest target-validation analogue for PHVS, but injection, exposure, and formulation differ. Generics give payers a cheaper acute alternative; exact realized net prices not observed [C2].
- Berinert: plasma-derived C1-INH replacement, IV self-administration, adult/pediatric acute treatment. Ruconest: recombinant C1-INH, IV self-administration, adult/adolescent. Experience and pediatric reach compete with oral convenience; route/cold-chain/administration tradeoffs matter [C2].
- Kalbitor/ecallantide: kallikrein inhibitor, age >=12, three subcutaneous injections per 30 mg dose; boxed anaphylaxis warning requires professional administration. That changes time-to-treatment and access, not just molecular efficacy [C2,C18].

Prophylaxis
- Orladeyo/berotralstat: oral kallikrein inhibitor, adult/adolescent capsule 150 mg daily with food; pediatric weight-based pellets age 2–<12 approved December 2025 and began shipping August 2026. GI tolerability and interaction/exposure issues are relevant label dimensions. Daily oral administration already has demonstrated paid demand [C7,C16].
- Takhzyro/lanadelumab: injected anti-kallikrein antibody, age >=2; adult/adolescent 300 mg every two weeks, potentially every four weeks after good control for >6 months. Strong disease control can reduce willingness to risk switching; injection interval competes with daily-pill burden [C14].
- Haegarda: subcutaneous C1-INH prevention, age >=6; Cinryze: IV C1-INH prevention, age >=6. Established replacement biology and home-use experience; recurring administration is the burden [C2].
- Andembry/garadacimab: anti-activated-factor-XII antibody; age >=12, June 2025 approval; 400 mg loading then 200 mg subcutaneous monthly. Already competes on infrequent self-administration [C15].
- Dawnzera/donidalorsen: antisense lowers prekallikrein production, age >=12, August 21, 2025 approval; 80 mg subcutaneous every four weeks, every eight weeks may be considered. OASIS-HAE n45 q4w/n23 q8w/n22 placebo; 24-week adjusted attacks/4 weeks 0.44/1.02/2.26; attack-free weeks4–24 53%/35%/9%. Do not rank these percentages against PHVS CHAPTER endpoints/window. Hypersensitivity including anaphylaxis is a labeled risk [C17].

Credible upcoming pressure
- Lonvoguran ziclumeran/lonvo-z (formerly NTLA-2002): one-time IV CRISPR inactivation of KLKB1. HAELO is completed, not an upcoming efficacy readout: 52 active/28 placebo; weeks5–28 mean monthly attacks 0.26 vs2.10; 62% vs11% attack- AND therapy-free. On-demand-treated attack rate 0.19 vs1.79 gives direct evidence of acute consumption pressure [C19]. BLA accepted September 8, 2026 with priority review; PDUFA March 10, 2027 [C20]. Approval, long-horizon safety/durability, irreversible-treatment acceptance, center capacity and access remain uncertain. Do not assume all patients immediately switch or that a finite controlled period proves cure.
- Navenibart (formerly STAR-0215): long-acting anti-kallikrein antibody now BioCryst after Astria acquisition; ALPHA-ORBIT pivotal enrollment completed June 2026, q3mo/q6mo regimens, topline Q3 2027 expected [C7]. Early efficacy and convenience are not approval. Its value to the comparison is competitive duration, not speculative market-share precision.

No valid numerical cross-trial ranking follows from this map. Differences in entry attack frequency, prophylaxis washout, residual attack burden, baseline adjustment, treatment period, endpoint definition and handling of rescue can dominate apparent differences. Same target or route does not fix those differences. No head-to-head data or access-adjusted share model retrieved.

PATIENT FLOW AND WHAT THE RECORDS OBSERVE

Disease stock (genetic susceptibility, not always symptomatic) -> symptomatic stock -> recognized/referral -> biochemical/genetic workup -> documented subtype -> specialist encounter -> clinically eligible/offered -> patient accepts -> submitted authorization -> approval/appeal -> first paid dispense -> refill/persistence. Clinical diagnosis, prescriber plan, pharmacy dispense and insurer-paid claim are different events.

Relevant observers: testing labs see workups/positive results, specialist centers see confirmed cases and reasons for switches, HAEA can observe referrals/cascade screening, hub sees enrollments and free-drug bridges, payer sees submitted/approved/denied authorizations and claims, specialty pharmacy sees shipments/refills, manufacturers see channel sales. None alone measures the entire funnel. Claims often omit free drug and unsubmitted prescriptions; registries oversample engaged patients. A new HAE claim may be a switch, data-capture change, or previously diagnosed person returning to care rather than a newly diagnosed person.

Diagnosis evidence: IOS cohort shows shorter diagnosis delays for later birth cohorts and shorter median delay with family history (2.0 vs5.6y), but includes people born pre1990 and diagnosed by25; not an estimate of today's US annual flow [C10]. A US physician follow-up survey had only 177/5,382 responses (3%): home care increased and cost/insurance was the top reported non-efficacy choice factor at43.7%; informative for mechanism, not representative demand [C11]. Registry/claims counts cannot quantify remaining undiagnosed stock reliably. Normal-C1-INH disease has harder diagnostic confirmation and biologic heterogeneity; do not enlarge the reachable market using vague recurrent swelling alone.

The diagnosis-expansion hypothesis can be tested with deduplicated first confirmed diagnosis date, prior care and family-screening status, subtype, pre-diagnosis attacks, subsequent paid initiation and 12-month spend. A time trend in product sales cannot substitute. Separate family-screening discovery of existing prevalent cases from births/new incident manifestations, broader definitions, insurance changes, treatment intensity and price.

PRESCRIBING AND ACCESS

The UHC commercial Orladeyo policy effective April 1, 2026 is unusually decision-relevant [C12]: typically excluded; adults require failures of Andembry, Haegarda AND Takhzyro; allergist/immunologist prescription, severity/frequency justification, confirmed diagnostic pathway, no concurrent preventive product, 12-month authorization. Some nC1-INH pathways are allowed. This is observed for specified plans, not a national requirement or prediction of PHVS policy. State mandates/individual benefits and prior claims exceptions may apply. Oklahoma's May 1, 2026 product-based PA added tiers while grandfathering current users [C13]. Together these show how incumbency can protect existing treatment and new-start access can differ from continuation access.

Orladeyo 2024 primary release gives paid-patient rate74.4% and >60% of paid patients remaining at least a year [C8]. These are DIFFERENT denominators; multiplying enrollment by60% without first defining paid initiation and cohort age double-counts or miscounts attrition. 2026 earnings transcript says paid rate84%, but that number was not verified in a primary transcript/filing here; do not make it a hard model input. Do not infer 3,500-ever-treated/1,600-current implies54% discontinuation at1year: cohorts have different follow-up and possibly geographic/paid definitions.

An oral-option survey establishes burden/preferences, not coverage or switch shares. Relevant switch segments: inadequately controlled patients; injection-burden patients; stable well-controlled people who fear destabilization; low-frequency acute-only patients; children outside the current PHVS trial/likely initial label. Quantify each only when denominator and current-treatment distribution exist. Primary interview questions should include 'What would make you NOT switch?', 'Which failures satisfy this policy?', 'Does a refill replenish stock or replace consumed doses?', 'What proportion of bridge starts never becomes paid?', 'What is the time from decision to first paid fill?' No interviews performed.

ACUTE/PREVENTIVE INTERACTION AND QUANTITATIVE FRAMEWORK

Observed behavioral opening: 2025 KalVista-funded US survey,107/155 completers,80% female, intentionally balanced acute-only vs preventive+acute. 57% did not treat every attack; this DOES NOT mean57% of attacks untreated. Only14% always treated immediately; mean delay2.4h. Self-report/selection/confounding prevent a causal estimate of the effect of oral drugs [C21]. This supports a treatment-propensity sensitivity, not a direct market-share conversion.

Define segments g by prophylaxis state, subtype, age and access. For period t: treated acute attacks = sum_g N[g,t] * residual attacks/patient-period[g,t] * probability treated[g,t]. PHVS consumed doses = sum_g treated attacks[g,t] * PHVS acute share[g,t] * doses per PHVS-treated attack[g,t]. Paid dispenses = consumed doses + change in patient-held inventory + expiry/loss replacement, adjusted for free product and channel-inventory change separately. Sales recognized at channel shipment need not equal patient dispensing in the same quarter. Unit choices (tablet vs treatment dose vs pack) must match price. Ekterly dose is TWO tablets.

Preventive paid patient-months = sum_start-cohorts starts[c] * survival on therapy[c,t] * covered/paid fraction[c,t] * exposure months in period. Adherence affects attacks/trough protection; if measured paid units already capture missed fills, do not haircut revenue a second time. Discontinuation shifts patients into another prevention state or acute-only state, changing acute demand. Shared-franchise familiarity might reduce cost per start but does not imply shared automatic authorization; same-molecule concurrent use requires dose/safety/label evidence.

Hypothetical interaction example:1,000 patients,600 on prevention at0.5 attack/mo and400 acute-only at2/mo =1,100 attacks/mo. At60% treated and1.1 doses/attack:726 doses/mo. If prevention becomes800 at0.25/mo and200 remain acute-only at2/mo:600 attacks/mo. Even raising treated fraction to90% leaves594 doses/mo at1.1:18.2% fewer. At unchanged50% PHVS acute share the product doses decline363->297, while prevention expands. This illustrates dependency, not a forecast.

Hypothetical diagnosis/access example:100 newly identified patients *80% specialist engagement *75% clinical fit *70% acceptance *80% paid conversion =33.6 paid starts. If cohorts yield0.75 expected paid patient-years in first year,25.2 paid patient-years. Increasing diagnosis20% gives30.24, but lowering conversion to60% yields22.68 despite more diagnoses. Keep time-to-start and persistence in the same period. No assumed values are estimates for PHVS.

RANKED HYPOTHESES FOR INTEGRATION

A. [Supported mechanism; PHVS size unknown] Paid access/persistence, not total interest, is the binding launch constraint. Prior view evidence: company oral-convenience messaging plus start-form disclosures. Counterevidence: UHC steps and Orladeyo paid-conversion shortfall. Test: patient-level hub funnel with adjudicated claims and 6/12-month survival; variable: paid starts and paid patient-months. Disconfirm if rapid high conversion under broad non-step coverage, without large discounts. Can be economically material because recurring revenue multiplies both terms.
B. [Plausible, mixed] Oral rescue expands treatment intensity more than unique patient population. Behavioral survey and recurring Ekterly revenue support opening; competing explanation: refills are inventory/pull-forward and already treated attacks changing brand. Test: linked attack diary and dispense data versus prior rescue regimen. Variable: treated fraction, product share, repeat doses and stock. Unknowable from sales totals alone.
C. [Supported structural dependency] Better prevention limits acute units despite wide rescue enrollment. HAELO directly shows fewer attacks requiring rescue. Test: longitudinal rescue consumption by current prophylaxis. Variable: residual attack rate conditional on preventive state. Counterweight: greater treatment propensity/stock expiry. Do not make both prevention and acute sales independently maximal.
D. [Plausible bearish] PHVS is squeezed between an entrenched oral rescue launch and infrequent prevention/one-time treatment. Actual sequencing verified; market-share effect unknown. Test: reasons for competing starts/switches, payer preferences, lonvo-z uptake/safety after decision. Variables: share curves, launch cost, gross-to-net, time to peak. Disconfirm if incremental PHVS control reliably outweighs switching risk and access costs.
E. [Weak as base-case growth] Diagnosis growth drives a large premium beyond share capture. Long delays support possible stock; claims ascertainment and weak causal evidence undermine measured rate. Test: independent longitudinal confirmed new-diagnosis funnel, not drug-claim inclusion. Variables: undiagnosed stock and bounded diagnosis hazard; newly identified attack burden may be lower, but that is a hypothesis to measure. Put only in sensitivities until tested.
F. [Plausible bullish] A smaller reachable market can still support strong value with exceptional persistence. Orladeyo establishes both durable demand and substantial attrition; PHVS reliability/tolerability could alter survival. Test: randomized efficacy plus post-launch discontinuation reasons and paid survival, no inference from OLE completers alone. Variable: cohort lifetime paid months; no numerical PHVS uplift yet.
