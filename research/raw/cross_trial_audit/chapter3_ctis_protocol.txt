Study Objectives:  
Primary 
• To evaluate the efficacy of deucrictibant 40 mg extended-release (XR) tablet compared with 
placebo for prophylaxis against angioedema attacks  
Secondary 
• To characterize the efficacy of deucrictibant 40 mg XR tablet in prophylactic treatment of 
hereditary angioedema (HAE)  
• To evaluate the safety and tolerability of deucrictibant 40 mg XR tablet in prophylactic 
treatment of HAE  
• To evaluate the pharmacokinetics (PK) of deucrictibant 40 mg XR tablet in prophylactic 
treatment of HAE  
• To evaluate the impact on health-related quality of life (HRQoL) of deucrictibant 40 mg XR 
tablet in prophylactic treatment of HAE 
Study Endpoints: 
Primary Endpoint: Efficacy 
• Time-normalized (per 4 weeks) number of Investigator-confirmed HAE attacks during the 
24-week Treatment Period (Day 1 through Day 168)  
Secondary Endpoints: Efficacy 
• Time-normalized number of Investigator-confirmed HAE attacks treated with on-demand 
medication during the 24-week Treatment Period 
• Time-normalized number of Investigator-confirmed moderate or severe HAE attacks during the 
24-week Treatment Period  
• Time-normalized number of Investigator-confirmed severe HAE attacks during the 24-week 
Treatment Period

Study Design: 
This is a Phase 3, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study to evaluate the 
efficacy and safety of orally administered deucrictibant XR tablet once daily (QD) for prophylaxis to 
prevent attacks of HAE in participants aged ≥12 years. 
Following signing of the informed consent form (ICF), participants will undergo screening 
assessments. The Screening Period will last up to 10 weeks (70 days) to confirm underlying disease 
status and assess eligibility for study participation. The eligibility of participants receiving HAE 
medications for long-term prophylaxis (LTP) should be compliant with eligibility criteria.  
The baseline attack rate will be based on results from the Run-in Period, which occurs during the 
Screening Period and will last up to 8 weeks (56 days). The Run-in Period will begin after electronic 
diary (eDiary) activation during the Screening Visit and will end after 14, 28, 42, or 56 days in case the 
baseline attack criterion has been met. The signing of the ICF, Screening Visit, and start of Run-in 
Period should all occur on the same day. During the Run-in Period, the Investigator and/or designee 
will consult with the participant (or participant’s parent/legally designated representative/guardian) 
weekly to discuss occurrences of HAE attacks. The baseline attack rate requirement for eligibility 
should not be disclosed to study participants.  
Participants must experience  Investigator -confirmed attacks during the up to 8-week Run-in 
Period as detailed in the eligibility criteria. Randomization should take place as soon as possible after 
the baseline attack criterion and all other eligibility criteria have been met. 
Participants cannot be randomized during an HAE attack period. An HAE attack must be resolved for 
at least 24 hours before randomization.

Eligible participants will be randomized in a 2:1 ratio to receive deucrictibant 40 mg XR tablet or 
matching placebo QD for 24 weeks. Stratification factors of the randomization will be age group 
(adolescents [≥12 to <18 years] and adults [≥18 years]) and baseline HAE attack rate (  attacks 
per 4 weeks versus  attacks per 4 weeks).  
Throughout the study, an eDiary will be completed by participants to record HAE attacks, standard of 
care on-demand treatments, daily administration of study drug, and to complete electronic patient-
reported outcomes (ePROs).  
Participants must have access to standard of care on-demand treatments and be able to treat any HAE 
attack that occurs. 
Participants who discontinue study drug before Week 24 of the Treatment Period will complete the 
End of Treatment (EOT) Visit and will be encouraged to remain in the study per the study protocol 
defined visit schedule through the End of Study (EOS) Visit.  
Participants who withdraw early from the study will be encouraged to complete the EOT and EOS 
Visits after their last administration of study drug. The assessments for both visits may be done at the 
same time. For participants who have already discontinued study drug and completed the EOT Visit at 
an earlier time, only the EOS Visit needs to be completed when they withdraw from the study. 
Participants who complete the 24-week Treatment Period will enter a 4-week Follow-up Period or may 
roll over into the open-label study PHA022121-C307.

Adolescent and adult participants (male or female, ≥12 years of age) with HAE will be randomized.

The study will be conducted at approximately 80 study sites in approximately 30 countries. 
Number of Participants (Planned):  
Approximately 81 participants with HAE, including at least 8 adolescents, are planned to be 
randomized in the study.

Study Drug, Dosage, and Mode of Administration:  
Deucrictibant has been adopted as the international nonproprietary name (INN) for the active 
ingredient, previously referred to as PHA121 or PHA-022121. Deucrictibant 40 mg XR is a tablet 
formulation.  
The study drug consists of deucrictibant 40 mg XR tablet and matching placebo tablet for oral use. The 
QD administration of study drug throughout the Treatment Period is recommended to occur at the 
same time daily without a meal or with a light meal, with 24 ± 2 hours between each dose when study 
drug is not administered during a study visit. The tablets are to be swallowed with water.  
Duration of Treatment:  
24 weeks 
Management of HAE Attacks:  
Participants must have access to standard of care on-demand treatments and be able to treat any HAE 
attack that occurs. Participants should continue administering study drug and following study 
procedures without alteration to the protocol schedule, even if they receive standard of care on-demand 
treatment for HAE attacks. All HAE medications associated with the management of attacks will be 
reported.  
Criteria for Evaluation: 
Efficacy 
Efficacy will be assessed based upon the occurrences of HAE attacks, which will be confirmed by 
Investigators. During the Screening Visit, site personnel will discuss with participants the requirements 
for reporting HAE attacks, including the information that is to be captured for each HAE attack. 
Participants will receive training on how to use the eDiary at the Screening Visit and throughout the 
study, as needed. Information on HAE attacks will be collected in an eDiary by the participant. Each 
reported HAE attack will be assessed by the Investigator and/or designee and confirmed by the 
Investigator within 72 hours from onset of symptoms of an attack. 
The criteria provided in this section should be used by the Investigator to assess if an event reported by 
a participant can be confirmed as an HAE attack. 
To be confirmed as an HAE attack, the event must have signs or symptoms consistent with an attack in 
at least one of the following locations: 
• Peripheral angioedema: cutaneous swelling involving an extremity, face, neck, torso and/or 
genitourinary region 
• Abdominal angioedema: abdominal pain, with or without abdominal distension, nausea, vomiting, 
or diarrhea 
• Upper airway angioedema: sensation of lump in the throat, difficulty swallowing, throat tightening, 
difficulty speaking, hoarseness, stridor, dyspnea, or upper airways swelling on the tongue, palate, 
uvula, or larynx 
Despite the presence of these symptoms, the Investigator may still determine that the event does not 
represent an HAE attack if there are clinical features that strongly refute such a diagnosis (eg, 
associated with wheals or longer in duration than the typical time course of an attack or alternate 
etiology such as recent trauma resulting in swelling or pain). 
To be counted as a unique HAE attack distinct from a previous attack, the new symptoms must occur 
at least 24 hours after complete resolution of symptoms of a prior attack (ie, there must be a 24-hour 
symptom-free period to be considered a new attack). 
Participant-reported occurrences of prodromal symptoms alone or pre-emptive use of acute treatments 
without swelling symptoms are not considered to meet the criteria of an HAE attack.

Statistical Methods: 
A Statistical Analysis Plan (SAP) will be finalized prior to database lock and will include a more 
technical and detailed description of all planned statistical analyses of all study endpoints. 
A Poisson distribution of Investigator-confirmed HAE attacks was assumed for the analysis of primary 
endpoint ‘Time-normalized (per 4 weeks) number of Investigator-confirmed HAE attacks during the 
24-week Treatment period’. In the Phase 2 study PHA022121-C301,  
in the 
placebo group. With a conservative assumption that the time-normalized number of 
Investigator-confirmed HAE attacks  in the placebo group during the 24- week Treatment 
Period, a sample size of 81 randomized participants (2:1 randomization; 54 on deucrictibant 40 mg XR 
tablet and 27 on placebo) will provide a power of at least 95% to detect a 50% reduction in 
time-normalized number of Investigator-confirmed HAE attacks for deucrictibant 40 mg XR 
comparing to placebo, based on a two-sided test at a significance level of 5%. 
The primary endpoint is the time-normalized (per 4 weeks) number of Investigator-confirmed HAE 
attacks during the 24-week Treatment Period (Day 1 through Day 168). 
The primary estimand of interest is defined by the following attributes: 
• Treatment: Deucrictibant 40 mg XR tablet versus placebo, orally administered QD 
• Population: The target patient population is defined through the study inclusion/ exclusion criteria. 
• Endpoint: Time normalized (per 4 weeks) number of Investigator-confirmed HAE attacks during 
the 24-week Treatment Period (Day 1 through Day 168). Unless otherwise specified, this is also 
referred to as the 4-week HAE attack rate, or HAE attack rate, which is calculated for each 
participant as the number of Investigator-confirmed HAE attacks occurring during the 24-week 
Treatment Period divided by the number of days the participant contributed to the 24-week 
Treatment Period, multiplied by 28 days. 
• Intercurrent events (ICEs):  
− Early study drug discontinuation for any reason unless accompanied by a switch to any 
alternative LTP HAE therapy during the 24-week Treatment Period 
− Early study drug discontinuation for any reason accompanied by a switch to any alternative 
LTP HAE therapy during the 24-week Treatment Period 
− Use of prohibited medication (including prohibited short/long-term HAE prophylaxis) during 
the 24-week Treatment Period  
− Non-compliance to study drug during the 24-week Treatment Period 
• Population-level summary: Ratio of the 4-week HAE attack rates between deucrictibant 40 mg XR 
tablet and placebo 
Composite variable strategy will be used to address the ICE ‘Early study drug discontinuation for any 
reason accompanied by a switch to any alternative LTP HAE therapy during the 24-week Treatment 
Period’. For the remaining ICEs, a treatment policy strategy will be applied.  
The analysis of the primary efficacy endpoint will be performed based on the Intent-to-Treat (ITT) 
Analysis Set and will compare deucrictibant 40 mg XR tablet with placebo using a generalized linear 
model (GLM) assuming a Poisson distribution with a log link function and Pearson chi-square scaling 
of standard errors to account for potential (over)-dispersion. The model will include fixed effects for 
treatment group, the baseline attack rate and age group (adolescent and adult), and an offset term of the 
logarithm of participant’s follow-up time during the Treatment Period. The estimated least squares 
(LS) rate for deucrictibant 40 mg XR tablet and placebo and the rate ratio relative to placebo will be 
provided with 95% confidence intervals. These estimates will be reported as 4-week attack rates by 
transforming the estimates using the exponential function and scaling by the unit of time. The

4. INTRODUCTION 
4.1. Hereditary Angioedema 
Hereditary angioedema (HAE) is a rare disease that manifests as unpredictable and recurrent 
edema attacks of cutaneous or submucosal tissue most commonly affecting the limbs, intestinal 
tract, face (lips and tongue), genitals, and airways. HAE attacks often lead to discomfort and 
pain, and can include nausea, vomiting and diarrhea when the intestinal tract is affected. HAE 
attacks can become life-threatening in the case of airway obstruction and asphyxiation if the 
attack remains untreated. The number, duration, and severity of attacks vary highly between 
patients and throughout a patient’s lifetime. Trauma, stress, and medical procedures are known 
triggers for attacks. 
Three types of HAE have been characterized. HAE type 1 and type 2 (HAE type 1/2) are defined 
by mutations in the C1 esterase inhibitor (C1INH) gene and result either in reduced levels 
(type 1) or reduced activity (type 2) of C1INH. In both cases, decreased C1INH protease activity 
leads to uncontrolled activity of Factor XII and plasma kallikrein, resulting in an activated 
kallikrein-kinin system, ie, excessive cleavage of high molecular weight kininogen and increased 
plasma levels of bradykinin (BK).  
HAE with normal C1INH (type 3) is characterized by similar clinical manifestations to HAE 
type 1/2 in the presence of normal levels and activity of C1INH and, in a number of cases, is 
associated with characterized mutations in other genes associated with HAE type 3 as listed in 
the Hereditary Angioedema Association (HAEA) and World Allergy Organization 
(WAO)/European Academy of Allergy and Clinical Immunology (EAACI) Guidelines 
(Appendix 2). Recently, some laboratory tests have been developed to identify these patients by 
measuring the BK levels in the blood and stimulated kallikrein activity (Li, 2023, Lara-Marquez, 
2018).  
BK is the principal mediator of the signs and symptoms associated with HAE attacks. Two BK 
receptor types, bradykinin B1 and bradykinin B2, have been identified, cloned, and 
characterized. BK has a high affinity and high agonist potency at the bradykinin B2 receptor, but 
a near 1000-fold lower affinity and potency at the B1 receptor, making it a selective bradykinin 
B2 receptor agonist. 
The bradykinin B2 receptor is expressed on the cell surface of the luminal side of vascular 
endothelial cells. Excessive BK generation, as in HAE, promotes vascular permeability, leading 
to plasma extravasation and cutaneous or submucosal tissue edema and swelling typical of an 
angioedema attack. 
4.2. Current Treatment Options for Hereditary Angioedema 
There are currently 2 treatment approaches to the management of HAE: acute (on-demand) 
treatment of attacks and prevention of attacks with short- or long-term prophylactic therapy. 
Patients and their physicians have several choices for on-demand or prophylactic treatment, but 
because there are differences in the route of administration, side effect profiles, and efficacy 
between medications, patient preference and experience are considered in selecting the most 
appropriate therapy.

Patients must have access to standard of care on-demand HAE medication that can be 
self-administered. Treatment should be taken regardless of the location of the attack, and 
treatment should be initiated early to mitigate progression of the attack. Regular monitoring of 
the patient’s frequency of use of on-demand treatment and treatment outcomes should be 
conducted (Busse, 2021, Maurer, 2022). In addition to on-demand treatment of HAE attacks, a 
patient may also require prophylactic treatment. 
The requirement for long-term prophylactic treatment is not made on rigid criteria, but rather the 
needs of the individual patient (Busse, 2021, Maurer, 2022). The decision to initiate long-term 
prophylaxis is made with consideration for the patient’s quality of life and treatment preferences 
in the context of attack frequency, attack severity, comorbid conditions, and access to treatment 
(Busse, 2021, Maurer, 2022). Because disease severity may change over time, the need to initiate 
or continue long-term prophylactic treatment is periodically reviewed. 
4.2.1. Prophylactic Treatments 
The plasma-derived C1INH concentrates (Cinryze® and Haegarda®/Berinert® 2,000/3,000) are 
administered by intravenous (iv) and subcutaneous (sc) routes, respectively. These products are 
administered twice weekly (every 3 or 4 days). Plasma kallikrein inhibitors include lanadelumab 
(Takhzyro®), a fully human recombinant monoclonal antibody, which is administered sc every 2 
or 4 weeks, and berotralstat (Orladeyo®), which is administered orally, once daily. Attenuated 
androgens (including danazol, stanozolol, oxandrolone, oxymetholone, tibolone, 
methyltestosterone) and antifibrinolytics, such as ɛ-aminocaproic acid and tranexamic acid, are 
used for the prevention of acute attacks and are licensed in some countries. However, current 
treatment guidelines recommend against the use of attenuated androgens and antifibrinolytics as 
first-line therapy due to limited efficacy and/or safety issues with associated contraindications 
(Busse, 2021, Maurer, 2022). 
4.3. Unmet Medical Needs in Managing Bradykinin-Mediated 
Angioedema 
There is a high unmet medical need for efficacious oral medicines that can facilitate timely 
treatment of attacks due to HAE and other various forms of bradykinin-mediated angioedema 
and thereby improve outcomes, compliance, and the overall care and quality of life for patients. 
All but one of the medications approved in the last decade for the management of HAE are 
administered parenterally via iv or sc routes, which are often associated with a delay of drug 
administration, injection pain, local side effects, and a lower quality of life. In a 2017 Food and 
Drug Administration (FDA) Patient-Focused Drug Development meeting, patients expressed that 
the route of administration (iv, sc, or oral) is the most important consideration for a new HAE 
therapeutic, with a preference for oral treatments (FDA, 2018).  
4.4. Deucrictibant 
Deucrictibant is under development as an orally administered BK B2 receptor antagonist with 
potential for both acute therapeutic and prophylactic use in patients with different types of 
BK-mediated angioedema, including HAE type 1/2, HAE with normal C1INH (type 3), and 
acquired angioedema due to C1INH deficiency.

Deucrictibant has been extensively characterized in primary pharmacological studies as a potent, 
orally available B2 receptor antagonist. 
Deucrictibant did not interact in vitro with human cardiac ion channels known to contribute to 
the cardiac action potential, including the human ether-a-go-go-related gene, at up to 
concentrations 211- to 228-fold higher than exposure levels anticipated in this study. Nor did 
single oral doses of deucrictibant induce any relevant changes in heart rate, arterial blood 
pressure, or body temperature, or in any electrocardiogram (ECG) parameters assessed in the 
cynomolgus monkey. In addition, there were no clinically significant treatment-related adverse 
effects of deucrictibant observed in ECG parameters, cardiac weights, or microscopic evaluation 
of cardiac tissue in the repeated dose toxicology studies or 28-day tolerability study.  
Completed Phase 1 clinical studies did not reveal any deucrictibant-related cardiac disorders. 
Furthermore, no cardiac disorders were reported in the Phase 2 HAE studies PHA022121-C201 
or PHA022121-C301. There were no clinically significant changes in ECG parameters observed 
across all single- and multiple-dose clinical studies. A concentration-QTcF analysis indicated 
that deucrictibant does not demonstrate QT prolonging effects at concentrations that cover the 
therapeutic dose range as well as the high clinical exposure scenarios for both on-demand and 
prophylactic treatment.  
In nonclinical toxicology studies, acute adverse central nervous system (CNS)-related clinical 
signs at the time of peak plasma concentration (Cmax) were observed in several animal species on 
Day 1 or 2 after administration of supratherapeutic doses of deucrictibant at systemic exposure 
20- to 90-fold higher than the anticipated unbound plasma Cmax in humans administered 
deucrictibant 40 mg XR tablet. 
In the recently completed 26-week video-electroencephalography (EEG) study in rats, no 
increased risk was attributed to deucrictibant compared to placebo after long-term 
administration. 
As of the clinical safety cutoff date of 10 June 2024, all events except for 1 headache categorized 
under the System Organ Class, "Nervous System Disorder" were mild or moderate in intensity 
and the incidence rate has been comparable between active and placebo groups. One severe 
headache was reported and considered by the Investigator to be unrelated to study drug. 
Deucrictibant had no adverse effects on male or female fertility or embryofetal development 
(EFD) in the humanized bradykinin B2-receptor transgenic rat nor on EFD in rabbits, up to the 
highest dose tested. This supports the inclusion of women of childbearing potential in the 
Phase 3 clinical studies provided appropriate contraception precautions are in place. Functional 
assessment of male fertility has been completed both in the wild-type rat as part of the 6-month 
repeat-dose toxicity study and in a dedicated male fertility study in the humanized bradykinin 
B2-receptor transgenic rat following pre-mating dosing of at least 10 weeks. There are no effects 
on male reproductive parameters up to the highest dose tested. In addition, there were no organ 
weight or histopathological changes of concern in the rat reproductive tract of either sex. In 
conjunction with the EFD results, the clinical studies do not require male participants to use male 
contraception.  
Overall, in completed Phase 1 studies (using either deucrictibant oral solution or soft capsule) 
and in the Phase 2 clinical study of deucrictibant prophylactic treatment to prevent attacks of 
HAE in adults with HAE (PHA022121-C301), deucrictibant has been shown to be generally

well-tolerated. Most treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) reported have been mild to 
moderate in severity. No severe TEAEs or serious adverse events (SAEs) have been considered

laboratory parameters, vital signs, or ECG parameters were observed.  
4.4.1. Benefit-Risk with Deucrictibant 
Given the potential benefit to treat the HAE population, especially with the validated mechanism 
of action, positive pharmacodynamic and clinical effects demonstrated by the BK challenge in 
Study PHA022121-C001, and proof of concept to prevent HAE attacks in Study 
PHA022121-C301, the benefit-risk assessment of deucrictibant is considered acceptable if used 
according to the clinical study protocol, and in accordance with the recommendations and 
guidance provided in the Investigator’s Brochure.  
Based on nonclinical and clinical data to date, the cardiovascular risk of deucrictibant is 
considered low. However, as a precaution, participants with clinically significant cardiovascular 
history will not be eligible to enroll in the study and cardiovascular health will be monitored with 
12-lead ECGs, vital signs, and physical examinations throughout the study. 
While the neurological effects in nonclinical studies are believed to be associated with 
supratherapeutic Cmax systemic concentrations, neurological AEs will be monitored as adverse 
events of special interest (AESIs) in this study as a precautionary measure, and participants with 
epilepsy or other significant neurological diseases will not be eligible to enroll in the study. 
Hereditary angioedema is a disease with the same pathophysiology in adults and pediatric 
patients. There is a lack of evidence demonstrating any significant differences in HAE etiology 
and pathophysiology based on age, and the overall clinical aspects of adult and pediatric HAE 
are quite similar (Farkas, 2017, Wahn, 2020). The comparable exposure-response relationship is 
thus anticipated in view of the development biology of the kallikrein-kinin system. Moreover, 
the adult and pediatric data from icatibant, sharing the same mechanism of action, supports this 
assumption (Farkas, 2017, Wang, 2021, EMA, 2017). 
Overall, the benefit-risk assessment appears to be favorable to support the clinical investigation 
of deucrictibant for prophylactic treatment to prevent HAE attacks in the adult and pediatric 
populations planned for enrollment in this study.

6. INVESTIGATIONAL PLAN 
6.1. Overall Study Design 
This is a Phase 3, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study to evaluate 
the efficacy and safety of orally administered deucrictibant XR tablet once daily (QD) for 
prophylaxis to prevent attacks of HAE in participants aged ≥12 years. 
Following signing of the informed consent form (ICF), participants will undergo screening 
assessments. The Screening Period will last up to 10 weeks (70 days) to confirm underlying 
disease status and assess eligibility for study participation. The eligibility of participants 
receiving HAE medications for long-term prophylaxis (LTP) will be compliant with eligibility 
criteria.  
The baseline attack rate will be based on results from the Run-in Period, which occurs during the 
Screening Period and will last up to 8 weeks (56 days). The Run-in Period will begin after 
electronic diary (eDiary) activation during the Screening Visit and will end after 14, 28, 42, or 56 
days in case the baseline attack criterion has been met. The signing of the ICF, Screening Visit, 
and start of Run-in Period should all occur on the same day. During the Run-in Period, the

6.2. Rationale for Study Design, Control Group, Study Population, and 
Dose 
This study is designed as a double-blind, placebo-controlled, and randomized study. This design 
is ethically justified as participants will have adequate access to standard of care on-demand 
treatment (to be reimbursed or provided by the Sponsor if needed) to treat any HAE attack. 
Therefore, participants are not placed at any unnecessary risk during this clinical study with 
adequate clinical monitoring. The placebo-controlled study design had been successfully used in 
the double-blind placebo-controlled Phase 2 Study PHA022121-C301; there was no increased 
risk reported to participants. In addition, the placebo-controlled study design has been used and 
accepted for evaluation of investigational drugs in all ongoing or completed pivotal clinical 
studies of prophylactic treatment of HAE as of April 2024 (Donidalorsen:Clinicaltrials.gov 
NCT05139810, 2024; Garadacimab: Craig, 2023; Berotralstat: Zuraw, 2021; Lanadelumab: 
Banerji, 2018). After completion of the 24-week Treatment Period, participants will be eligible 
to roll-over into an open-label Study PHA022121-C307, to receive active deucrictibant 
prophylactic treatment for long-term safety and efficacy assessment. 
In addition to adult participants enrolled in Phase 2 Study PHA022121‑C301, adolescent 
participants (≥12 to <18 years of age) with confirmed diagnosis were also eligible to enroll. The 
inclusion of adolescents is justified by the lack of any significant safety or tolerability issues 
identified in the completed nonclinical and clinical studies with deucrictibant, robust efficacy 
findings of significant reduction of HAE attack rate demonstrated in the Phase 2 prophylactic 
PHA022121-C301 study, and PK analysis suggesting that deucrictibant exposure in participants 
with HAE is comparable to what has been observed in healthy participants. Previous population 
PK has shown that age and weight are not significant covariates of deucrictibant PK. In addition, 
there have been no known differences in disease presentation and pathophysiology reported 
between adults and adolescents with HAE. Therefore, it is projected that deucrictibant exposure 
level in adolescents will be within the same range as for adult participants. The inclusion of 
adolescents in PHA022121-C305 study can be well justified by the favorable benefit to risk 
assessment.

In this study, deucrictibant 40 mg XR tablets QD will be evaluated. In the Phase 1 Study 
PHA022121-C015, the median pre-dose deucrictibant plasma levels at steady state (Day 5) of 
deucrictibant 40 mg XR tablets QD was 43.4 ng/mL, which is approximately twice the steady 
state pre-dose levels obtained with deucrictibant 20 mg soft capsules BID in Study 
PHA022121-C301; meanwhile, the median maximal deucrictibant plasma level at steady state 
(Day 5) of deucrictibant 40 mg XR tablets QD was 106 ng/mL, which is approximately 68% of 
the steady state maximal level obtained with deucrictibant 20 mg soft capsules BID in Study 
PHA022121-C301. Therefore, compared to the deucrictibant 20 mg soft capsules BID, 
deucrictibant 40 mg XR tablets QD can be reasonably expected to provide comparable or better 
benefit-risk profiles. In addition, once daily administration is also more convenient for patients 
and can lead to better treatment adherence.

11.1. Angioedema Quality of Life Questionnaire 
The Angioedema Quality of Life (AE-QoL) questionnaire is a symptom-specific tool developed 
and validated to assess health-related quality of life impairment in patients with recurrent 
angioedema (Weller, 2012). The AE-QoL consists of 17 questions across 4 domains/dimensions 
(functioning, fatigue/mood, fear/shame, nutrition). Each of the 17 items has a 5-point Likert-type 
response scale ranging from 1 (Never) to 5 (Very Often). The AE-QoL is scored to produce a 
score for each domain and a total score ranging from 0 to 100, with higher scores indicating 
greater impairment.

11.2. Patient Global Assessment of Change 
One anchor question will be completed by participants, the Patient Global Assessment of Change 
(PGA-change). The PGA-change item covers the overall change in how the patient’s QoL has 
been impacted by their HAE since starting to take the study drug with a 5-point Likert response 
scale (much better, a little better, no change, a little worse, or much worse). It will be used to 
support the interpretation of other patient reported outcomes. 
11.3. Angioedema Control Test 
The Angioedema Control Test (AECT) was developed and validated to retrospectively quantify 
disease control and to aid treatment decisions in patients with recurrent angioedema (Weller, 
2020a, Weller, 2020b). The AECT has 4 questions with each question having 5 answer options 
(each scored 0 to 4 points), addressing the frequency of angioedema, angioedema related QoL 
impairment, unpredictability of angioedema attacks, and angioedema control by the current 
treatment approach. The AECT ranges from 0 to 16 points, with total scores of 0 to 9 points 
indicating poorly controlled disease and 10 to16 points indicating well controlled disease. The 
AECT version with a 4-week recall period (ie, AECT-4wk) will be used in this study. 
11.4. Treatment Satisfaction Questionnaire for Medication  
Developed in 2007, the Treatment Satisfaction Questionnaire for Medication 9-items (TSQM-9) 
is a streamlined version of the longer TSQM 1.4 and evaluates patient treatment satisfaction with

12.9. Adverse Events and Serious Adverse Events 
12.9.1. Definition of Adverse Events 
12.9.1.1. Adverse Event  
An AE is defined as any untoward medical occurrence in a patient or clinical investigation 
participant administered a pharmaceutical product and which does not necessarily have to have a 
causal relationship with this treatment. An AE can therefore be any unfavorable and unintended 
sign, symptom, or disease temporally associated with the use of a study drug, whether or not 
considered related to the study drug. 
Baseline medical history/conditions, abnormal physical examination findings, or 
diagnostic/laboratory values present at the Screening Visit are not considered to be AEs and 
should be recorded in the eCRF as baseline medical history. Events with a start date after the 
signing of the ICF are considered AEs, and any worsening in severity, or an increase in 
frequency, or change of concomitant medication for management of a pre-existing baseline 
medical condition is considered to be an AE. For the purpose of this protocol, HAE attacks and 
their associated symptoms will not be defined as AEs, unless they meet the criteria for an SAE. 
Prescheduled hospitalizations for non-emergency procedures will not be considered SAEs. 
12.9.1.2. Serious Adverse Event  
An SAE or reaction is any untoward medical occurrence that at any dose: 
• Results in death 
• Is life-threatening (at the time of the event)  
NOTE: The term “life-threatening” in the definition of “serious” refers to an event in 
which the participant was at risk of death at the time of the event; it does not refer to 
an event which hypothetically might have caused death if it were more severe.

• Requires inpatient hospitalization or prolongation of an existing hospitalization (note: 
a pre-existing condition that requires planned hospitalization is not considered as

• Results in persistent or significant disability/incapacity 
• Is a congenital anomaly/birth defect

12.9.2. Relationship to Study Drug 
All AEs/SAEs (also referred to as “events”) will be assessed to determine their relationship to 
the study drug (causality). The Investigator must assess the causality using the binary choices of 
“Related” or “Not Related,” and by considering the rationales listed below. Events should be

drug. These rationales allow the Investigator to more clearly support their own assessments. All 
AEs and SAEs should be reported, regardless of causality, including events that are thought to be

• Event attributed to concomitant medication or disease 
• Event not reasonably temporally associated with study drug administration 
• Negative dechallenge and/or negative rechallenge 
Examples of rationales for a Related assessment may include: 
• Temporal relationship of event to study drug exposure 
• Event is known to be associated with the study drug or drug class  
• Event improved on discontinuation of study drug 
• Event reoccurred on rechallenge with study drug 
• Event cannot be explained by disease or other drugs 
• Biological plausibility

12.11. Reporting of Study Treatment Errors, Including Misuse/Abuse 
Medication errors are unintentional errors in the prescribing, dispensing, administration, or 
monitoring of a medicine or study drug while under the control of a healthcare professional, 
participant, or consumer. The administration or consumption of an unassigned treatment and 
administration of an expired product are always reportable as medication errors; cases of patients 
missing doses of investigational product are not considered reportable as medication errors. 
In a clinical study, misuse refers to situations where the study drug is intentionally and 
inappropriately used not in accordance with the protocol. 
In a clinical study, abuse corresponds to the persistent or sporadic, intentional excessive use of a 
study drug, which is accompanied by harmful physical or psychological effects. 
In this clinical study, overdose refers to situations where the participant ingests more than 
1 tablet of study drug within 18 (24 minus 6) hours of the previous dose.  
Special situations, including any unintended study treatment/medication error, misuse, abuse, or 
overdose of study drug described above must be reported on the Special Situations Report Form

For the primary endpoint, the following null hypothesis and alternative will be tested for 
deucrictibant 40 mg XR tablet against placebo:  
• H0: λdeucrictibant 40 mg XR / λplacebo = 1 
• H1: λdeucrictibant 40 mg XR / λplacebo ≠ 1 
λ deucrictibant 40 mg XR refers to the time normalized (per 4 weeks) number of Investigator-confirmed 
HAE attacks during the 24-week Treatment Period in deucrictibant 40 mg XR tablet treatment 
group. 
λ placebo refers to the time normalized (per 4 weeks) number of Investigator-confirmed HAE 
attacks during the 24-week Treatment Period in placebo treatment group. 
The null hypothesis H0 is that the Investigator-confirmed HAE attacks rate ratio = 1, which 
means that there is no difference between treatment groups.  
The alternative H1 is that the Investigator-confirmed HAE attacks rate ratio ≠ 1, which means 
that there is difference between treatment groups. 
The analysis of the primary endpoint will be performed based on the ITT Analysis Set and will 
compare deucrictibant 40 mg XR tablet with placebo using a generalized linear model (GLM) 
assuming a Poisson distribution with a log link function and Pearson chi-square scaling of 
standard errors to account for potential (over)-dispersion. The model will include fixed effects 
for treatment group, the baseline attack rate and age group (adolescent and adult), and an offset 
term of the logarithm of participant’s follow-up time during the Treatment Period.  
The estimated least squares (LS) rate for deucrictibant 40 mg XR tablet and placebo and the rate 
ratio relative to placebo will be provided with 95% confidence intervals. These estimates will be 
reported as 4-week attack rates by transforming the estimates using the exponential function and 
scaling by the unit of time. The treatment effect in the primary efficacy endpoint evaluation will 
be tested using a Wald-based chi-square test.

17.3. Protocol Deviations 
Protocol deviations will be defined and recorded based on the Protocol Deviation Plan. The 
following deviations from the protocol will be considered protocol violations:  
• Deviations from the inclusion and exclusion criteria 
• Deviations from concomitant medication restrictions 
• Deviations from any other protocol requirements that result in a significant added risk 
to the participant or have an impact on the quality of the data collected or the outcome 
of the study. 
Protocol deviations will be reported in the final Clinical Study Report. 
17.4. Data Generation and Analysis 
The Investigator or designee must enter all protocol-required data in the available eCRF, which 
is a validated system. In the interest of collecting data in the most efficient manner, the 
Investigator or designee should enter data into the eCRF as soon as possible after the 
participant’s visit. Laboratory results from the central laboratory will be electronically 
transferred. The eCRFs and any supporting documents should be available for retrieval at any 
given time. The Monitor should verify the data in the eCRFs with source documents and confirm 
that there are no inconsistencies between them. If any inconsistency is detected on the eCRFs, 
the Monitor must query the Investigator and the Investigator or designee should make 
corrections/additions in the eCRF or provide an explanation within the eCRF system. The 
Monitor should verify the corrected data with source documents to confirm that there are no 
inconsistencies between them, and also check that appropriate records on the 
corrections/additions of data are maintained. 
Electronic patient reported outcomes (ePRO) data will be collected with an electronic device and 
electronic application. The electronic device is a validated 21 CFR Part 11-compliant electronic 
data capture tool. Provisions will be made for back-up method(s) in case of device malfunction.  
For Screening failures, defined as participants who signed informed consent but were not eligible 
for study participation, relevant data will be collected in the eCRF. 
The Investigator is responsible for ensuring that all data in the eCRF are accurate and complete, 
and that all entries are verifiable with source documents. Source data must be available at the site 
to document the existence of the study participants, and to substantiate the integrity of study data 
collected. In case a participant is prematurely discontinued, the reason for discontinuation must 
be documented in the source documents and eCRF. Study data collected from a participant 
before study discontinuation will still be used in the overall data analysis.
