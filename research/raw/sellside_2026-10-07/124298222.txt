                         Safety generally looked clean, though liver signals will be worth watching as data matures in
                         OLE. Deucrictibant XR was generally well tolerated with the most common AEs upper respiratory
                         tract infection, nasopharyngitis, and headache though these were generally mild-to-moderate
                         and balanced between the active and placebo groups. There was also a slight imbalance in gd.3
                         events (5.5% vs. 0% in placebo) - including a tooth infection and two gd.3 liver enzyme elevations
                         above 5x ULN; while the liver enzyme elevations are somewhat concerning, we note that these
                         cases were both asymptomatic and potentially confounded by long term androgen use/fatty
                         liver though one of these patients discontinued (discontinuations were balanced with placebo).
                         We would note that Orladeyo also had one discontinuation due to liver enzyme elevations (vs.
                         0 on placebo) although this is not something indicated on the label. Still, our KOL feedback
                         suggests that these are AEs worth monitoring, and if they persist in the OLE trial, would likely
                         necessitate liver monitoring; we would look for additional context at upcoming medical congress
                         presentations/publications. There was one gd.4 TEAE in the drug arm of laryngeal dyskinesia,
                         though we do not think this was related to drug as the patient had a history of laryngeal attacks
                         and was still otherwise a good responder.                  
                         Given the compelling efficacy data, and our KOL feedback, we see upside to our prior
                         estimates. Our KOL believes that deucrictibant XR is likely to be highly competitive, taking market
                         share from existing prophy drugs and expects XR could be the preferred front-line option for
                         prophylaxis-naive patients. Our KOL also expects all of his current Orladeyo patients (4/17) to
                         switch within the first year of XR being available (acknowledging this may not be the view taken
                         by all docs), noting that even patients who appear nearly attack-free on Orladeyo may have
                         simply modified their lives significantly to reduce the risk of breakthrough attacks and so could
                         have quality of life improvements on XR. For those on injectables, he noted the HAE space has a
                         culture of switching given how rapidly the treatment landscape has evolved, and since patients
                         have already switched multiple times, he sees entrenchment as less of a major barrier. We now

      Company description                                                           
                                                                                    
      Pharvaris (PHVS) is a late-stage biopharmaceutical company focused on the development of novel treatments for bradykinin-
      mediated angioedema disorders, including hereditary angioedema (HAE) and acquired angioedema (AAE). The company's lead
      candidate, deucrictibant, is a selective bradykinin B2 receptor antagonist available in both immediate-release (IR) oral capsule
      formulation for on-demand treatment of acute angioedema attacks and an extended-release (XR) film-coated tablet formulation
      for QD prophylaxis. PHVS is preparing global marketing authorization applications for deucrictibant IR as an on-demand treatment
      of HAE attacks, with the pivotal RAPIDe-3 trial demonstrating statistically significant efficacy and tolerability, and also for XR
      following recent positive CHAPTER-3 data. Additionally, CREAATE, a ph.III study of deucrictibant for both prophylactic and on-
      demand treatment of AAE-C1INH attacks, is underway. PHVS was founded in 2015 and is headquartered in Zug, Switzerland.
