     More From the Call                                                           
     Mgmt. Insights on Potential Commercial Launch                                
                                                                                  
     ■ IR launch could accelerate XR adoption. Deucrictibant IR will allow PHVS   
       to establish physician familiarity and patient experience with the MoA ahead
       of XR approval, simplifying treatment decisions, potentially shortening the
       typical launch curve. ~95% of the commercial team should be hired by YE26, 
       with initial focus on a concentrated base of ~2,000 core HAE prescribers,  
       which can be well-managed by a lean sales force.                           
                                                                                  
     ■ Dual-formulation strategy an advantage for payer negotiations;             
       engagement is already underway. Formulary, reimbursement, and              
       patient-support infrastructure established during the IR launch will provide
       a meaningful head start when entering the larger LTP market, with the dual 
       formulation potentially creating a more attractive value proposition. >90% 
       of coverage discussions with PBMs, commercial plans, and government        
       payers should be active by IR approval (PDUFA 4/23/27). Mgmt. is already   
       leveraging both RAPIDe-3 and CHAPTER-1 data in payer engagements.          
     Mgmt. Insights on Competitive Profile                                        
                                                                                  
     ■ Onset of benefit rivals (potentially exceeds) injectables. Deucrictibant   
       XR appeared to achieve near-maximal efficacy by Wk1, which we view as      
       impressive for an oral therapy and compares favorably with injectable peers
       and could ease transitions to deucrictibant from other therapies.

     Valuation and Risks                                                          
     Valuation. Our $65 price target is based on a discounted cash flow (DCF) valuation with cash flows forecasted through 2035, using a 12% discount
     rate and a 0% terminal growth rate. Key Investment Risks: (1) While early data on deucrictibant appear promising, results from late-stage
     trials could fall short of expectations. Any delays or unfavorable outcome from its planned clinical trials could affect the company’s valuation;
     (2) Material delays or failure to obtain regulatory approvals could severely affect the viability of Pharvaris’ business as a going concern; (3)
     Projected future sales may not materialize due to unsuccessful launch strategies, lower-than expected uptake, competitive pressure, and/or
     payor pushback; and (4) Pharvaris is a clinical-stage biotech company, and we do not expect it to be profitable on a continuing basis in the near
     term. As such, the company is likely to raise additional capital, potentially through secondary stock offerings that could dilute the holdings of
     current investors.
