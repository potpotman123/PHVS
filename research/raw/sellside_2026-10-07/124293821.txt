        ● Deucrictibant XR has injectable-like efficacy in a daily oral pill. Treatment
          with deucrictibant XR in participants with all types of hereditary angioedema
          (HAE) showed 83% attack rate reduction (p<0.0001). In Type I and II HAE, which
          more closely resembles other prophylactic clinical trial populations, attack rate
          reduction was 87%. Protection onset was rapid (therapeutic exposure within a day,
          steady state in 2–3 days) and durable through 24 weeks, and nearly half of treated
          patients were completely attack-free for the full treatment period.       
        ● Liver enzymes should not impact labeling. There were 3 Gr3 AEs: 1 tooth   
          infection and 2 "Level 2" liver enzyme elevations (3.6%, 2/55, defined as level
          2 by FDA guidance here, Table 56). Per mgmt, these elevations were 'slightly
          above' 5xULN and asymptomatic with no elevated bilirubin. Both patients had
          confounding factors: either long term androgen therapy (continued therapy and
          completed the trial) or fatty liver disease (discontinued trial). The discontinuation in
          the pbo arm was due to urticaria. By definition, a Gr3 elevation is >5-20x ULN, and
          it appears these elevations are on the low end of that range. We are encouraged
          that bilirubin was not affected and that this has not been seen in any prior studies.
          Recall, TAK's (Not Rated) Takhzyro had three liver enzyme elevations >3xULN
          and one elevation >8xULN in its Ph3, and one patient discontinued due to  
          elevated LFTs (AST 4.1x ULN). In the Orledayo (BCRX, Not Rated) Ph3, there
          was one asymptomatic Gr4 ALT elevation in a patient with previous androgen
          exposure who discontinued the study drug. Taken together, we do not believe
          these elevations reported today will warrant liver monitoring on the label.
        ● Efficacy in nC1INH was mixed, though lack of broad label does not change  
          the thesis. There were only 5 patients with HAE with normal C1 inhibitor  
          (nC1INH) in the trial: 2 in pbo and 3 in the treatment arm. Of those treated,
          one patient had <50% attack rate reduction, one was at 90%, and one was   
          fully attack free. We estimate ~1,000 nC1INH patients are in the US, though
          genetic testing is not standard, so many patients are initially treated as Type I
          or II patients. Still, deucrictibant's direct mechanism on the bradykinin receptor
          makes sense for nC1INH, so we don't expect there to be any competition from
          other prophylactic therapies in this population. Even without nC1INH in the label,
          we expect deucrictibant to be used broadly in prophylactic HAE, and the lower
          penetration in nC1INH does not meaningfully impact our peak sales (~$1.3B in
          2038).                                                                    
        ● What's next for deucrictibant: NDA submission in 1H27. CHAPTER-4 OLE      
          study of deucrictibant XR for the prophylaxis of HAE attacks is ongoing, and the
          data are gating to expected NDA submission in 1H27. Topline data from Part 1 of
          CREAATE (NCT07266805), a global, pivotal Ph3 evaluating deucrictibant XR for
          the prevention of AAE-C1INH attacks, are anticipated in the 1Q27. Enrollment in
          CREAATE is ongoing and progressing as planned. Commercially, PHVS says it 
          will have ~95% of its sales infrastructure staffed by YE26 and is targeting >90%
          live payer coverage conversations by on-demand approval, using CHAPTER    
          1 Ph2 prophy data today to pre-condition payers ahead of the prophy launch.
          Management also confirmed EU filing validation with EMA is already in motion
          alongside the US filing.

        INVESTMENT   THESIS                                                         
                                                                                    
        We rate PHVS shares Outperform. PHVS, led by an experienced and deeply      
        knowledgeable management team, is developing therapies for those with rare diseases,
        with a focus on hereditary angioedema (HAE), targeting the well-understood and clinically
        validated bradykinin B2 receptor. While there are many efficacious HAE therapies available,
        most require burdensome subcutaneous injections or infusions, inconvenient dosing
        regimens, or undesired side effects, all of which further disrupt the life of the patient.
        Numerous patient surveys and conversations with KOLs demonstrate the need for new and
        more convenient therapy options. PHVS is building a budding HAE platform around its lead
        asset, deucrictibant, a first-in-class, oral small molecule antagonist of the bradykinin B2
        receptor, which has the potential to offer comparable efficacy to currently available therapy
        while also allowing for discrete and quick oral administration. In our view, deucrictibant is
        partially de-risked, as icatibant (Firazyr), an approved therapy for HAE, utilizes a similar
        mechanism that acts directly at the bradykinin B2 receptor to treat all types of HAE by
        inhibiting bradykinin interaction with its bradykinin B2 receptor target. While extremely
        efficacious, icatibant, like many of the approved therapies, is limited as it requires an invasive
        route of administration, a major sticking point for patients. PHVS plans to leverage the same
        API and is developing deucrictibant into two different formulations: a soft-capsule form
        (PHVS416) for the on-demand treatment of an HAE attack, and an extended-release tablet
        formulation (PHVS719) for the prevention of HAE attacks. We ascribe an 95% probability
        of success (PoS) to deucrictibant for the treatment of on-demand attacks, and project
        gross peak sales of ~$440M in 2037E. As for deucrictibant for the prophylaxis treatment
        of HAE attacks, we ascribe a 95% PoS and project gross peak sales of ~$1.3B in 2038E.
        Additionally, PHVS is exploring deucrictibant in acquired angioedema due to C1 inhibitor
        deficiency, which is not currently included in our valuation.               
        VALUATION                                                                   
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
        We derive a one-year PT of $65 for PHVS based on a DCF analysis using a 13% discount
        rate and a 2% terminal growth rate, converting the implied market cap from EUR to USD
        by using a 6-month FX average, representing an implied ~$3.8B market capitalization.
        Our valuation is driven by probability-adjusted revenue for the two different formulations
        of deucrictibant for HAE. We ascribe a 95% PoS for on-demand and a 95% PoS for
        prophylaxis.                                                                
        RISKS  TO VALUATION                                                         
                                                                                    
        As a late-clinical-stage company, risks to PHVS include potential clinical, regulatory,
        and financial setbacks. Promising early clinical data may not translate to success in later-
        stage development. Regulatory decisions are unpredictable and may adversely affect
        the commercial potential of Pharvaris product candidates. Competition in the hereditary
        angioedema space (e.g., Takhzyro, Haegarda, Ekterly, Orladeyo) may limit commercial
        success(es) of Pharvaris products, if approved. From a financial perspective, dilutive
        financing is a potential risk. Other unanticipated situations could arise that may negatively or
        positively impact the stock.
