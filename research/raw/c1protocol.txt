Primary Objectives: 
Part 1 
• To evaluate the safety and tolerability of two dose regimens of PHA-022121 administered 
as prophylaxis against hereditary angioedema (HAE) attacks.  
• To evaluate the efficacy of two dose regimens of PHA-022121 administered as 
prophylaxis against HAE attacks.  
Part 2 
• To evaluate the safety of PHA-022121 administered as long-term prophylactic HAE 
treatment. 
Secondary Objective: 
Part 1 
• To characterize the pharmacokinetics/pharmacodynamics (PK/PD) of two dose regimens 
of PHA-022121 administered as prophylaxis against HAE attacks.  
• To evaluate the impact on quality of life (QoL) of two dose regimens of PHA-022121 
administered as prophylaxis against HAE attacks. 
Part 2 
• To evaluate the efficacy of PHA-022121 administered as long-term prophylactic HAE 
treatment.

Study Design: 
• This is a Phase II, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, 
dose-ranging, parallel group, 2-part study to evaluate the safety and efficacy of 
PHA-022121 administered orally for prophylaxis against angioedema attacks in HAE 
patients due to C1-inhibitor (C1-INH) deficiency (type I or type II) (see study design 
schematic below).  
• The study consists of 2 parts, with patients completing participation in Part 1 prior to 
initiation of treatment in Part 2. Part 1 is a double-blind, placebo-controlled, 12-week 
evaluation of the efficacy and safety of 2 dose regimens of PHA-022121. Part 2 is an 
open-label evaluation of the long-term safety and efficacy of a single dose regimen of 
PHA-022121. The duration of the treatment period in Part 2 is 30 months and may be 
extended until another clinical study is available for patients to continue open-label 
treatment with PHA-022121, provided that the safety and efficacy data from the ongoing 
studies support continuing the use of PHA-022121. If the treatment period is extended, 
revisions will be addressed via a protocol amendment. Any patient who withdraws consent 
or is discontinued from the study at any time will complete an end-of-study visit 4 weeks 
after the last treatment administration. 
• Following signing of informed consent, patients will undergo screening assessments. The 
screening period may last up to 8 weeks.  
• For Part 1, eligible patients will be randomized in a 1:1:1 ratio to receive placebo or one of 
two dose regimens of PHA-022121 (10 mg or 20 mg twice daily [BID]). Each patient will 
receive blinded investigational medicinal product (IMP) (i.e., PHA-022121 or placebo) to 
be administered orally BID after breakfast and dinner for a duration of 12 weeks of 
treatment. 
• For Part 2, patients will administer open-label PHA-022121 20 mg BID after breakfast and 
dinner for a duration of 30 months. To be eligible for continued treatment in Part 2, the 
patient must have completed Part 1, unless discontinued from Part 1 due to regulatory 
actions. If there is a gap in treatment between Parts 1 and 2, Visit 7 safety assessments are 
to be performed, with results reviewed by the Investigator, and patient eligibility 
reconfirmed before the patient may restart treatment in Part 2.  
• Throughout the study (both Part 1 and Part 2), an electronic patient-reported outcomes 
(ePRO) HAE diary (i.e., ePRO HAE diary) will be used daily to record any symptoms and 
details of HAE attacks, including relevant treatments. Patients will have access and be able 
to treat any HAE attack that may occur using standard of care on-demand acute therapies, 
in accordance with World Allergy Organization (WAO) guidelines and local regulations. 
In addition to the collection information on HAE attacks, the ePRO HAE diary will be 
used as a dosing diary to record each administration of IMP. 
• Patients will complete an end-of-study visit 4 weeks after the last treatment administration. 
This visit may be waived if patients continue in another clinical study of open-label

Study Population:  
Approximately 30 male or female, adult patients with HAE type I/II will be randomized. 
HAE diagnosis will be confirmed through documented clinical history consistent with HAE 
and diagnostic testing conducted during the screening visit. 
The study will be conducted at approximately 30 study sites globally (North America, and 
Europe). 
Test Product, Dose, and Mode of Administration:  
PHVS416 is a soft capsule formulation containing PHA-022121. Patients will record daily in 
the ePRO HAE diary each administration of IMP. 
Part 1 
The IMP consists of 10 mg PHA-022121 soft capsules and matching placebo capsules for oral 
intake. The capsules are to be swallowed with at least 200 mL of water after breakfast and 
dinner, with 12 ± 2 hours between each dose.  
Patients will randomly be assigned to one of three treatment groups and will receive blinded 
IMP as indicated:  
• 10 mg BID: one capsule of 10 mg PHA-022121 and one placebo capsule after breakfast 
and dinner 
• 20 mg BID: two capsules of 10 mg PHA-022121 after breakfast and dinner 
• Placebo: two placebo capsules after breakfast and dinner 
Part 2 
All patients will receive open-label PHA-022121 20 mg BID, which consists of two capsules 
of 10 mg PHA-022121 to be administered orally after breakfast and dinner. 
The capsules are to be swallowed with at least 200 mL of water after breakfast and dinner, 
with 12 ± 2 hours between each dose. 
Duration of Treatment: 
In Part 1, patients will administer blinded IMP (PHA-022121 10 mg BID, or PHA-022121 
20 mg BID, or placebo BID) for a duration of 12 weeks. 
In Part 2, patients will administer open-label PHA-022121 20 mg BID for a duration of 
30 months, which may be extended until another clinical study is available for patients to 
continue open-label treatment with PHA-022121, provided that the safety and efficacy data

Safety Assessments: 
Safety will be assessed based on the following: 
• Adverse events 
• Safety laboratory testing (hematology, biochemistry, coagulation, urinalysis and 
pregnancy) 
• Vital signs (heart rate, blood pressure, body temperature and, in Part 1 only, respiratory 
rate) 
• Physical examination 
• 12-lead electrocardiogram (ECG) (digital triplicate) 
Efficacy Assessments: 
Efficacy will be assessed based upon investigator-confirmation of patient-reported details of 
occurrences of HAE attacks. During screening, site personnel will discuss with patients the 
requirements for reporting attacks, including the information that is to be captured for each 
HAE attack.  
Information on HAE attacks will be collected daily in an ePRO HAE diary. Patients will 
receive training on how to use the ePRO device at the screening visit and throughout the study, 
as needed. Patients will be instructed to report details of any HAE attack to the study site 
within 72 hours of onset of symptoms of an attack. 
The investigator will complete a separate electronic case report form (eCRF) for each 
confirmed attack based on their review of the data in the patient’s ePRO HAE diary and in 
follow-up with the patient. Additional queries by the investigator or site personnel during 
patient follow-up will include questions such as: whether the patient only experienced 
prodromal symptoms, whether the symptoms experienced were typical or different from prior 
attacks, or whether there were possible alternative etiologies for the symptoms. Any patient-
reported HAE attack that is not confirmed by the investigator as an HAE attack, must have an 
alternate adverse event (AE) diagnosis recorded.  
Information recorded in the eCRF will include, but is not limited to, the following:  
• Date and time of onset of symptoms of an HAE attack 
• Location (site) of the swelling or pain (e.g., upper airways [laryngeal, pharyngeal], 
gastrointestinal/abdominal, facial, truncal, genitourinary; multiple sites may be involved)  
• Severity (mild, moderate, or severe) based on impact of symptoms on patient’s daily 
activity 
• Investigator assessment of symptom(s) 
• Acute treatments administered for the HAE attack (date, time, and medication(s) including 
the number of doses administered) 
• Date and time of resolution of HAE symptoms 
• If hospitalization or a visit to emergency department occurred 
Pharmacokinetic Assessments: 
Blood samples will be collected at specified time points to determine plasma concentrations of 
PHA-022121 and metabolites.

Quality of Life Assessments: 
The angioedema quality of life questionnaire (AE-QoL), the angioedema control test 4-week 
version (AECT-4wk), patient global assessment of quality of life (PGA-QoL) and patient 
global assessment of change (PGA-change) questions, and the treatment satisfaction 
questionnaire for medication (TSQM) will be used to assess health-related quality of life. 
Statistical Methods:  
Safety Analysis  
Part 1 and Part 2 
The analysis of safety will be performed on the Safety Analysis Set (i.e., all randomized 
patients who have received at least 1 dose of PHA-022121 or placebo). Treatment-emergent 
AEs (TEAEs) are defined as AEs that start or pre-existing AEs that have worsened after the 
initiation of IMP treatment. For patients with no gap between Part 1 and Part 2, Part 1 TEAEs 
are AEs that start or pre-existing AEs that have worsened during the time period between the 
first dose of IMP in Part 1 and the first dose in Part 2; Part 2 TEAEs are AEs that start or pre-
existing AEs that have worsened during the time period between the first dose of IMP in Part 2 
and 4 weeks after the last dose of IMP in Part 2 or the End of Study visit, whichever is later. 
For patients with a gap between Part 1 and Part 2, Part 1 TEAEs are AEs that start or pre-
existing AEs that have worsened during the time period between the first dose of IMP in Part 1 
and 4 weeks after the last dose of IMP in Part 1. Part 2 TEAEs are AEs that start or pre-
existing AEs that have worsened during the time period between the first dose of IMP in Part 2 
and 4 weeks after the last dose of IMP in Part 2 or the End of Study visit, whichever is later.  
The number and percentage of patients with TEAEs in Part 1 will be displayed for each 
treatment group by body system organ class (SOC) and preferred term using the Medical 
Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (MedDRA
®). Summaries in terms of Common 
Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) grade/severity and relationship to IMP 
will also be provided. Serious AEs (SAEs) will be summarized separately in a similar fashion. 
Patient listings of TEAEs leading to discontinuation of IMP, AEs/SAEs leading to death, 
patient withdrawals, pregnancies, and SAEs will also be produced.  
Usage of concomitant medications (other than rescue medications) in Part 1 will be 
summarized descriptively for each of the treatment groups and the combined active treatment 
group. 
Clinical laboratory data, vital signs, physical examination, and ECG data will be presented in 
summary tables and/or individual listings. 
The safety data will be analyzed separately for Part 1. Safety data from Parts 1 and 2 will be 
combined for a long-term safety assessment. 
Efficacy Analysis 
Part 1 
The primary efficacy endpoint will be the number of investigator-confirmed HAE attacks 
during the treatment period in Part 1 (Day 0 through Day 84) and will be expressed as the 
normalized number of attacks per month (4 weeks) of exposure. The monthly attack rate is 
calculated for each patient as number of HAE attacks occurring during the treatment period 
divided by the number of days the patient contributed to the treatment period multiplied by 
28 days.

The analysis of the primary efficacy endpoint will be performed based on the intent-to-treat 
(ITT) Analysis Set, and will compare each PHA-022121 dose group with the placebo group 
using a generalized linear model (GLM) assuming a Poisson distribution with a log link 
function and Pearson chi-square scaling of standard errors to account for potential dispersion. 
The model will include fixed effects for treatment group (categorical), the normalized baseline 
attack rate (continuous), the stratification factor of baseline attack rate (categorical), and an 
offset term of the logarithm of patient’s follow-up time (in months) during the efficacy 
evaluation period. The estimated least squares (LS) mean rate for each PHA-022121 dose 
group and the mean rate ratios relative to the placebo group will be provided with 95% 
confidence intervals. These estimates will be reported as monthly attack rates (mean numbers 
of events per 4 weeks) by transforming the estimates using the exponential function and 
scaling by the unit of time. The treatment effect in the primary efficacy endpoint will be tested 
using a Wald-based chi-square test. Analysis using a negative binomial GLM will be 
performed as sensitivity analysis. In addition, Wilcoxon two-sample test will also be 
conducted comparing each PHA-022121 dose group with the placebo group. 
The primary efficacy analysis will be conducted after all patients have completed Part 1 of the 
study. 
Part 2 
The analysis of the efficacy endpoints in Part 2 will be performed based on the ITT analysis 
set. The number of investigator-confirmed HAE attacks during the treatment period in Part 2 
will be expressed as the normalized number of attacks per month (4 weeks) of exposure. The 
normalized number of investigator-confirmed HAE attacks during the treatment period in 
Part 2 will be descriptively summarized. Similarly, normalized number of investigator-
confirmed moderate or severe HAE attacks during the treatment period in Part 2, normalized 
number of investigator-confirmed HAE attacks requiring acute treatment during the treatment 
period in Part 2, and proportion of days with HAE symptoms during the treatment period in 
Part 2 will be summarized.  
All efficacy analyses in Part 2 will be descriptive. 
Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Evaluation 
The plasma concentrations of PHA-022121 will be summarized. 
The pharmacokinetic evaluation will consist of a population pharmacokinetic analysis of the 
sparse samples taken in this study. The relationship between plasma exposure and 
efficacy/safety parameters will also be evaluated (pharmacodynamics).

1 INTRODUCTION 
1.1 Hereditary Angioedema 
Hereditary angioedema (HAE) is a rare disease. It is estimated that HAE affects 1 in 50,000 
individuals, though reports vary between 1 in 10,000 and 1 in 150,000 individuals (Zuraw 2008; 
Bygum 2009; Zanichelli et al. 2015; Aygören-Pürsün et al. 2018).  
The disease manifests as edematous attacks most commonly in the limbs, face (lips and tongue), 
intestinal tract, urogenital region, and airways. Attacks often lead to discomfort, pain and nausea 
and can become life-threatening in case of airway obstruction and asphyxiation if the attack 
remains untreated (Zuraw 2008; Bork et al., 2012). Asphyxiation can occur rapidly in patients of 
any age, and it has occurred in patients with no previous history of respiratory symptoms (Bork 
et al., 2012). The number and severity of attacks vary highly between patients and throughout a 
patients’ lifetime. If untreated, attacks last 2 to 5 days (Zuraw, 2008; Nordenfelt et al., 2016; 
Cicardi and Zuraw, 2018). The most severely affected patients can experience multiple attacks a 
month.  
Acute episodes of HAE often occur without warning and may be precipitated by triggers such as 
estrogens, dental procedures, pregnancy, and stress (Busse and Christiansen, 2020). The 
unexpected nature of HAE attacks result in the disruption of normal life with the need of 
frequent healthcare visits, many days of absence from work or school, and impaired quality of 
life (Lumry et al., 2010; Nordenfelt et al., 2014; Caballero et al., 2014; Nordenfelt et al., 2016). 
Despite advances in treatment options, patients with HAE continue to have a high burden of 
illness (Banerji et al., 2020). 
Three types of HAE have been characterized. Type I and II are defined by mutations in the 
SERPING1 gene which encodes the C1-inhibitor (C1-INH) protein and result either in reduced 
levels of C1-INH (type I) or in reduced activity of C1-INH (type II). In both cases, decreased 
C1-INH protease activity results in increased activity of Factor XII and plasma kallikrein, 
resulting in an activated kallikrein-kinin system (KKS), with associated excessive breakdown of 
high molecular weight kininogen and increased plasma levels of bradykinin (Kaplan and Joseph, 
2010). A new form of HAE was first described in 2000 where affected individuals (mainly 
women) had normal levels and activity of C1-INH (Binkley and Davis, 2000; Bork et al., 2000). 
Initially classified as type III HAE, the disease is currently defined as HAE with normal C1-INH 
(HAE-nC1-INH). The disease is characterized by mutations in other genes (e.g., FXII, PLG, 
ANGPT1, KNG1, MYOF) involved in the activation of the KKS and generation of bradykinin or 
in the stabilization of the vasculature (Dewald and Bork, 2006; Bork et al., 2018; Bafunno et al., 
2018; Bork et al., 2019a; Ariano et al., 2020). 
Bradykinin is the principal mediator of the signs and symptoms that represent acute HAE 
attacks. Excessive bradykinin generation, as in HAE, promotes vascular permeability, leading to 
plasma extravasation and subcutaneous or submucosal tissue swelling typical of an angioedema 
attack. Two bradykinin receptor types, B1 and B2, have been identified, cloned and 
characterized. Both are G protein-coupled receptors, seven transmembrane proteins. Bradykinin 
B1 receptors are inducible, while B2 receptors are constitutively expressed. Bradykinin has a 
high affinity and high agonist potency at the B2 receptor, but a near 1000-fold lower affinity and 
potency at the B1 receptor, making bradykinin a selective B2 receptor agonist.

1.2 Current Treatment of HAE 
There are currently two treatment approaches to the management of HAE: acute (on-demand) 
treatment of attacks and prevention of attacks with short or long-term prophylactic therapy. 
Patients and their physicians have several choices for on-demand or prophylactic treatment, but 
because there are differences in the route of administration, side effect profiles, and efficacy 
between these medications, patient preference and experience need to be considered in selecting 
the most appropriate therapy. 
Various guidelines for treatment of HAE have evolved over the years, in part in response to the 
increasing availability of treatment options. According to the United States Hereditary 
Angioedema Association (US HAEA) Medical Advisory Board 2020 Guidelines (Busse et al., 
2021), all patients must have ready access to effective on-demand and self-administered (or 
administered by a caregiver) treatment, and all attacks should be treated regardless of their 
location or severity. According to these same guidelines, the decision on when to use long-term 
prophylactic treatment cannot be made on rigid criteria but should reflect the needs of the 
individual patient. Early on, guidelines proposed that the overall goal of treatment in HAE was to 
“restore normal quality of life to the patient” (Zuraw et al., 2013). The most recent guidelines 
(Bork et al., 2019b; Maurer et al., 2022) and the ones developed with a state-of-the-art 
methodology (Delphi process), which is the international consensus on treatment goals in HAE 
(Maurer et al., 2021), define the goal of HAE treatment as achieving an absence of attack, thus 
implying that most patients should be on a prophylactic treatment. The 2022 World Allergy 
Organization/European Academy of Allergy and Clinical Immunology (WAO/EAACI) guideline 
(Maurer et al., 2022) recommends that patients are evaluated for long-term prophylaxis at every 
visit, taking disease activity, burden, and control as well as patient preference into consideration. 
On-demand treatments 
The bradykinin B2 receptor antagonist icatibant (Firazyr
®) is the mainstay of acute treatment due 
to its quick onset of action and easy subcutaneous (sc) self-administration by a prefilled syringe. 
The kallikrein inhibitor ecallantide (Kalbitor®) requires hospital or health professional sc 
administration due to potential anaphylaxis. Ecallantide has approval in the United States only. 
Plasma-derived or recombinant C1-INH concentrates (Berinert®, Cinryze® and Ruconest®) 
require intravenous (iv) administration. 
Prophylactic treatments 
The plasma-derived C1-INH concentrates (Cinryze and Haegarda®/Berinert® 2000/3000) are 
administered by iv and sc route, respectively. These products are administered twice weekly 
(every 3 or 4 days). Plasma kallikrein inhibitors include lanadelumab (Takhzyro®), a fully human 
recombinant monoclonal antibody which is administered sc once or twice a month and 
berotralstat administered orally, once daily. Attenuated androgens (including danazol, stanozolol, 
oxandrolone, oxymetholone, tibolone, methyltestosterone) and antifibrinolytics, such as 
ɛ-aminocaproic acid and tranexamic acid are used for the prevention of acute attacks and are 
licensed in some countries. However, current treatment guidelines recommend against the use of 
attenuated androgens and antifibrinolytics as first-line therapy due to limited efficacy and/or 
safety issues with associated contraindications (Maurer et al., 2022; Busse et al., 2021).

Currently there are two other oral medications in clinical development for on-demand or 
prophylactic therapy in patients with HAE; these products are plasma kallikrein inhibitors

1.3 PHA-022121 for the Treatment of HAE 
PHA-022121 is an orally bioavailable, low-molecular weight, competitive antagonist of the 
human bradykinin B2 receptor with high affinity, high antagonist potency, and high selectivity 
(more than 5,000-fold compared with B1 receptor and other molecular targets).  
Nonclinical support for the potential efficacy of PHA-022121 derives from nonclinical studies in 
which orally administered PHA-022121 demonstrated dose-dependent inhibition of bradykinin-
induced hemodynamic changes in cynomolgus monkeys and carrageenan-induced paw edema in 
humanized bradykinin B2 receptor Sprague Dawley rats. Support for the safety of PHA-022121 
derives from completed respiratory, central nervous system (CNS), and cardiovascular safety 
pharmacology studies, which did not reveal any safety concerns at the exposure levels 
anticipated to be achieved at clinically planned doses, as well as results of 4- and 13-week Good 
Laboratory Practice (GLP) repeat-dose toxicology studies in rat and GLP studies in monkey of 
up to 39 weeks duration. In a 6-month general toxicity rat study and 10-week humanized 
bradykinin B2-receptor transgenic male rat fertility study, a low incidence of handling-induced 
convulsions was observed. The overall incidence of convulsions in the studies was 4.2% in the 
26-week rat toxicity study and 5% in the male fertility study. These incidences were within the 
upper range of historical incidences at the conducting sites and in the literature. Moreover, the 
onset, frequency, and severity were not dose- or exposure-related, had no histopathologic 
correlate, and occurred in animals at 22 weeks of age or older when spontaneous convulsions can 
be observed in rats. Furthermore, the incidence of handling-induced convulsions in an ongoing 
2-year carcinogenicity study in of 0.57%, is well below the incidence observed in the 26-week 
rat study and male fertility study despite higher systemic exposure, longer duration of treatment 
(up to 17 months duration), and greater number of animals treated (≥500). 
Results of the primary analysis from double-blind, placebo-controlled Study PHA022121-C201, 
showed that PHA-022121 10, 20, and 30 mg significantly (p<0.0001, nominal for 10 mg) 
reduced attack symptoms from pre-treatment to 4 hours post treatment versus placebo.

3 INVESTIGATIONAL PLAN 
3.1 Overall Study Design and Plan 
This is a Phase II, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, dose-ranging, 
parallel group, 2-part study to evaluate the safety and efficacy of PHA-022121 administered 
orally for prophylaxis against angioedema attacks in HAE patients due to C1-INH deficiency 
(type I or type II). 
The study will be conducted at approximately 30 sites globally (North America and Europe). 
The study consists of 2 parts with patients completing participation in Part 1 prior to initiation of 
treatment in Part 2. Part 1 is a double-blind, placebo-controlled, 12-week evaluation of the 
efficacy and safety of 2 dose regimens of PHA-022121. Part 2 is an open-label evaluation of the 
long-term safety and efficacy of a single dose regimen of PHA-022121. The duration of the 
treatment period in Part 2 is 30 months and may be extended until another clinical study is 
available for patients to continue open-label treatment with PHA-022121, provided that the 
safety and efficacy data from the ongoing studies support continuing the use of PHA-022121. If 
the treatment period is extended, revisions will be addressed via a protocol amendment. Any 
patient who withdraws consent or is discontinued from the study at any time will complete an 
end-of-study visit 4 weeks after the last treatment administration. 
Following signing of informed consent, patients will undergo screening assessments. The 
screening period may last up to 8 weeks. All patients receiving HAE medications for long-term 
prophylaxis (LTP) will be required to discontinue these medications prior to screening. The 
duration of the washout period for prophylactic treatments is dependent upon pharmacokinetic 
profile of the prophylactic medication and should be equivalent to at least 5 half-lives of the 
drug. For patients who receive any form of C1-INH replacement, there is the additional 
restriction that the last dose before the confirmatory C1-INH testing should be given at least 
5 half-lives before the date of sampling.  
For Part 1, eligible patients will be randomized in a 1:1:1 ratio to receive placebo or one of two 
dose regimens of PHA-022121 (10 mg or 20 mg BID). Each patient will receive blinded IMP to 
be administered orally BID after breakfast and dinner for a duration of 12 weeks of treatment. 
For Part 2, patients will administer open-label PHA-022121 20 mg BID after breakfast and 
dinner for a duration of 30 months. patients must have completed Part 1. To be eligible for 
continued treatment in Part 2, the patient must have completed Part 1, unless discontinued from 
Part 1 due to regulatory actions. If there is a gap in treatment between Parts 1 and 2, Visit 7 
safety assessments are to be performed, with results reviewed by the Investigator, and patient 
eligibility reconfirmed before the patient may restart treatment in Part 2. 
Throughout the study (both Part 1 and Part 2), an electronic patient-reported outcomes (ePRO) 
HAE diary (i.e., ePRO HAE diary) will be used daily to record any symptoms and details of 
HAE attacks, including relevant treatments. Patients will have access and be able to treat any 
HAE attack that may occur using standard of care on-demand acute therapies, in accordance with 
WAO guidelines and local regulations. In addition to the collection of information on HAE 
attacks, the ePRO HAE diary will be used as a dosing diary to record each administration of

3.2 Rationale for Study Design and Control Group 
This Phase II, double-blind, placebo-controlled, randomized, dose-ranging, parallel group, 2-part 
study aims to evaluate the safety and efficacy of 2 dosing regimens of PHA-022121 for the 
prophylaxis of angioedema attacks in patients with HAE type I/II. 
Part 1 
Part 1 of the study employs a placebo comparator to evaluate and interpret the efficacy of 
PHA-022121 for preventing attacks of HAE. The use of a placebo-controlled design is justified 
as (i) patients are required to have adequate access and are able to treat any HAE attack using 
standard of care on-demand acute therapies in accordance with WAO guidelines and local 
regulations; therefore patients are not placed at any unnecessary risk, and (ii) all therapies 
currently approved and recommended for prophylaxis of HAE attacks (Maurer et al., 2022; 
Busse et al., 2021), are administered either by iv or sc routes, which are not feasible for blinding 
the study. 
The duration of treatment is reflective of Part 1 study objectives. The primary objectives are the 
evaluation of safety, with number of investigator-confirmed attacks during the treatment period 
being the main endpoint for the assessment of efficacy. A treatment duration of 12 weeks will 
allow an appropriate initial evaluation of safety of the prophylactic PHA-022121 dose regimens 
compared with placebo, while also providing reasonable power (approximately 85%) to 
demonstrate whether the BID administration of PHA-022121 is able to reduce attack rates versus 
placebo.  
The study will employ endpoints that are standard in HAE trials for evaluating the efficacy of 
prophylactic treatment (Zuraw and Kalfus, 2012; Longhurst et al., 2017; Banerji et al., 2018; 
Zuraw et al., 2021). Efficacy endpoints detailing HAE attacks are medically appropriate, as 
attacks represent the fundamental event underlying the morbidity and mortality of HAE.

time of different dosing regimens for PHA-022121. These simulations facilitated the

engagement and efficacy over the chosen dosing interval during long-term daily

were implemented in NONMEM® version 7.3 using subroutine ADVAN 4, and parameter 
estimation was performed using the first order conditional estimation method (FOCE) with

5.6.1 Management of HAE Attacks 
Patients enrolling in the study must have reliable access to and ability to use standard of care 
acute treatments to manage any HAE attack that requires pharmacological intervention. In 
accordance with the WAO guidelines, standard of care acute treatments include: 
• Icatibant (Firazyr or generic equivalent) 
• Ecallantide (Kalbitor) 
• Plasma-derived or recombinant C1-INH concentrates (eg, Cinryze, Berinert, Ruconest) 
Use of standard of care acute treatments will follow local regulatory approval mandates. 
Icatibant is an oligopeptide drug that is metabolised by proteolytic enzymes, while ecallantide 
and the C1-INH concentrates are all biologics. While there is no data available on the 
coadministration of PHA-022121 with other approved acute HAE medications, due to the 
differences in the pathways for drug metabolism, the potential for drug-drug interactions is 
considered unlikely.  
Patients should continue to administer IMP and follow study procedures without alteration to the 
protocol schedule, even if they receive acute treatment for an HAE attack. All medications 
associated with the management of an acute attack must be recorded in the ePRO HAE diary.

6.11 Health-related Quality of Life Assessments 
6.11.1 Angioedema Quality of Life Questionnaire 
The Angioedema Quality of Life (AE-QoL) questionnaire is symptom-specific tool developed 
and validated to assess health-related quality of life impairment in patients with recurrent 
angioedema (Weller et al. 2012). The AE-QoL consists of 17 questions across four 
domains/dimensions (functioning, fatigue/mood, fear/shame, nutrition). Each of the 17 items has 
a five-point Likert-type response scale ranging from 1 (Never) to 5 (Very Often). The AE-QoL is 
scored to produce a score for each domain and a total score ranging from 0 to 100, with higher 
scores indicating greater impairment. 
6.11.2 Angioedema Quality of Life Anchor Questions  
Two anchor questions will also be completed by patients: a patient global assessment of quality 
o

Impression of Change [PGIC]). The PGA-QoL question covers how much the patient’s QoL was 
impacted by their HAE over the past 4 weeks with a four-point Likert response scale (not at all, 
slightly, moderately, highly). The PGA-change question covers the overall change in how the 
patient’s QoL has been impacted by their HAE since starting to take the IMP with a five-point 
Likert response scale (much better, a little better, no change, a little worse, or much worse). Both 
anchor questions were developed according to the FDA standards (FDA, 2018b) and will be used 
to help interpretation of a clinically meaningful within-patient score change in the AE-QoL 
questionnaire.

6.12 Adverse Events and Serious Adverse Events 
6.12.1 Definitions 
An adverse event (AE) is defined as, any untoward medical occurrence in a patient or clinical 
investigation subject administered a pharmaceutical product and which does not necessarily have 
to have a causal relationship with this treatment. An AE can therefore be any unfavorable and 
unintended sign, symptom, or disease temporally associated with the use of a medicinal product, 
whether or not considered related to the medicinal product. 
Baseline medical history/conditions, or abnormal diagnostic/laboratory values present at the 
screening visit, are not considered to be AEs, and should be recorded in the eCRF as baseline 
medical history. However, any worsening in severity, or an increase in frequency, of a pre-
existing baseline medical condition is considered to be an AE. 
Per ICH E2A, "In the pre-approval clinical experience with a new medicinal product or its new 
usages, particularly as the therapeutic dose(s) may not be established: all noxious and unintended 
responses to a medicinal product related to any dose should be considered adverse drug 
reactions. The phrase 'responses to a medicinal products' means that a causal relationship 
between a medicinal product and an adverse event is at least a reasonable possibility, i.e., the 
relationship cannot be ruled out." 
A serious adverse event (SAE) or reaction is any untoward medical occurrence that at any dose: 
• results in death; 
• is life-threatening (at the time of the event);  
NOTE: The term “life-threatening” in the definition of “serious” refers to an event in which 
the patient was at risk of death at the time of the event; it does not refer to an event which 
hypothetically might have caused death if it were more severe. 
• requires inpatient hospitalization or prolongation of an existing hospitalization; 
• results in persistent or significant disability/incapacity; 
• is a congenital anomaly/birth defect; or 
• is an important medical event that may jeopardize the patient or may require intervention to 
prevent one of the other outcomes listed above. 
Medical and scientific judgement should be exercised in deciding whether expedited reporting 
is appropriate in other situations, such as important medical events that may not be 
immediately life-threatening or result in death or hospitalization but may jeopardize the 
patient or may require intervention to prevent one of the other outcomes listed in the

6.12.3 Classification of an Adverse Event 
6.12.3.1 CTCAE Grade and Severity 
The National Cancer Institute (NCI) CTCAE is a descriptive terminology which can be utilized 
for event/Adverse Event (AE) reporting. Adverse events (also referred to as "events") are 
classified by CTCAE grade/severity.  
The CTCAE incorporates certain elements of the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities 
(MedDRA) terminology. The System Organ Class (SOC), is the highest level of the MedDRA 
hierarchy, is identified by anatomical or physiological system, etiology, or purpose (e.g., the 
SOC called "Investigations" is used for laboratory test results). An AE is a term that is a unique 
representation of a specific event used for medical documentation and scientific analyses. Each 
CTCAE term is a MedDRA lowest level term. 
Per NCI CTCAE, grade refers to the severity of the event. The CTCAE grade/severity should be 
evaluated and recorded on the eCRF according to the NCI CTCAE, version 5.0 (NCI, 2017). 
The grade refers to the severity of the AE. CTCAE displays Grades 1 through 5 with unique 
clinical descriptions of severity for each AE based on this general guideline:  
• Grade 1: Mild; asymptomatic or mild symptoms; clinical or diagnostic observations only; 
intervention not indicated 
• Grade 2: Moderate; minimal, local or noninvasive intervention indicated; limiting age- 
appropriate instrumental activities of daily living (ADL)
*. 
• Grade 3: Severe or medically significant but not immediately life-threatening; hospitalization 
or prolongation of hospitalization indicated; disabling; limiting self-care ADL**. 
• Grade 4: Life-threatening consequences; urgent intervention indicated 
• Grade 5: Death related to AE 
Not all Grades are appropriate for all AEs. Therefore, some AEs are listed with fewer than 
five options for Grade selection. 
* Instrumental ADL refer to preparing meals, shopping for groceries or clothes, using the 
telephone, managing money, etc. 
** Self-care ADL refer to bathing, dressing and undressing, feeding self, using the toilet, 
taking medications, and not bedridden.

6.12.3.2 Relationship to IMP 
All AEs/SAEs (events) will be assessed to determine their relationship to the IMP (causality). 
The investigator must assess the causality using the binary choices of “Related” or “Unrelated," 
and by considering the rationales listed below. Events should be considered “Related” only if 
there is a reasonable possibility of a causal relationship to the IMP. These rationales allow the 
investigator to more clearly support their own assessments. All AEs and SAEs should be 
reported, regardless of causality, including events that are thought to be "Related" to targeted 
disease and/or population. 
Rationales for an Unrelated assessment may include: 
• Event attributed to concomitant medication or disease 
• Event not reasonably temporally associated with IMP administration 
• Negative dechallenge and/or negative rechallenge 
Rationales for a Related assessment may include: 
• Temporal relationship of event to IMP exposure 
• Event is known to be associated with the IMP or drug class 
• Event improved on discontinuation or dose reduction of IMP 
• Event reoccurred on rechallenge with IMP 
• Biological plausibility

9 PLANNED STATISTICAL METHODS 
9.1 General Considerations 
Planned analyses for this study are summarized below. Details of the planned analyses, method 
of handling missing data, sensitivity analyses, subgroup analyses, and exploratory analyses will 
be provided in the SAP. The SAP will comply with the GCP requirements. Any deviations from 
the planned analyses will be justified in the clinical study report. All analyses will be carried out 
using SAS
® software Version 9.4 or higher (SAS Institute, Cary, NC, United States). 
9.2 Determination of Sample Size 
Power analysis and sample size estimation were conducted by assuming a Poisson distribution 
for angioedema attacks.  
  
Approximately 30 patients will be enrolled with an equal allocation rate to each group, with a 
randomization ratio of 1:1:1. Randomization to treatment groups will be stratified by the 
documented baseline attack rate prior to screening or confirmed during the screening period (1 to 
< 2 attacks, 2 to < 3 attacks, or ≥ 3 attacks per 4 weeks). For a treatment effect of 60% reduction 
in attacks as compared to placebo,  
 a sample size of 10 patients per group receiving active treatment and 10 patients receiving 
placebo would provide an approximate 85% power, at a significance level of 0.05 for two-sided 
test comparing each dose of PHA-022121 to placebo, without multiplicity correction for the two 
comparisons. Assuming a larger treatment effect of 80% reduction in attack rate, the power 
would increase to approximately 98%. The sample size consideration is to support the statistical 
testing for data from the blinded, placebo-controlled treatment phase (i.e., Part 1 analysis). 
9.3 Analysis Sets  
The analysis sets for this study are defined below: 
• Intent-to-treat (ITT) Analysis Set includes all randomized patients who received at least one 
dose of IMP (PHA-022121 or placebo). In the analyses performed on the ITT Analysis Set, 
unless otherwise specified, patients will be analyzed according to their assigned treatment 
group per randomization regardless of actual treatment received. 
• Safety Analysis Set includes all randomized patients who received at least one dose of IMP 
(PHA-022121 or placebo), essentially the same set of patients included in the ITT Analysis 
Set. In analyses performed on the Safety Analysis Set, patients will be analyzed according to 
their actual treatment received. 
• Pharmacokinetic Analysis Set includes as all randomized patients who contribute data to PK 
evaluations.

9.6 Efficacy Endpoints 
Part 1 
The primary efficacy endpoint will be the number of investigator-confirmed HAE attacks during 
the treatment period in Part 1 (Day 0 through Day 84) and will be expressed as the normalized 
number of attacks per month (4 weeks) of exposure. The monthly attack rate is calculated for 
each patient as number of HAE attacks occurring during the treatment period divided by the 
number of days the patient contributed to the treatment period multiplied by 28 days. 
The analysis of primary efficacy endpoint will be performed based on the ITT Analysis Set, and 
will compare each PHA-022121 dose group with the placebo group using a generalized linear 
model (GLM) assuming a Poisson distribution with a log link function and Pearson chi-square 
scaling of standard errors to account for potential dispersion. The model will include fixed 
effects for treatment group (categorical), the normalized baseline attack rate (continuous), the 
stratification factor of baseline attack rate(categorical), and an offset term of the logarithm of 
patient’s follow-up time (in days) during the efficacy evaluation period. The estimated least 
squares (LS) mean rate for each PHA-022121 dose group and the mean rate ratios relative to the 
placebo group will be provided with 95% confidence intervals. These estimates will be reported 
as monthly attack rates (mean numbers of events per 4 weeks) by transforming the estimates 
using the exponential function and scaling by the unit of time. The treatment effect in the 
primary efficacy endpoint will be tested using a Wald-based chi-square test. Analysis using a 
negative binomial GLM will be performed as sensitivity analysis. In addition, Wilcoxon two-
sample test will also be conducted comparing each PHA-022121 dose group with the placebo 
group. 
The secondary efficacy endpoints in Part 1 of the study are listed below. The responder 
endpoints (e.g., achieving ≥50% reduction in attack rate relative to baseline during the treatment 
period in Part 1) are derived for subjects with at least 4 weeks of study drug in Part 1. Summary 
statistics on the number or events, event rates, and number and proportion of patients will be 
provided. Similar methods used for the primary efficacy endpoint will be performed for the 
endpoints, if applicable. Fisher’s exact test will be used for testing responder proportions. 
Kaplan-Meier method will be used to analyze the time to first investigator-confirmed HAE 
attack. 
• Number of investigator-confirmed moderate or severe HAE attacks during the treatment 
period in Part 1 (Day 0 to Day 84). 
• Number of investigator-confirmed HAE attacks requiring acute treatment during the 
treatment period in Part 1. 
• Number of patients achieving ≥50% reduction in attack rate relative to baseline during the 
treatment period in Part 1. 
• Number of patients achieving ≥70% reduction in attack rate relative to baseline during the 
treatment period in Part 1. 
• Number of patients achieving ≥90% reduction in attack rate relative to baseline during the 
treatment period in Part 1. 
• Number of patients that are attack-free during the treatment period in Part 1. 
• Number and proportion of days with angioedema symptoms during the treatment period in 
Part 1.
