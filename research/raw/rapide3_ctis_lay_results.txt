WHY WAS THIS STUDY DONE? 
HAE is a rare, genetic condition that causes unpredictable and painful swelling attacks in the 
limbs, face, intestines, and airways. HAE attacks can potentially be life-threatening. Current 
treatment involves on­demand medications to stop attacks, and preventative therapies. 
Deucrictibant is an investigational drug, taken by mouth. Deucrictibant is thought to work by 
stopping opening of a receptor on blood vessels called the bradykinin B2 receptor. The 
bradykinin B2 receptor is like a control valve that can stop or allow fluid to move out of blood 
vessels into the surrounding tissues. By stopping the opening of this receptor, deucrictibant

Researchers are studying whether an on-demand treatment taken by mouth could provide an 
additional option for people with HAE. Currently, deucrictibant is only available in clinical studies. 
It is not approved by any health authorities as a treatment for HAE. The deucrictibant used in 
this study was an “immediate release” pill, also called “IR”. This means that deucrictibant is 
designed to be absorbed into the blood stream quickly after it is taken. The IR pill might work 
well for on-demand usage. 
This study included: 
• Deucrictibant IR (20 mg) or placebo taken by mouth to treat 2 HAE attacks where 
participants did not have breathing difficulties (1 attack treated with deucrictibant IR and 
1 attack treated with placebo). 
 
The main purpose of this study was to see what effect deucrictibant IR had as an 
on-demand treatment for HAE attacks, compared to the effect of a placebo. 
To measure the response to treatment, participants were asked to complete questionnaires 
asking them about their attack symptoms. These questionnaires are called Patient Global 
Impression of Change (PGI-C) and Patient Impression of Severity (PGI-S). These questionnaires 
help to standardize the results participants reported by using a simple scale.

WHAT HAPPENED IN THE STUDY? 
This was a “phase 3” study, where researchers compare the study drug to a standard treatment 
or placebo. This study used a placebo. 
WHO WAS IN THE STUDY? 
Males and females aged 12 to 75 years who had a diagnosis of HAE, with: 
• History of at least 2 HAE attacks in the last 3 months before screening  
• Experience with using standard-of-care drug to effectively manage on-demand 
treatment of HAE attacks 
134 participants were enrolled in the study:

• This type of study is called a “cross over” study, because the treatment that participants 
took changed over during the study. 
• Adolescent participants received a single dose of deucrictibant IR by mouth to test how 
much ended up in the blood stream. They were then assigned to the 2 groups in the 
cross over phase of the study, as above. 
Participants were assigned to the treatment sequence by equal chance. 
• Group 1: 66 people were assigned to get deucrictibant IR then placebo 
• Group 2: 68 people were assigned to get placebo then deucrictibant IR 
During the study: 
• 114 participants received at least 1 dose of deucrictibant IR or placebo 
• 113 participants treated a total of 201 HAE attacks 
o 100 participants treated 1 HAE attack with deucrictibant IR 
o 101 participants treated 1 HAE attack with placebo 
o 88 participants treated 2 HAE attacks, either 1 with deucrictibant IR then 1 with 
placebo, or 1 with placebo then 1 with deucrictibant IR. These were referred to as 
“paired attacks” 
This study was “double-blind”, neither the participants nor the study doctors knew which 
treatment sequence they were assigned to. This was done to make sure the study results were 
not influenced in any way. 
HOW LONG DID THE STUDY LAST? 
Each participant was to be treated for 2 HAE attacks with either deucrictibant IR or placebo for 
the first attack, and then the opposite treatment for the second attack.  
Of the 134 participants who entered the study, 46 left the study before they completed the 
study. This included: 
• 35 participants who left because Pharvaris had collected enough information and stopped 
the study 
• 6 participants who chose to stop taking part 
• 2 participants who could not be contacted 
• 2 participants who did not meet the requirements of the study 
• 1 participant who was randomized but did not receive any study drug.  
88 participants completed the study with 2 paired HAE attacks.

WHAT WERE THE STUDY RESULTS? 
Question 1 
How long did it take participants to feel at least “a little better” for 2 consecutive 
timepoints within 12 hours after treatment during an HAE attack? This was measured 
using the PGI-C scale.  
To study this, researchers calculated the median time to start of symptom relief for participants 
when taking deucrictibant IR or placebo. The median is the middle of a set of numbers when the 
set of numbers is sorted into a list of biggest to smallest or smallest to biggest. It was not 
possible for researchers to calculate this time for participants who took placebo. This is because 
fewer than half of the participants who took placebo reached this level of improvement within 
the study’s observation period (12 hours), and the median can only be calculated when at least 
half of participants reach this target. 
• The median time it took participants to feel at least “a little better” on the PGI-C scale after 
taking deucrictibant IR to treat their attack was 1.28 hours. The median time could not be 
calculated for participants who took placebo.  
❖ Based on these results, researchers concluded that the difference between deucrictibant IR 
and placebo on time to start of symptom relief was unlikely due to chance.  
Question 2a 
What effect did taking deucrictibant IR have compared with taking placebo on the time it 
took for the HAE attack to get a PGI-C rating of at least “better” for 2 consecutive 
timepoints within 12 hours after treatment?

• In this study, the median time it took participants to feel at least “better” on the PGI-C scale 
for 2 consecutive timepoints after taking deucrictibant IR to treat their attack was 2.85 hours. 
As above, the median time could not be calculated for participants who took placebo. 
Question 2b 
What effect did taking deucrictibant IR have compared with taking placebo on the time it 
took to get at least a 1-point reduction in PGI-S rating for 2 consecutive timepoints within 
12 hours after treatment? 
• In this study, the median time it took participants to get symptom relief of at least 1 point on 
the PGI-S scale for 2 consecutive timepoints after taking deucrictibant IR was 2.41 hours. As 
above, the median time could not be calculated for participants who took placebo. 
Question 2c 
What effect did taking deucrictibant IR have compared with taking placebo on the time it 
took for the HAE attack to get a PGI-S rating of “none” (defined as “complete resolution”)? 
• The median time it took participants to get completely better after taking deucrictibant IR 
was 11.95 hours. It was not possible for researchers to calculate this time for participants 
who took placebo.

Question 2d 
What effect did deucrictibant IR have compared with taking placebo on the time it took 
for the HAE attack to stop getting worse (based on PGI-C)? 
• The median time it took participants to stop getting worse after taking deucrictibant IR was 
17.47 minutes. After taking placebo, it took participants a median of 228.67 minutes to stop 
getting worse.  
Researchers concluded that all these differences were unlikely due to chance. Deucrictibant IR 
may improve the symptoms and length of HAE attacks compared with placebo. 
This does not mean that everyone in this study had these results, and individual results could be 
better or worse than the overall group. Other studies may have different results. These are just 
some of the main results of this study. More results may be available at the websites listed at 
the end of this summary. 
DID PARTICIPANTS HAVE SIDE EFFECTS? 
None of the participants had severe or serious side effects considered related to deucrictibant IR 
within 3 days of treatment. 
None of the participants stopped deucrictibant IR treatment due to side effects. 
Two (2) participants experienced a single event of fatigue within 3 days of treatment, one of 
which was considered unrelated to deucrictibant IR treatment. 
Information about other side effects may be available at the websites listed at the end of this 
summary. 
WHERE CAN I LEARN MORE ABOUT THIS STUDY? 
Researchers will use the information from this study and other similar studies to help determine 
if deucrictibant is safe and effective at improving symptoms of HAE attacks and stopping 
progression of HAE attacks. Additional studies with deucrictibant are ongoing.
