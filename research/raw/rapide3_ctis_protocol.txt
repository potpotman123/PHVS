Objectives: 
Primary: 
• To evaluate the efficacy of deucrictibant as on-demand treatment compared with placebo 
on the onset of symptom relief during hereditary angioedema (HAE) attacks  
Secondary: 
• To evaluate the efficacy of deucrictibant as on-demand treatment compared with placebo 
on symptom relief and resolution of HAE attacks 
• To evaluate the safety and tolerability of deucrictibant compared with placebo for on-
demand treatment of HAE attacks 
• To assess the pharmacokinetics (PK) of deucrictibant in adolescent participants (≥12 to 
<18 years) in a non-attack state  
Exploratory: 
• To evaluate participants’ quality of life (QoL) 
Study Endpoints:  
Primary Efficacy Endpoint 
• Time to onset of symptom relief, defined as Patient Global Impression of Change (PGI-C) 
rating of at least “a little better” for 2 consecutive timepoints within 12 hours post-
treatment 
Secondary Efficacy Endpoints 
1. Proportion of study drug-treated attacks achieving PGI-C rating of at least “a little better” 
at 4 hours post-treatment

2. Time to substantial symptom relief, defined as achieving PGI-C rating of at least “better”

• Deucrictibant plasma concentration-time profiles

Exploratory Endpoint 
• Changes in health-related quality of life (QoL) 
Study Design: 
This is a Phase 3, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, 2-period, 2-treatment 
cross-over study to evaluate the efficacy and safety of orally administered deucrictibant for the on-

demand treatment of attacks in participants ≥12 to ≤75 years of age with HAE (type 1, type 2, or type 
3), a proportion of whom are using long-term prophylactic medication for HAE.  
The study consists of a Screening Phase during which eligibility is confirmed, a Treatment Phase in 
which participants will be randomized and receive double-blinded study drug to treat 2 qualified HAE 
attacks (i.e., 2 Treatment Periods within the Treatment Phase) at home, and an End-of-Study Follow-
up Phase after the second study drug-treated attack. In addition, for adolescent participants (age ≥12 to 
<18 years), PK samples are collected after administration of deucrictibant at Day 1 in a non-attack 
state.  
After signing the informed consent form (ICF)/assent form, participants will be screened for eligibility. 
During the Screening Phase, participants will attend an on-site visit to confirm eligibility, to undergo 
screening procedures, and to receive training on the use of the electronic device with the electronic 
patient-reported outcome (ePRO) instruments and the electronic HAE diary. The participants will 
complete an ePRO competency assessment (attack simulation) to ensure their ability to complete 
required ePRO assessments and questionnaires with the electronic device during an actual attack 
during the treatment phase.  
After confirmation of eligibility, participants will be randomized on Day 1 to a crossover treatment 
sequence of double-blinded study drug in a 1:1 ratio (deucrictibant 20 mg for the first attack, placebo 
for the second attack or the reverse order) to treat 2 qualifying attacks, where each attack could be 
either a qualifying non-laryngeal attack or, in adult participants only, a non-severe laryngeal attack not 
associated with breathing difficulties or stridor, during the Treatment Phase. 
Randomization will be stratified according to participant age (≥12 to <18 years, ≥18 years) and use of 
long-term prophylaxis HAE therapy (Yes/No). The study population will include approximately 30% 
of participants who are using long-term prophylactic medication for HAE globally; in the United States 
(including Puerto Rico), enrollment is targeted to be approximately 50% of participants using long-
term prophylactic medication. 
Refer to Section 6.1.2.3.1 for details regarding attack qualification by the investigator (or designee) 
and treatment, based on attack type. 
After the participant self-administers study drug for the first qualifying HAE attack, they will have 
either an on-site or remote Post-Attack Visit ≥48 hours to ≤10 days after study drug administration for 
evaluation and recording of adverse events (AEs) and concomitant medication use.  
For study drug-treated non-laryngeal attacks, a second dose of study drug is permitted ≥4 hours post 
first dose in case symptoms are persisting or progressing after the first study drug dose. If at ≥1 hour 
after the second study drug dose, symptoms still persist or progress, then HAE rescue medication may 
be administered. Note that, if needed for participant safety, participants may self-administer HAE 
rescue medication at any time. The time of administration of study drug as well as HAE rescue 
medication must be recorded in the electronic device. 
For study drug-treated non-severe laryngeal attacks (in adults only), the participant may self-
administer rescue medication at any time, if needed. A second dose of study drug should not be 
administered for non-severe laryngeal attacks. 
Laryngeal attacks, regardless of severity, in adolescent participants or severe laryngeal attacks, 
including those associated with breathing difficulties or stridor, in adult participants should not be 
treated with study drug, and should be treated immediately with HAE stand-of-care medication and 
medical care must be sought urgently. 
After the participant self-administers study drug for the second qualifying HAE attack, an on-site End-
of-Study Visit is to occur at 10 (±5) days following attack resolution.

Qualitative interviews regarding experience with HAE standard-of-care medication (including double-

Participants who complete the current study will have an opportunity for continued access to 
deucrictibant in an open-label extension study, if inclusion and exclusion criteria are met.

The study will enroll approximately 120 adult and adolescent participants (≥12 to ≤75 years of age) 
with HAE. The study will target enrollment of approximately 9 adolescent participants (≥12 to

The study is planned to be conducted at study sites in approximately 30 countries.

Exclusion Criteria: 
Participants who meet any of the following criteria will be excluded from the study. 
1. Any female who is pregnant, plans to become pregnant, or is breastfeeding.  
2. Any diagnosis of angioedema other than HAE. 
3. Any clinically significant comorbidity or systemic dysfunction (e.g., cardiovascular, 
gastrointestinal, renal, neurological, respiratory) that, in the opinion of the Investigator, would 
interfere with the participant’s safety or ability to participate in the study.  
4. Use of attenuated androgens for short-term prophylaxis within 2 weeks before screening. 
5. Abnormal hepatic function (aspartate aminotransferase [AST] >2×upper limit of normal 
[ULN], alanine aminotransferase [ALT] >2×ULN, or total bilirubin >1.5×ULN, or Child-Pugh 
class B or C). Participants with Gilbert’s syndrome, being defined as an isolated increase in 
total bilirubin ≤3×ULN, with AST and ALT in the normal range, will not be excluded.  
6. Abnormal renal function (estimated glomerular filtration rate <60 mL/min/1.73 m2). 
7. History of alcohol or drug abuse within the previous year, or current evidence of substance 
dependence or abuse. 
8. Has received prior on-demand HAE treatment with deucrictibant. 
9. Currently participating in any other investigational drug study or receiving other 
investigational treatment within the last 30 days, or within 5 half-lives (whichever is longer) of 
the time of randomization. 
10. Prior gene therapy for any indication at any time. 
11. Use of concomitant medications with systemic absorption that are strong inhibitors of 
CYP3A4 (such as clarithromycin, itraconazole, ketoconazole, and ritonavir) or strong inducers 
of CYP3A4 (such as carbamazepine and phenytoin) within the last 30 days or within 5 half-
lives (whichever is longer) of the time of randomization. 
12. Known hypersensitivity to deucrictibant or any of the excipients of study drug. 
Test therapy, dosage, and mode of administration: 
Deucrictibant is the active ingredient, previously referred to as PHA121 or PHA-022121.  
The study drug consists of deucrictibant (20 mg strength) and matching placebo for oral use. 
A study drug-treated attack refers to an attack that has been treated with orally administered 
deucrictibant 20 mg or matching placebo in a double-blinded fashion. 
Timing of Treatment:

Adolescents only (≥12 to <18 years) will receive deucrictibant (20 mg) in a non-attack state for PK 
assessment on Day 1 at the study site.

their randomized crossover treatment sequence for use during the Treatment Phase. Participants will 
self-administer oral doses of study drug: deucrictibant for the first qualifying attack followed by 
placebo for the second qualifying attack or the reverse order.

Criteria for evaluation: 
Efficacy: 
Efficacy will be assessed with the following Patient Reported Outcomes (PROs): 
• PGI-C (7-point scale) is used to evaluate the change in the HAE attack symptoms as 
compared to pre-treatment. 
• PGI-S (5-point scale) is used to evaluate the severity of HAE attack symptoms. 
• AMRA is used to evaluate the severity of HAE attack symptoms.  
− For non-laryngeal attacks, AMRA-3 is used for participants to rate from ‘0’ (No 
swelling/No pain) to ‘100’ (Worst possible swelling/Worst possible pain) for the 
severity of angioedema symptoms during an HAE attack. For abdominal or peripheral 
attacks, the AMRA-3 composite score is calculated as the mean of the single item 
scores for each of the 3 symptoms: skin pain, skin swelling, and abdominal pain. 
− For non-severe laryngeal attacks, the AMRA-5 is used for participants to rate the 
5 major HAE symptoms between ‘0’ (No symptom) and ‘100’ (Worst symptom) for 
the severity of each of 5 angioedema symptoms during an HAE attack. The AMRA-5 
score is calculated as the mean of the single item scores for each of the 5 symptoms: 
skin pain, skin swelling, abdominal pain, voice change, and difficulty swallowing.

subset of participants.

Statistical Methods: 
The general information of study participants and baseline characteristics will be summarized based on 
all enrolled participants. All efficacy endpoints will be evaluated using the Primary Efficacy Analysis 
Set (PEAS, which consists of all randomized participants who had an attack treated with study drug in 
each of the two attack periods), unless otherwise specified. Supportive analyses will be performed 
using the Supportive Efficacy Analysis Set (SEAS, defined as all randomized participants who had at 
least one study drug treated attack) for selected endpoints, which will be detailed in the full Statistical 
Analysis Plan (SAP). Safety analysis will be based on the Safety Analysis Set. 
For the primary endpoint, the estimated median time to onset of symptom relief, defined as PGI-C 
rating of at least “a little better” for 2 consecutive timepoints within 12 hours post-treatment, will be 
summarized for each treatment group using Kaplan-Meier (KM) method. 
The intercurrent event (ICE) is the intake of rescue medication or prohibited medication after the attack 
is treated with study drug. Composite variable strategy will be used to address the ICE for the primary 
endpoint. The occurrence of ICE will be regarded as a treatment failure. Namely, if rescue medication 
or prohibited medication was taken within 12 hours post-treatment, the time to onset of symptom relief 
will be censored at the maximum time of consideration (i.e., 13 h, which is the upper end of the 12-
hour time window in the electronic device for participant to record data at that nominal timepoint), 
regardless whether PGI-C reached the rating of at least “a little better”. 
For the primary endpoint,  will be used to test the 
null hypotheses that there is no difference between the deucrictibant and placebo treatments. 
Supportive analysis will be performed on SEAS using marginal Cox proportional hazards model 
(CPHM) with a robust variance-covariance estimator to account for the within-participant correlation. 
The model will include treatment (deucrictibant 20 mg or placebo), attack (attack 1 or attack 2), 
sequence (deucrictibant 20 mg/placebo or placebo/deucrictibant 20 mg), and randomization 
stratification factors as independent variables. The estimated hazard ratio between deucrictibant 20 mg 
and placebo will be provided with 95% confidence interval (CI) and p-value.  
As supplementary analyses, the above analyses will be performed using a treatment policy strategy, in 
which the data from all efficacy assessments through 12 hours post-treatment, including those 
collected after intake of rescue medication or prohibited medication, will be used for the analysis of the 
primary endpoint. 
For all secondary efficacy endpoints including time-to-event endpoints and binary endpoints, 
estimands will be defined in a similar way as for the primary endpoint, composite variable strategy will 
be consistently used to handle ICEs in all secondary efficacy endpoints.

4. INTRODUCTION 
4.1. Hereditary Angioedema 
Hereditary angioedema (HAE) is a rare disease that manifests as unpredictable and recurrent 
edema attacks of subcutaneous or submucosal tissue most commonly affecting the limbs, 
intestinal tract, face (lips and tongue), genitals, and airways. HAE attacks often lead to 
discomfort and pain, and can include nausea, vomiting and diarrhea when the intestinal tract is 
affected. HAE attacks can become life-threatening in the case of airway obstruction and 
asphyxiation if the attack remains untreated. The number, duration, and severity of attacks vary 
highly between patients and throughout a patient’s lifetime. Trauma, stress, and medical 
procedures are known triggers for attacks. 
Three types of HAE have been characterized. HAE type 1 and type 2 (HAE type 1/2) are defined 
by mutations in the C1-INH gene and result either in reduced levels (type 1) or reduced activity 
(type 2) of C1-INH. In both cases, decreased C1-INH protease activity leads to uncontrolled 
activity of Factor XII and plasma kallikrein, resulting in an activated kallikrein-kinin system, i.e., 
excessive cleavage of high molecular weight kininogen and increased plasma levels of 
bradykinin (BK). HAE with normal C1-INH (HAE type 3) is characterized by similar clinical 
manifestations to HAE type 1/2 in the presence of normal levels and activity of C1-INH and, in a 
number of cases, is associated with characterized mutations in other genes  as listed in the HAEA 
and WAO/EAACI Guidelines (Appendix 1). Laboratory tests that measure bradykinin levels in 
blood and stimulated kallikrein activity are available to aid identification of patients with HAE 
type 3 (Li 2023; Lara-Marquez 2018).  
BK is the principal mediator of the signs and symptoms associated with HAE attacks. Two BK 
receptor types, bradykinin B1 and bradykinin B2, have been identified, cloned, and 
characterized. BK has a high affinity and high agonist potency at the bradykinin B2 receptor, but 
a near 1000-fold lower affinity and potency at the B1 receptor, making it a selective bradykinin 
B2 receptor agonist. 
The bradykinin B2 receptor is expressed on the cell surface of the luminal side of vascular 
endothelial cells. Excessive BK generation, as in HAE, promotes vascular permeability, leading 
to plasma extravasation and subcutaneous or submucosal tissue edema and swelling typical of an 
angioedema attack. 
4.2. Current Treatment Options for Hereditary Angioedema 
There are currently 2 treatment approaches to the management of HAE: acute (on-demand) 
treatment of attacks and prevention of attacks with short- or long-term prophylactic therapy. 
Patients and their physicians have several choices for on-demand or prophylactic treatment, but 
because there are differences in the route of administration, side effect profiles, and efficacy 
between medications, patient preference and experience are considered in selecting the most 
appropriate therapy. 
Patients must have access to a readily available on-demand HAE standard-of-care medication 
that can be self-administered. Treatment should be taken regardless of the location of the attack, 
and treatment should be initiated early to mitigate progression of the attack. Regular monitoring 
of the patient’s frequency of use of on-demand treatment and treatment outcomes should be

conducted (Busse 2021, Maurer 2022). In addition to on-demand treatment of HAE attacks, a 
patient may also require prophylactic treatment. 
The requirement for long-term prophylactic treatment is not made on rigid criteria, but rather the 
needs of the individual patient (Busse 2021, Maurer 2022). The decision to initiate long-term 
prophylaxis is made with consideration for the patient’s quality of life and treatment preferences 
in the context of attack frequency, attack severity, comorbid conditions, and access to treatment 
(Busse 2021, Maurer 2022). Because disease severity may change over time, the need to initiate 
or continue long-term prophylactic treatment is periodically reviewed. 
4.2.1. On-demand Treatments 
The bradykinin B2 receptor antagonist icatibant (Firazyr
®) (Firazyr® prescribing information, 
2021) is the mainstay of acute treatment due to its quick onset of action and subcutaneous (sc) 
self-administration with prefilled syringe. The kallikrein inhibitor ecallantide (Kalbitor®) 
requires hospital or health professional sc administration due to potential anaphylaxis. Plasma-
derived (Berinert® 500/1,500, Cinryze®) or recombinant (Ruconest®) C1-INH concentrates 
require intravenous (iv) administration. 
4.2.2. Prophylactic Treatments 
The plasma-derived C1-INH concentrates (Cinryze and Haegarda®/Berinert® 2,000/3,000) are 
administered by iv and sc routes, respectively. These products are administered twice weekly 
(every 3 or 4 days). Plasma kallikrein inhibitors include lanadelumab (Takhzyro®), a fully human 
recombinant monoclonal antibody, which is administered sc every 2 or 4 weeks, and berotralstat 
(Orladeyo®), which is administered orally, once daily. Attenuated androgens (including danazol, 
stanozolol, oxandrolone, oxymetholone, tibolone, methyltestosterone) and antifibrinolytics, such 
as ɛ-aminocaproic acid and tranexamic acid, are used for the prevention of acute attacks and are 
licensed in some countries. However, current treatment guidelines recommend against the use of 
attenuated androgens and antifibrinolytics as first-line therapy due to limited efficacy and/or 
safety issues with associated contraindications (Busse 2021, Maurer 2022). 
4.3. Unmet Medical Needs in Managing Bradykinin-Mediated 
Angioedema 
There is a high unmet medical need for efficacious oral medicines that can facilitate timely 
treatment of attacks due to HAE and other various forms of bradykinin-mediated angioedema 
and thereby improve outcomes, compliance, and the overall care and quality of life for patients. 
All currently available approved medications for on-demand treatment of HAE are administered 
parenterally via iv or sc routes, which are often associated with injection pain, local side effects, 
a delay of drug administration, and a lower quality of life. In a 2017 Food and Drug 
Administration (FDA) Patient-Focused Drug Development meeting, patients expressed that the 
route of administration (iv, sc, or oral) is the most important consideration for a new HAE 
therapeutic, with a preference for oral treatments (FDA Report of Public Meeting 2018).  
4.4. Deucrictibant 
Deucrictibant is under development as an orally administered BK B2 receptor antagonist with 
potential for both acute therapeutic and prophylactic use in patients with different types of BK-

Deucrictibant has been extensively characterized in primary pharmacological studies as a potent,

Deucrictibant had no adverse effects on male or female fertility or embryofetal development

adults with HAE (PHA022121-C303), deucrictibant has been shown to be generally well 
tolerated. Most treatment-emergent AEs reported have been mild to moderate in severity. No 
severe treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) or serious adverse events (SAEs) have been 
considered by the investigator as related to deucrictibant. No serious adverse reactions or deaths 
have been reported.  
In Study PHA022121-C201, single-dose PK was characterized and participants treated up to 
3 qualifying HAE attacks with blinded study drug. Efficacy data from the completed primary 
analysis of the HAE attack period showed that deucrictibant 10, 20, and 30 mg, versus placebo,

vital signs, or electrocardiogram (ECG) parameters. These data support further development of 
deucrictibant as an on-demand treatment for patients with HAE. The results of the full analysis

4.4.1. Benefit-Risk with Deucrictibant 
Overall, deucrictibant has been generally well-tolerated in the completed and ongoing clinical 
studies. The majority of TEAEs reported as of the safety data cutoff date have been mild/Grade 1 
or moderate/Grade 2 in severity and have resolved. TEAEs in assigned deucrictibant treatment 
group reported by ≥2 participants with frequency ≥5% from studies completed or with 
predefined primary data analysis have included headache, dizziness, nasopharyngitis, gamma 
glutamyl transferase increased, urinary tract infection, and diarrhea. Similar events were also 
reported in participants receiving placebo. No serious adverse reactions (i.e., study drug-related 
SAEs) or TEAEs with an outcome of death have been identified thus far. Independent Data 
Monitoring Committees have been established to review the unblinded safety data from all of the 
studies on a regular basis and ensure the safety of participants. Safety monitoring rules have been 
defined in study protocols to mitigate the risks for participants in clinical studies of deucrictibant. 
In the Phase 2 double-blind, placebo controlled and randomized on-demand Study 
PHA022121-C201 in patients with HAE, deucrictibant demonstrated robust efficacy measured 
by rapid onset of attack symptom relief, substantial reduction of attack symptoms, early attack 
symptom resolution, and markedly reduced use of rescue medication compared to placebo.  
Given the potential benefit to treat the HAE population, especially with the validated mechanism 
of action, positive pharmacodynamic and clinical effects demonstrated by the BK challenge in 
Study PHA022121-C001 and on-demand treatment of HAE attacks in Study PHA022121-C201, 
the benefit-risk assessment of deucrictibant is considered acceptable if used according to the 
clinical study protocol, and in accordance with the recommendations and guidance provided in 
the Investigator’s Brochure.  
Overall, the benefit-risk assessment appears to be favorable to support the clinical investigation 
of deucrictibant for on-demand treatment of HAE.

6. INVESTIGATIONAL PLAN 
6.1. Overall Study Design 
This is a Phase 3, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, 2-period, 
2-treatment cross-over study to evaluate the efficacy and safety of orally administered 
deucrictibant for the on-demand treatment of attacks in participants ≥12 to ≤75 years of age with 
HAE (type 1, type 2, or type 3), a proportion of whom are using long-term prophylactic 
medication for HAE.  
The study consists of a Screening Phase during which eligibility is confirmed, a Treatment Phase 
in which participants will be randomized and receive double-blinded study drug to treat 
2 qualified HAE attacks (i.e., 2 Treatment Periods within the Treatment Phase) at home, and an 
End-of-Study Follow-up Phase after the second study drug-treated attack. In addition, for 
adolescent participants (age ≥12 to <18 years), PK samples are collected after administration of 
deucrictibant at Day 1 in a non-attack state.  
After signing the informed consent form (ICF)/assent form, participants will be screened for

6.2. Rationale for Study Design and Control Group 
In this study, a placebo control is utilized, and randomization will be used to assign participants 
to a treatment sequence (active then placebo, or placebo then active). Each participant will 
receive both active and placebo treatment in a crossover fashion to treat 2 separate qualifying 
attacks; a washout of at least 72 hours must elapse between taking study drug or any other HAE 
medication (standard or rescue medication) for the attack to qualify for study drug administration 
to avoid the possibility of any carry-over effect. To best represent a scenario closer to real-world 
clinical practice, a heterogeneous population will be included in this study (adolescents ≥12 to 
<18 years of age, adults [≥18 years of age], participants not using long-term HAE prophylaxis 
and participants using long-term HAE prophylaxis, and multinational locations with different 
disease prevalence, including by gender, and standard-of-care). Therefore, a crossover design 
was chosen for this study to minimize inter-participant variability as each participant serves as 
their own control. 
Retreatment of a non-laryngeal attack with deucrictibant in the absence of sufficient symptom 
relief (i.e., worsening, partial response, or recurrence of symptoms) at least 4 hours after the first 
dose is supported by clinical PK and efficacy data. If there is no symptom relief or if symptoms 
recur after the second dose of study drug, HAE rescue medication can be used. 
Inclusion of adolescents in this Phase 3 study is justified because safety and efficacy data from 
the Phase 2 (PHA022121-C201) study were robust and support the initiation of the pediatric 
development of deucrictibant. In the study, all primary and key secondary efficacy endpoints 
were met, and deucrictibant was demonstrated to be well-tolerated. The majority of TEAEs 
reported was mild or moderate in severity, with no treatment-related SAEs, severe AEs, or AEs 
leading to treatment discontinuation being reported. Some adolescents experience HAE attacks at 
a frequency that is similar to that for adults, and they have body mass indexes approaching those 
of adults. Therefore, it is proper to include adolescents in this study. Additionally, inclusion of 
adolescents also is supported by population PK analyses conducted by the Sponsor. HAE attacks 
in younger children (<12 years) are rare and will be evaluated in a separate study.

8.4. Randomization and Blinding 
Adolescents only (≥12 to <18 years): Each adolescent participant will be provided a single, oral 
dose of deucrictibant (20 mg) for administration for PK assessment during the Day 1 Visit. 
Adults and adolescents: Randomization to a crossover treatment sequence of blinded study drug 
(active-placebo or placebo-active) will be done via an interactive response technology (IRT) 
system. Randomization will be stratified by age group (≥12 to <18 years, ≥18 years) and by use 
of HAE medication for long-term prophylaxis (Yes/No).  
The study population will include approximately 30% of participants who are using long-term 
prophylactic medication for HAE globally; in the United States (including Puerto Rico), 
enrollment is targeted to be approximately 50% of participants using long-term prophylactic 
medication. 
This target proportion of participants using long-term prophylactic medication is intended to 
increase an opportunity to assess on-demand treatment with deucrictibant in participants with 
severe attacks. Severe attacks, including laryngeal attacks, appear to be observed more 
frequently in patients who are not receiving long-term prophylactic therapy compared to those 
receiving such therapy, as suggested by global HAE experts consulted in the design of this study, 
and observed in the Phase 2 study with deucrictibant for prophylactic treatment (Study 
PHA022121-C301). In that study, during deucrictibant prophylactic treatment, all breakthrough 
attacks were mild or moderate and there were no severe attacks reported. In addition, the global 
proportion of 30% for participants with long-term prophylactic therapy for enrollment is aligned 
with the enrollment status from a recently completed global Phase 3 oral on-demand treatment 
study, in which approximately 22% of participants enrolled were receiving long-term 
prophylactic therapy (Riedl, 2024). The higher proportion of approximately 50% in the United 
States reflects the fact that there are a relatively greater number of patients with HAE receiving 
long-term prophylactic therapy in the United States than in the rest of world due to variable 
availability of such medications. 
The packaging and labeling are blinded.

11.11. Adverse and Serious Adverse Events 
11.11.1. Definition of Adverse Events 
11.11.1.1. Adverse Event 
An adverse event (AE) is defined as any untoward medical occurrence in a patient or clinical 
investigation participant administered a pharmaceutical product and which does not necessarily 
have to have a causal relationship with this treatment. An AE can therefore be any unfavorable 
and unintended sign, symptom, or disease temporally associated with the use of a medicinal 
product, whether or not considered related to the medicinal product. 
Baseline medical history/conditions, abnormal physical examination findings or 
diagnostic/laboratory values present at the screening visit, are not considered to be AEs, and 
should be recorded in the eCRF as baseline medical history. Events with a start date after the 
signing of the ICF are considered AEs, and any worsening in severity, or an increase in 
frequency, or change of concomitant medication for management of a pre-existing baseline 
medical condition is considered to be an AE. For the purpose of this protocol, HAE attacks and 
their associated symptoms will not be defined as AEs, unless they meet the criteria for an SAE. 
Prescheduled hospitalizations for non-emergency procedures will not be considered SAEs. 
11.11.1.2. Serious Adverse Event  
An SAE or reaction is any untoward medical occurrence that at any dose: 
• Results in death 
• Is life-threatening (at the time of the event)

• Requires inpatient hospitalization or prolongation of an existing hospitalization (note: 
a pre-existing condition that requires planned hospitalization is not considered as

11.11.3.2. Severity 
The National Cancer Institute (NCI) CTCAE version 5.0 (NCI 2017) is a descriptive terminology 
which can be utilized for event/AE reporting. Adverse events (also referred to as "events") are 
classified by CTCAE grade/severity.  
The CTCAE incorporates certain elements of the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities 
(MedDRA) terminology. The System Organ Class (SOC), is the highest level of the MedDRA 
hierarchy, is identified by anatomical or physiological system, etiology, or purpose (e.g., the 
SOC called "Investigations" is used for laboratory test results). An AE is a term that is a unique 
representation of a specific event used for medical documentation and scientific analyses. Each 
CTCAE term is a MedDRA lowest level term. 
Per NCI CTCAE, grade refers to the severity of the event. The CTCAE grade/severity should be 
evaluated and recorded on the eCRF according to the NCI CTCAE, version 5.0 (NCI 2017). 
If an AE description is not graded according to the CTCAE grading system, the Investigator will 
assess the severity of AEs based on the following CTCAE grading definitions:  
• Grade 1: Mild; asymptomatic or mild symptoms; clinical or diagnostic observations 
only; intervention not indicated 
• Grade 2: Moderate; minimal, local or noninvasive intervention indicated; limiting 
age- appropriate instrumental activities of daily living (ADL)*. 
• Grade 3: Severe or medically significant but not immediately life-threatening; 
hospitalization or prolongation of hospitalization indicated; disabling; limiting self-
care ADL**. 
• Grade 4: Life-threatening consequences; urgent intervention indicated 
• Grade 5: Death related to AE 
− Not all Grades are appropriate for all AEs. Therefore, some AEs are listed with 
fewer than five options for Grade selection. 
* Instrumental ADL refers to preparing meals, shopping for groceries or clothes, using 
the telephone, managing money, etc. 
** Self-care ADL refers to bathing, dressing, and undressing, feeding self, using the 
toilet, taking medications, and not bedridden.

12.2. Determination of Sample Size 
The primary endpoint of the study is time to onset of symptom relief, defined as PGI-C rating of 
at least “a little better” for 2 consecutive timepoints within 12 hours post-treatment.  
 
 
, a total of 60 participants in the Primary Efficacy Analysis Set

12.4. Efficacy Estimands and Analyses 
12.4.1. Primary Efficacy Estimand and Analysis 
The targeted treatment regimen to be used for this study is deucrictibant (20 mg strength) or 
placebo for oral use in treating HAE attacks. The primary estimand on efficacy of deucrictibant 
in treating HAE attacks is for the patient population as defined through the study inclusion and 
exclusion criteria and currently having an HAE attack, with the primary endpoint as time to onset 
of symptom relief, defined as PGI-C rating of at least “a little better” for 2 consecutive 
timepoints within 12 hours post-treatment. The Kaplan-Meier (KM) estimated median and 
percentiles of time to onset of symptom relief by PGI-C will be provided along with a KM plot 
for each treatment group. 
The intercurrent event (ICE) is the intake of rescue medication or prohibited medication after the 
attack is treated with study drug. Composite variable strategy will be used to address the ICE in 
the primary estimand. The occurrence of ICE will be regarded as a treatment failure. Namely, if 
rescue medication or prohibited medication was taken within 12 hours post-treatment, the time to 
onset of symptom relief will be censored at the maximum time of consideration (i.e., 13 hours, 
which is the upper end of the 12-hour time window in the electronic device for participant to 
record data at that nominal timepoint), regardless whether PGI-C reached the rating of at least "a 
little better".  
For the primary endpoint of time to onset of symptom relief by PGI-C (defined as PGI-C rating 
of at least “a little better” for 2 consecutive timepoints within 12 hours post-treatment), the 
 test will be used to test the null hypotheses that there is no 
difference in time to onset of symptom relief by PGI-C between the treatment with deucrictibant 
and with placebo. Supportive analysis will be performed using a marginal Cox proportional 
hazards model (CPHM) with a robust variance-covariance estimator to account for the within-
subject correlation. The model will include treatment (deucrictibant or placebo), attack period 
(attack 1 or attack 2), and sequence (deucrictibant/placebo or placebo/deucrictibant) as 
independent variables. The estimated hazard ratio between deucrictibant and placebo will be 
provided with 95% confidence interval (CI) and p-value.  
As supplementary analyses, the above analyses will be performed using a treatment policy 
strategy, in which the data from all efficacy assessments through 12 hours post-treatment, 
including those collected after intake of rescue medication or prohibited medication, will be used 
for analysis on the primary endpoint. 
When appropriate, supportive analyses may be performed on the primary endpoint and other 
selected efficacy endpoints using the Supportive Efficacy Analysis Set based on subgroups by 
pre-treatment attack characteristics such as attack location, severity, and timing of study drug 
administration.

12.4.2. Secondary Efficacy Estimands and Analyses 
The secondary endpoints in fixed order of testing are presented in Section 5. 
For all secondary efficacy endpoints including time-to-event endpoints and binary endpoints, 
estimands will be defined in a similar way as the primary endpoint, composite variable strategy 
will be consistently used to handle ICEs for all secondary efficacy endpoints. 
For binary secondary endpoints, i.e., secondary efficacy endpoints related to proportions, 
composite variable strategy will also be used to address ICEs as follows:  
• For the secondary endpoint of proportion of study drug-treated attacks achieving a 
PGI-C rating of at least “a little better” at 4 hours post-treatment, the ICE is again the 
intake of rescue medication or prohibited medication. Following the composite 
variable strategy, the ICE is regarded as an unfavorable outcome, that is, the attacks 
with rescue medication use or prohibited medication use within 4 hours will be 
regarded as not achieving symptom relief at 4 hours post-treatment (i.e., an outcome 
of "No" in the binary outcome variable) regardless of whether PGI-C achieved “a 
little better”. The proportion and 95% CI will be provided for each treatment group 
together with the p-value from Prescott test (Prescott 1981). Other binary endpoints 
characterizing attack symptom relief and symptom resolution will the handled 
similarly. 
• For the secondary endpoint of proportion of study drug-treated attacks requiring 
rescue medication within 24 hours post-treatment, the ICE is the intake of prohibited 
medication. Following the composite variable strategy, the attacks with intake of 
prohibited medication within 24 hours post-treatment will be regarded as an 
unfavorable outcome and counted in the number of attacks requiring rescue 
medication at 24 hours. The proportion and 95% CI will be provided for each 
treatment group together with the p-value from Prescott test. 
• For binary secondary efficacy endpoints, supplementary analyses will be provided 
using the Supportive Efficacy Analysis Set and based on the generalized estimating 
equation (GEE) with a logit link. The model will include treatment (deucrictibant or 
placebo), attack (attack 1 or attack 2), and sequence (deucrictibant /placebo or 
placebo/deucrictibant) as independent variables, and an identity matrix as the working 
correlation matrix of the robust empirical estimates of error (Jones, 2014). The 
estimated odds ratio between deucrictibant and placebo will be provided with the 
95% CI and p-value.

APPENDIX 2. SUMMARY OF CHANGES 
Amendment 1.2 (dated 03 July 2024) was amended to Amendment 4.1 (dated 17 January 2025).  
The primary reasons for the amendment were to allow enrollment of participants with HAE type 
3. This change is intended to help address an unmet medical need for the development of an 
effective oral treatment for this type of HAE. Also, based on European Medicines Agency 
Paediatric Committee feedback, it was stipulated that study drug treatment of qualifying non-
severe laryngeal attacks will be permitted in adult participants only.  
Substantive changes to the protocol included the following:  
• The text was updated to indicate that the study will include participants with HAE 
type 3 (Section 6.1 Overall Study Design, Section 7 Selection and Withdrawal of 
Participants). 
• The study inclusion/exclusion criteria were revised to allow enrollment of participants 
with HAE type 3. 
− Inclusion criterion 3b (Section 7.1 Participant Inclusion Criteria) 
− Exclusion criterion 2 (Section 7.2 Participant Inclusion Criteria) 
• HAEA and WAO/EEACI Guidelines were cited to support diagnosis of HAE type 3 
(Section 4.1 Hereditary Angioedema, Section 7.1 Participant Inclusion Criteria, 
Appendix 1 HAEA and WAO/EAACI Guidelines). 
• The list of references was updated to include sources included in the HAEA and 
WAO/EAACI Guidelines (Section 18 List of References). 
• The protocol text and list of references were updated to cite scientific articles 
describing laboratory tests that can be used to support identification of HAE type 3 
patients (Section 4.1 Hereditary Angioedema, Section 18 Section 4.1 List of 
References).  
• The text was revised to indicate that non-severe laryngeal attacks qualify for study 
drug treatment in adult participants only (Section 6.1 Overall Study Design, 
Section 6.1.2.3.1 Attack Qualification and Treatment, based on Attack Type, 
Section 6.1.2.3.1.2 Laryngeal Attacks in Adolescents or Laryngeal Attacks in Adults 
with Breathing Difficulties or Stridor, Section 6.1.2.3.1.3 Non-Severe Laryngeal 
Attacks without Breathing Difficulties or Stridor (in Adults Only).

the study schedule of events and study activities that for participants with HAE type 
3, diagnostic testing results are to confirm C1-INH function ≥50% of normal and C4 
level not below lower level of normal range performed by the central laboratory 
(Section 6.1.1 Schedule of Events Table 4 and Table 4 Footnote h, Section 6.1.2.1 
Screening Phase (Days -28 to -1).
