1 INTRODUCTION 
The purpose of this Statistical Analysis Plan (SAP) is to provide a description of the 
statistical methods to be implemented for the Primary Analysis (PA) using data from the 
double-blind treatment period (Part 1 data) up through protocol PHA022121-C301 version 7, 
dated 03 May, 2023. Therefore, this SAP is referred to as the Part 1 data SAP. It will be 
finalized prior to the PA database lock. Analysis related to Part 2 data or combined datasets 
of Part 1 and Part 2 will be described in the Final Analysis (FA) SAP. Any deviations from 
this SAP after PA database lock and release of treatment code will be documented in the final 
Clinical Study Report (CSR).

2 STUDY OVERVIEW 
2.1 Study Objectives and Endpoints 
The objectives and endpoints are separately described by Part 1 and Part 2 because the study 
consists of 2 parts, with participants completing Part 1 prior to initiation of treatment in Part 
2. Part 1 is a double-blind, placebo-controlled, 12-week evaluation of the efficacy and safety 
of 2 dose regimens of PHA-022121. Part 2 is an open-label evaluation of the long-term safety 
and efficacy of a single dose regimen of PHA-022121. Further details are outlined in Section 
2.2. Part 2 analysis is not covered in this SAP. 
2.1.1  The Primary Objectives 
Part 1 
x To evaluate the safety and tolerability of two dose regimens of PHA-022121 
administered as long-term prophylaxis against HAE attacks. 
x To evaluate the efficacy of two dose regimens PHA-022121 administered as long-term 
prophylaxis against HAE attacks. 
 
Part 2 
x To evaluate the safety of PHA-022121 administered as long-term prophylactic HAE 
treatment. 
 
2.1.2 The Secondary Objectives 
Part 1 
x To characterize the pharmacokinetics/pharmacodynamics (PK/PD) of two dose regimens 
of PHA-022121 administered as long-term prophylaxis against HAE attacks. 
x To evaluate the impact on health-related quality of life (QoL) of two dose regimens of 
PHA-022121 administered as long-term prophylaxis against HAE attacks. 
 
Part 2 
x To evaluate the efficacy of PHA-022121 administered as long-term prophylactic HAE 
treatment. 
x To evaluate the PK/PD of PHA-022121 administered as long-term prophylactic HAE 
treatment.  
x To evaluate the impact on health-related quality of life (QoL) of PHA-022121 
administered as long-term prophylactic HAE treatment.

2.2 Study Design 
2.2.1    Overview 
x This is a Phase II, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, dose-
ranging, parallel group, 2-part study to evaluate the safety and efficacy of PHA-022121 
soft capsules (PHVS416) administered orally for long-term prophylaxis against 
angioedema attacks in participants with HAE due to C1-inhibitor (C1-INH) deficiency 
(type I or type II) (see study design schematic in Figure 1). 
The study consists of 2 parts, with participants completing Part 1 prior to initiation of 
treatment in Part 2. Part 1 is a double-blind, placebo-controlled, 12-week evaluation of 
the efficacy and safety of 2 dose regimens of PHA-022121. Part 2 is an open-label 
evaluation of the long-term safety and efficacy of a single dose regimen of PHA-022121. 
The duration of the treatment period in Part 2 is 30 months and may be extended until 
another clinical study is available for participants to continue open-label treatment with 
PHA-022121, provided that the safety and efficacy data from the ongoing studies support 
continuing the use of PHA-022121. If the treatment period is extended, revisions will be 
addressed via a protocol amendment. Such an extension will impact Part 2 analysis but 
not Part 1. Any participant who withdraws consent or is discontinued from the study at 
any time will complete an end-of-study visit approximately 4 weeks after the last 
treatment administration. 
x Following signing of informed consent, participants will undergo screening assessments. 
The screening period may last up to 8 weeks. 
x For Part 1, eligible participants will be randomized in a 1:1:1 ratio to receive placebo or 
one of two dose regimens of PHA-022121 (10 mg or 20 mg twice daily [BID]). Each 
participant will receive blinded investigational medicinal product (IMP) (i.e., PHA-
022121 or placebo) to be administered orally BID after breakfast and dinner with 12 ±2 
hours between doses for a duration of 12 weeks of treatment. 
x For Part 2, participants will administer open-label PHA-022121 20 mg BID after 
breakfast and dinner for a duration of 30 months. To be eligible for continued treatment 
in Part 2, the participant must have completed Part 1, unless discontinued from Part 1 due 
to regulatory actions. If there is a gap in treatment between Parts 1 and 2, Visit 7 safety 
assessments are to be performed, with results reviewed by the Investigator, and 
participant eligibility reconfirmed before the participant may restart treatment in Part 2. 
x Throughout the study (both Part 1 and Part 2), an electronic patient-reported outcomes 
(ePRO) HAE diary (i.e., ePRO HAE diary) will be used daily to record any symptoms 
and details of HAE attacks, including relevant treatments and questionnaires for QoL 
endpoints (see 4.7).  Participants will have access to standard of care on-demand acute 
therapies and will be able to treat any HAE attack that may occur using such therapies in 
accordance with World Allergy Organization (WAO)/European Academy of Allergy and 
Clinical Immunology (EAACI) guidelines and local regulations. In addition to the 
collection information on HAE attacks, the ePRO HAE diary will be used as a dosing 
diary to record each administration of IMP.

2.2.2 Sample Size Considerations
Power analysis and sample size estimation were conducted by assuming a Poisson 
distribution for angioedema attacks.  
 
For a treatment effect of 60% reduction in attacks as compared to placebo, 
 
 a sample size of 10 participants per group receiving 
active treatment and 10 participants receiving placebo would provide an approximate 85% 
power, at a significance level of 0.05 for two-sided test comparing each dose of PHA-022121 
to placebo, without multiplicity correction for the two comparisons. Assuming a larger 
treatment effect of 80% reduction in attack rate, the power would increase to approximately 
98%.

3 DEFINITIONS 
3.1 Investigational Medicinal Product (IMP) 
In Part 1, both double-blinded PHA-022121 and placebo are referred to as investigational 
medicinal product (IMP). 
The treatments to be administered in Part 1 consist of 10 mg PHA-022121 soft capsules 
(PHVS416) and matching placebo soft capsules for oral use.  
The IMP consists of 10 mg PHA-022121 soft capsules and matching placebo capsules for oral 
intake. The capsules are to be swallowed with at least 200 mL of water after breakfast and dinner, 
with 12 ± 2 hours between each dose.  
Participants are randomly assigned to one of three treatment groups and will receive blinded 
IMP as indicated: 
x PHA-022121 10 mg BID: 10 mg BID: one capsule of 10 mg PHA-022121 and one 
placebo capsule after breakfast and dinner  
x PHA-022121 20 mg BID: two capsules of 10 mg PHA-022121 after breakfast and 
dinner.  
x Placebo: two placebo capsules after breakfast and dinner. 
 
3.2 Dates, Periods, and Points of Reference 
Baseline 
 
Study baseline result refers to the results at randomization. Specifically, it is defined as the 
last assessment result prior to the first dose of IMP, i.e., the IMP administration in study Part 
1 to assess efficacy, safety and PK. For electrocardiogram (ECG), the baseline is defined as 
the mean of the last recorded triplicate before the first IMP administration. If no triplicate is 
available before the first dose of IMP, the last ECG value before the first IMP administration 
will be considered as baseline. For AE-QoL, the baseline may be measured within two days 
of the first dose of IMP. 
 
Study Day 
The first IMP administration date is defined as the first day on which the participant took 
the first dose of the IMP as recorded on the CRF. 
For assessments on or after the first IMP administration, study day is assessment date – the 
first IMP administration date + 1. As such, the first IMP administration date is study Day 1 
and it is used for all the outputs. Note that protocol refers to the first IMP administration date 
as Day 0. 
For assessments before the first IMP administration date, study day is calculated as 
assessment date – the first IMP administration, therefore, the day before the first IMP 
administration day is Day -1.

Analysis Periods 
 
The screening period is defined as the period from the date and time of informed consent to 
the time before randomization, i.e., Visit 1 Day 0. The duration of the screening period is 
calculated as: Date of Randomization (i.e., Visit 1 Day 0) – date of informed consent +1. 
 
The study period in Part 1 is defined as the period from randomization, (i.e., Visit 1, Day 0) 
to the date/time prior to the first IMP administration in Part 2 for participants who enrolled in 
Part 2, or to the end of study (EOS) date for participants who do not enroll in Part 2.
  
 
The treatment period in Part 1 is defined as the date and time of first IMP administration 
to the date and time of last IMP administration in Part 1. The duration of the treatment period 
is calculated as date of last IMP administration in Part 1 – date of first IMP administration of 
IMP +1.  
 
The treatment period without substantial IMP non-compliance in Part 1 is defined as 
the treatment period excluding all substantial IMP non-compliance periods in Part 1. A 
participant may have multiple IMP non-compliance periods. An instance of substantial IMP 
non-compliance occurs when 4 or more consecutive doses of IMP are missing. The duration 
of non-compliance period is calculated (in days) as the total number of missed doses during 
non-compliance periods divided by 2.  The substantial IMP non-compliance period is defined 
as the time period that starts from 12 hours after the most recent dose of IMP before the 
instance of substantial IMP-non-compliance and with duration defined as above.  
 
The treatment period censored at first substantial IMP non-compliance in Part 1 is 
defined as the period from the date and time of first IMP administration to the start date and 
time of first substantial IMP non-compliance period in Part 1.   
 
3.3 Analysis Sets 
Intent-to-treat (ITT) Analysis Set 
ITT analysis set includes all randomized participants who received at least one dose of IMP 
(PHA-022121 or placebo). In the analyses performed on the ITT Analysis Set, unless 
otherwise specified, participants will be analyzed according to their assigned treatment group 
per randomization regardless of actual treatment received. 
 
Safety Analysis Set 
Safety Analysis Set includes all randomized participants who received at least one dose of 
IMP (PHA-022121 or placebo), essentially the same set of participants included in the ITT

3.4.3  TSQM-II 
TSQM Version II has 11 items across four specific domains of Effectiveness, Side Effects, 
Convenience, and Global Satisfaction. Scores for each domain are computed by adding the 
TSQM items in each domain and then transforming the composite score into a value ranging 
from 0 to 100. Of note, a score can be computed for a domain only if no item is missing. If 
the participant answers ‘yes’ to item 3, he/she will be prompted to answer items 4-6. If the 
answer to question 3 is ‘no’, items 4-6 are skipped, and the participant moves right to

Data displays produced for this study will include summary tables, figures, and data listings

All assessments including scheduled, unscheduled, and early termination visits will be 
assigned to analysis visits using analysis visit windows based on the study day. If more than 
one assessment (scheduled, unscheduled, or early termination) occurs within a single visit 
window, then the analysis will be based on the assessment that is closest to the target day of 
the visit with the planned assessment. If there are multiple assessments that are equidistant 
from the target day, the visit with the later date and time will be used. 
The analysis visit windows will be applied to all efficacy and safety visits. The details can be 
found in Appendix A.

4.6 Efficacy Endpoints   
4.6.1 General Analysis Methods for Efficacy Endpoints 
Efficacy data will be summarized by randomized treatment based on the ITT Analysis Set.  
All statistical tests are carried out 2-sided (with 5% significance level). No multiplicity 
adjustments will be applied. The statistical test for the primary endpoint will be performed to 
compare each dose of PHA-022121 to placebo without multiplicity correction for the two 
comparisons. For the analyses of secondary efficacy endpoints, all statistical tests comparing 
treatments are descriptive in nature and will be made without adjustment for multiplicity. 
 
4.6.2 Primary Efficacy Endpoint  
The primary efficacy endpoint will be the number of Investigator-confirmed HAE attacks 
during the treatment period in Part 1 (Day 0 through Day 84) and will be expressed as the 
normalized number of attacks per month (4 weeks) of exposure. The monthly attack rate in 
Part 1 is calculated for each participant as the number of HAE attacks occurring during the 
treatment period in Part 1 divided by the number of days the participant contributed to the 
treatment period in Part 1 multiplied by 28 days.  
The analysis of the primary efficacy endpoint will be performed based on the intent-to-treat 
(ITT) Analysis Set and will compare each PHA-022121 dose group with the placebo group 
using a generalized linear model (GLM) assuming a Poisson distribution with a log link 
function and Pearson chi-square scaling of standard errors to account for potential dispersion. 
The model will include fixed effects for treatment group (categorical), the normalized 
baseline attack rate (continuous), the randomized stratification factor of baseline attack rate 
(categorical), and an offset term of the logarithm of participant’s follow-up time (in months) 
during the efficacy evaluation period. The stratification factor of baseline attack rate will be 
PAGE 24
PHA-022121  Pharvaris Netherlands B.V. 
Statistical Analysis Plan for PHA022121-C301   Version 1.0, October 27, 2023 
Confidential  Page 23 of 35 
combined into two groups (1 to < 2 attacks vs. >= 2 attacks) due to small sample size for 
certain category. The estimated least squares (LS) mean rate for each PHA-022121 dose 
group and the mean rate ratios relative to the placebo group will be provided with 95% 
confidence intervals. These estimates will be reported as monthly attack rates (mean numbers 
of events per 4 weeks) by transforming the estimates using the exponential function and 
scaling by the unit of time. The treatment effect in the primary efficacy endpoint will be 
tested using a Wald-based chi-square test. In addition, Wilcoxon two-sample test and the 
Hodges-Lehman estimate will be conducted comparing each PHA-022121 dose group with 
the placebo group. In the event that Poisson regression has model fitting issues due to limited 
sample size and excessive zero counts, the Wilcoxon two-sample test will be used as the 
primary analysis method to test the treatment effect.  
The SAS code for Poisson Model, Wilcoxon rank sum test and Hodges-Lehman estimator are 
presented in Appendix B.

If there are differences between the actual baseline attack rate and the stratification factor

baseline attack rate category in the model.
