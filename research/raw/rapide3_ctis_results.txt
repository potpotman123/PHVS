B. SUBJECT DISPOSITION 
 
1. RECRUITMENT 
 
Recruitment details: This Phase 3, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, 2-period, 2-treatment cross-over 
study evaluated the efficacy and safety of orally administered deucrictibant immediate-release (IR) soft capsules compared with 
placebo for the on-demand treatment of attacks in participants with HAE. 
 
Pre-Assignment Period: A total of 134 participants were enrolled in the study. 
 
Eligibility 
 
Key Inclusion Criteria: 
 
The following inclusion criteria had to be met for a subject to be eligible for inclusion in the study: 
1. Provision of written informed consent. If the participant is a minor (i.e., <18 years of age or as determined by local law), 
consent will be obtained from the participant’s parent/legally designated representative/guardian and written assent will 
be obtained from the participant, per country regulations. 
2. Male or female, aged ≥12 to ≤75 years at the time of providing written informed consent/assent. 
3. Diagnosis of HAE - Type1/2 (HAE-C1INH), and Type 3(HAE-nC1INH). 
4. History of at least 2 HAE attacks in the last 3 months before screening. 
5. Experience with using standard-of-care treatment to effectively manage on-demand treatment for HAE attacks. 
6. For adolescent participants aged ≥12 to <18 years: body weight ≥40 kg. 
7. Either was on a stable dose and regimen of long-term prophylactic therapy indicated for HAE for at least 6 months before 
the screening visit and intend to remain on the same dose for the duration of the study or was receiving only on-demand 
treatment for HAE, then had followed the protocol specified washout period before the screening visit.

Key Exclusion Criteria: 
 
1. Any female who is pregnant, plans to become pregnant, or is breastfeeding. 
2. Any diagnosis of angioedema other than HAE. 
3. Any clinically significant comorbidity or systemic dysfunction that would interfere with the participant's safety or 
ability to participate in the study. 
4. Use of attenuated androgens for short-term prophylaxis within 2 weeks before screening. 
5.  Abnormal hepatic function (aspartate aminotransferase [AST] >2×upper limit of normal [ULN], alanine 
aminotransferase [ALT] >2×ULN, or total bilirubin >1.5×ULN, or Child-Pugh class B or C). 
6. Abnormal renal function (eGFR <60 ml/min/1.73 m^2). 
7. Has received prior on-demand HAE treatment with deucrictibant.

1. Primary Endpoint: 
 
End point Title Time to onset of symptom relief assessed by Patient Global Impression of Change 
(PGI-C) assessment 
End point Description PGI-C is a 7-point response scale designed to evaluate the change in the attack 
symptoms from pre-treatment (the last symptom assessment before study drug 
administration) over time with a 7-point response scale. Participants were asked to 
choose the response that best describes currently at the time of assessment the 
overall change in their HAE attack symptoms since they took the study drug: Much 
better, better, a little better, same, a little worse, worse and much worse. Time to 
onset of symptom relief is calculated as the time from study drug administration for 
treating an attack (the time of the first dose if a second dose is administered) to the 
earliest time when PGI-C rating achieves “a little better” or higher response 
(“better”, or “much better”) and is sustained for 2 consecutive timepoints. The 
difference in time to the event of interest between deucrictibant and placebo was 
tested using Gehan’s generalized Wilcoxon test. The Kaplan-Meier estimated 
median and percentiles of time to onset of symptom relief by PGI-C assessment is 
presented. The intercurrent event (ICE) was the intake of rescue medication or 
prohibited medication after the attack was treated with study drug. The occurrence 
of ICE was regarded as a treatment failure.

8. Secondary endpoint 
 
Endpoint Title Time to substantial symptom relief by Angioedema Symptom Rating Scale (AMRA) assessment 
Endpoint Description The AMRA-3 is a composite score (average) of the 3 major HAE symptoms: skin swelling, skin 
pain, and abdominal pain. The AMRA-3 scale allowed scoring on a scale between 0 (No 
swelling/No pain) and 100 (Worst possible swelling/Worst possible pain). The AMRA-5 is a 
composite score of the 5 major HAE symptoms: skin swelling, skin pain, abdominal pain, voice 
change and difficulty swallowing. For non-severe laryngeal attacks, the AMRA-5 scale allowed 
scoring on a scale between 0 (No skin swelling, skin pain, abdominal pain, voice change, or 
difficulty swallowing) and 100 (Worst skin swelling, skin pain, abdominal pain, voice change, or 
difficulty swallowing). Time to substantial symptom relief is calculated as the time from study drug 
administration for treating an attack (the time of the first dose if a second dose is administered) to 
the earliest time when a ≥50% reduction from pre-treatment in AMRA is achieved and a composite 
score from pre-treatment for 2 consecutive timepoints within 12 hours post-treatment. If the event 
of interest has not been achieved based on data entered within the timeframe, an attack will be 
treated as right-censored at the last observation before 13h, which is the upper end of the ePRO data 
entry window for 12h timepoint. The Kaplan-Meier estimated median and percentiles of time to 
substantial symptom relief by Angioedema Symptom Rating Scale assessment has been presented.

and abdominal pain. The AMRA-3 scale allowed scoring on a scale between 0 (No swelling/No 
pain) and 100 (Worst possible swelling/Worst possible pain). The AMRA-5 is a composite score of 
the 5 major HAE symptoms: skin swelling, skin pain, abdominal pain, voice change and difficulty 
swallowing. For non-severe laryngeal attacks, the AMRA-5 scale allowed scoring on a scale 
between 0 (No skin swelling, skin pain, abdominal pain, voice change, or difficulty swallowing) 
and 100 (Worst skin swelling, skin pain, abdominal pain, voice change, or difficulty swallowing). 
Time to EoP in attack symptoms is calculated as the time from study drug administration for 
treating an attack (the time of the first dose if a second dose is administered) to the earliest post-
treatment timepoint after which all subsequent AMRA results are stable or improved, namely, at all 
subsequent timepoints, for the AMRA assessment, each individual item score value is equal to or 
lower (improved) than the corresponding item score value at the current time (the identified time of 
EoP). The ICE was the intake of rescue medication or prohibited medication after the attack was 
treated with study drug. The occurrence of ICE was regarded as a treatment failure. 
Time Frame Up to 12 hours
