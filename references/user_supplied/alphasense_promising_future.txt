Summary
The interview covered the expert's approach to diagnosing and managing HAE, current treatment preferences, and detailed impressions of emerging oral therapies, particularly deucrictibant, for both acute and prophylactic use.
EXPERT BACKGROUND
Allergist immunologist with experience managing both adult and pediatric patients in an outpatient multi-specialty group, including direct management of approximately 12 hereditary angioedema (HAE) patients.
EXPERT SENTIMENT
Strongly optimistic about the potential of new oral therapies, particularly deucrictibant, for both on-demand and prophylactic HAE treatment.
Believes that oral options will improve patient outcomes by enabling earlier and more frequent treatment of attacks, and is highly enthusiastic about the future of HAE management with these agents.
Expresses some concern about mechanism of action redundancy when the same drug class is used for both prophylaxis and acute treatment, but overall sees significant value in new oral agents.
TAKEAWAYS
Most HAE patients in the expert's practice are well-controlled on current prophylactic drugs, with an average of two to three attacks per year.
Diagnosis of HAE type 1 and 2 is straightforward, but there are still significant diagnostic delays for many patients.
Current standard of care involves both long-term prophylaxis and on-demand therapy, with a preference for oral on-demand agents due to ease of use and improved patient adherence.
Ekterly (oral on-demand) and icatibant (sub-Q) are the most commonly used acute treatments, with patients preferring oral options for convenience and earlier intervention.
Deucrictibant is viewed as a highly promising oral on-demand and prophylactic agent, with favorable efficacy and safety data, and is expected to be competitive with or superior to current oral options like Orladeyo, though barriers such as being second to market and payer access remain.
