SMART SUMMARY
Summary
The interview covered the expert's approach to managing hereditary angioedema, current prophylactic treatment options, unmet needs in therapy, and reactions to new clinical trial data for an oral extended-release prophylactic option. Discussion also included practical challenges in prescribing and the future outlook for HAE prophylactic therapies.
EXPERT BACKGROUND
Practicing allergist immunologist in a suburban metropolitan area, part of a single-specialty group focused on specialty care, with experience managing hereditary angioedema (HAE) patients.
EXPERT SENTIMENT
Generally positive about new prophylactic options for HAE, especially oral therapies, but notes they are not a complete solution.
Believes new therapies will have some impact, particularly for patients not well controlled on current options, but does not expect them to dramatically change the landscape for well-controlled patients.
Expresses frustration with the administrative burden and lack of compensation for obtaining insurance approval for specialty medications.
TAKEAWAYS
The expert manages a small number of HAE patients, most of whom are well controlled on existing therapies.
Current prophylactic options like TAKHZYRO, ORLADEYO, and danazol are effective for most, but breakthrough attacks still occur in 15-20% of patients.
There is an unmet need for longer-acting therapies (e.g., injections every 3-6 months) and for options that can eliminate breakthrough attacks.
The expert views the Pharvaris deucrictibant extended release data positively, especially for its oral administration and tolerability, but notes efficacy is similar to existing options.
Administrative burden and lack of financial incentive make switching stable patients to new therapies unlikely; new options will likely be used for newly diagnosed or uncontrolled patients.
