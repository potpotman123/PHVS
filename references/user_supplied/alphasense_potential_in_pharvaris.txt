SMART SUMMARY
Summary
The interview covered the current treatment landscape for hereditary angioedema, focusing on the use of prophylactic therapies and the potential impact of Pharvaris's deucrictibant XR based on recent phase III results. Discussion included patient preferences, efficacy comparisons, barriers to adoption, and the evolving needs in HAE management.
EXPERT BACKGROUND
Allergist and immunologist in private practice treating both adults and pediatric patients with hereditary angioedema (HAE), currently managing 12 angioedema patients, with experience in both acute and prophylactic HAE therapies.
EXPERT SENTIMENT
The expert is optimistic about the efficacy and tolerability of deucrictibant XR, especially compared to current oral options.
They believe deucrictibant XR could be a compelling option for needle-phobic, younger, or newly diagnosed patients, and anticipate significant patient interest in switching from existing therapies if payer barriers are addressed.
TAKEAWAYS
About 50% of the expert's HAE patients are on long-term prophylaxis, with the rest managing with acute therapies due to infrequent attacks.
Current injectable prophylactic therapies are highly effective, but oral options lag in efficacy and tolerability; deucrictibant XR's 87% efficacy is seen as a significant improvement for oral therapy.
Rapid onset of action (within one week) and a favorable side effect profile are important factors for patient adherence and are seen as strengths of deucrictibant XR.
The expert estimates that up to 50% of patients already on prophylaxis might be willing to switch to deucrictibant XR, primarily due to its oral administration and comparable efficacy to injectables.
The biggest barrier to adoption is payer/insurance approval, given the high cost and the need for justification to switch therapies.
